01

A system, not a style guide

Sunlogic installs solar and does the paperwork. The identity has one job: look like a contractor who knows the trade and will tell you the truth about your roof. Warm, plain, unhurried. Nothing here is decorative for its own sake.

Warm, not clinical

Surfaces are beige, not white and not grey. The whole palette sits on the warm side, including the greys.

Flat, not layered

Depth comes from a tonal step and a hairline, never a shadow. The page reads as printed matter, not as floating cards.

One accent, rationed

A single orange. Used once or twice per screen, it means something. Used everywhere, it means nothing.

Honest about gaps

Unknown facts show as visible bracketed placeholders. A missing photograph is a labelled empty state, never stock.

Gradients

Do

Use orange for the one thing you most want clicked, and for the last phrase of a headline — #F66F00 on a dark photo, #B85300 anywhere the orange itself is the text on beige or white.

Don't

Set #F66F00 as text on beige or white. It's a fill/icon/border colour there — 2.7:1, fails AA. Use #B85300 for that case instead.

Don't

Use orange as an error or warning colour. Errors are #BA1A1A.

Do

Use #5A544B for secondary text on warm surfaces. It passes AA at 7.0:1.

Don't

Use #A09B93 for any text. At 2.4:1 on beige it fails. It is for rules and disabled states.

Case

Headings are Title Case; everything inside prose is sentence case. The split follows the copy document, which is the authority on wording.

Do — headings

Title Case for hero and section headings, card titles, step titles and CTA headings. "Solar That Pays For Itself." Minor words stay lower case — a, and, at, for, in, of, on, or, the, to — unless they open the heading.

Do — prose

Sentence case for sub-heads inside body copy, eyebrows, field labels and captions. "What will it actually save me?"

Do

Set the final phrase of a headline in orange — once per heading.

Don't

Colour a phrase mid-sentence, or use the device twice in one heading. And never ALL CAPS a heading — capitals belong to the mono face at label size.

Section rhythm is automatic

Every top-level block inside a section sits 40px from the next — heading stack, grid, callout, form. It is a property of the section, not something anyone applies. A heading can never butt up against the grid below it, and nobody has to remember a margin.

Cards in a row are always level

Every card in a grid stretches to the height of its tallest sibling, whatever the copy length. Ragged card bottoms are the most common way a grid stops looking designed, so it is enforced by the card rather than left to the page.

Matching card heights isn't enough on its own — a card's dl-actions row sits flush with the bottom edge too (.sl-card > dl-actions { margin-top: auto }), so buttons land level across the row instead of trailing wherever the copy above them happens to end. Don't add a fixed height, a spacer, or per-card margin to force this by hand; the rule already does it for any card with a button row.

05

Four behaviours, nothing else

Gradient drift, light sweep, fade-up, pulse. Motion in this system is slow and atmospheric, never reactive. Nothing bounces, nothing springs, nothing scales on hover.

BehaviourTimingWhere
Gradient drift14s linear infiniteHero layer 2, closing CTA
Light sweep9s linear infiniteHero layer 3
Fade-up700ms expo-outScroll reveals, feed rows
Pulse1.6s infiniteLive indicator only
Button wipe240ms expo-outEvery button, bottom to top
Link hover100ms ease-outOpacity 0.7. Never a scale.
PresstranslateY(1px)All buttons. Never a shrink.

The hero needs all five layers

Photograph, 14s gradient drift, 9s light sweep, side scrim, bottom scrim. The drift alone reads static in a single glance. The sweep is what makes motion legible over a photograph. The side scrim is what holds text contrast against a bright image nobody has seen yet.

Live, over an empty slot

Do

Honour prefers-reduced-motion — animations resolve to their end state rather than disappearing.

Don't

Add particles, parallax, spring physics or hover scaling. None are in the vocabulary.

Paired buttons can fill different colours

Where two buttons sit side by side and each should read as its own thing, wipe sets which colour fills. On the home page Solar fills orange and Electrical fills navy, so the pair is distinguishable before you read the labels. Only these two values exist — it is not a licence to introduce a colour.

Solar Electrical

50px tall, 38px small. 4px radius. Press is a 1px downward translate. Every button wipes on hover — a fill rising bottom-to-top over 240ms. It sits on the base button rather than a variant, so it is automatic wherever a button appears. Navy, beige and outline wipe to orange; the orange emphasis button wipes to navy; ghost-on-dark wipes to a restrained white. One emphasis button in view — the closing CTA is the allowed second.

Cards, steps and stats

When a card goes navy. Exactly one place: the final step of a numbered sequence, so the process closes on a full stop rather than trailing off. Never an interior step, never two in one sequence, and never as decoration on an ordinary card — it is navy #0D2028, not a second accent, and it carries meaning only because it is rationed. Elsewhere a dark card belongs to a dl-statement band or the closing CTA.

The stat value slot is 40px at weight 900 — sized for numerals and short values. For long word values at phone width, the same facts restate as a label/value row instead.

Cards hover

The hairline alone. The border warms over 100ms and nothing else moves — no fill change, no lift, no shadow, no scale. A card that is itself a link takes the orange edge instead. It is on the card, so it applies everywhere.

Statement band

One large line with the dawn gradient drifting behind it, full bleed. This is what breaks up a long service page — a beat between two runs of prose. One per two or three sections; more and it stops being a break.

Question sets

Three or fewer questions can be cards. More than three becomes an accordion — a grid of six cards is a wall, and nobody reads a wall.

Figures

Navigation, and the dock

Two layers of navigation, and one action. The header is sticky, 72px, four links plus the quote button. The section nav appears only on pages that serve several audiences in one scroll, sits under the header at 72px, and marks the section you are in.

Nav links take the accent on hover and when current — orange, except Electrical, which takes navy. That is the same pairing as the Solar/Electrical buttons, so the two trades read consistently wherever they appear together. When one link is current the rest drop to 55% opacity and come back to full on hover.

The dock is not a second nav. Repeating the header at the bottom of the page earns nothing. It carries the things someone actually wants within reach while reading a long page — a way to reach us and the quote — at 4px corners and with the same hover wipe as every other button. The contact slot rolls between both phone numbers and the email address, one visible at a time, sliding up every 3 seconds (<dl-roll> — the same component handles every rolling phone number on the site, not just the dock's).

Plugins

The savings calculator, the review carousel and the day feed are third-party web components with their own markup. They are themed, never forked. shared/plugins.css maps their CSS variables and class names onto the system tokens, so a change to the palette or the type scale reaches them too.

Do

Theme a plugin in shared/plugins.css, at :root specificity so it beats the style block the plugin injects at runtime.

Don't

Edit the plugin's JavaScript to restyle it. The next version overwrites you, and the plugin stops tracking the system.

Do

Bump the ?v= on every tag that loads a file you have just changed — the plugins and everything in shared/. There is no build step, so a browser will serve a cached copy of the old file and the page runs stale code without erroring.

Don't

Theme a plugin element that is an <a> tag without opting it out of the system's own link-dim rule. Plain text links dim to 70% opacity on hover (a:hover); every button and card is explicitly excepted (a.sl-btn:hover, a.sl-card:hover, …). A plugin card left off that list silently washes out on hover along with whatever hover state you did theme — a review card once dimmed exactly this way. Add opacity: 1 to the plugin's own hover rule in plugins.css, not to the central exception list.

07

Someone who knows the trade

Knowledgeable rather than technical. Not a friendly stranger, not a brochure. A contractor explaining what they found on your roof.

Do

"If solar is not worth it for your property yet, we will say so."

Don't

"The Western Cape's leading solar solutions provider."

Do

Explain a Certificate of Compliance in one clause, the first time it appears on a page.

Don't

Quote a payback period. It depends on tariff assumptions nobody can promise.

Rules

EmojiNone. Not in captions, not in bios, not as bullets.
HashtagsThree at most, at the end, lowercase, genuinely descriptive. No stacked blocks.
ClaimsNo savings percentages, no payback periods, no "leading" or "best". Specific facts only.
PricesNever in a post. The calculator on the site is the only place pricing appears.
PhotographyReal jobs only, with the client's permission. Never stock, never a render.
FacesSigned release required — see the video and photography release on the legal page.
AccentOne orange phrase per graphic. Never a full orange background.
TypeHanken Grotesk and JetBrains Mono. Never a platform default, never a script face.
GradientsBehind imagery only, never behind body text.
RepostsCredit the source. Do not silently re-caption someone else's photograph.

Open — flag, never guess

Missing

The logo. No mark exists. The wordmark sets the name in display type. Do not draw, trace, generate or approximate one.

Missing

All photography. Every slot is a labelled empty state awaiting real installation and team images.

Missing

Nine facts — phone, email, address, hours, registration number, response time, install cost.

Unresolved

Accessibility. No focus trap in the drawer, no skip link, no arrow-key navigation in tabs or menus. Contrast and reduced motion are fixed.

Unresolved

Landscape phone behaviour, and whether the sticky header should hide on scroll-down.

Inferred

Button typography (Hanken Grotesk 700 / 14px) was not sourced from an existing Sunlogic component. Flagged for design review.
