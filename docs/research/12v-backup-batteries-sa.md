# 12V Backup Batteries — South African Security & Access Equipment Market

**Research date:** 3 September 2026. All ZAR prices captured on this date unless noted.
**Purpose:** Raw material for the Solar site's "Backup" section and online store catalogue.
**Scope:** Standby batteries for gate motors, garage doors, alarm panels, electric fence energisers, CCTV, intercoms, small UPS. **Not** home solar storage.

> Everything in Part A is source-backed. Everything in Part B is **not verified** and must not be published as fact without checking.

## PART A — VERIFIED WITH SOURCES

### A1. Chemistries sold in SA for this application

| Chemistry | SA market position | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sealed lead acid (SLA / VRLA / AGM)** | Default, highest volume, cheapest | Forbatt, Centurion, Ritar, Stafix across SecurityKing, Stafix, Brite Lighting |
| **Gel** | Modest premium over SLA, same form factor | Stafix 7Ah Gel R337.25 vs 7Ah SLA R305.06 |
| **LiFePO4** | Growing fast, sold as "drop-in 7Ah replacement" | NICE, Vestwoods, UltraTech, Red Pole Energy, Lalela, GeeWiz, Extreme Lights, Nemtek |
| Li-ion (non-LFP) | Essentially absent in this niche | — |

In SA retail, "AGM" and "SLA" are used interchangeably for the same 12V 7Ah security battery. Gel is normally called out separately.

**Verified trade-offs:**

- **Cycle life:** SLA **200–400 cycles**; LiFePO4 **~2000 cycles**. Red Pole Energy specifies 2,000 cycles at 0.2C on its 12Ah unit.
- **Depth of discharge:** Stafix's own product page states its SLA is **"not suitable for deep-cycle applications"** and **"should not be discharged below 50%"**.
- **Temperature:** For lead acid, **25°C is optimum, and every 8°C rise halves service life**. A VRLA rated 10 years at 25°C lasts ~5 years at 33°C, ~30 months at 41°C. Heat damage is **not recoverable**.
- **Self-discharge:** Red Pole Energy LiFePO4 <3.5%/month. No SA-sourced lead acid figure verified.
- **Size/weight:** LiFePO4 12Ah = 1.35 kg, 152 × 65 × 97 mm — same footprint as a 7Ah SLA at double the capacity.

### A2. Capacities and form factors

**Dominant form factor: the 12V 7Ah "security brick", 151 × 65 × 94 mm (100 mm over terminals)**, with **F1 (4.75 mm) or F2 (6.35 mm)** spade terminals. F1 is more common on alarm panels and emergency lighting; F2 on UPS.

**Verified as stocked in SA:**
- **SecurityKing:** 12V in 1.4, 3.2, 7.2, 8, 9, 12, 18, 20, 100 Ah; 24V in 2.4 and 3.5 Ah
- **Stafix:** 12V in 1.3, 2.3, 4.5, 7, 17, 50, 105 Ah, plus 100Ah gel
- **Nemtek:** lead acid 7, 12, 28, 50, 100 Ah; LiFePO4 7 and 20 Ah *(from search summary, category page did not render specs)*

**7Ah / 7.2Ah / 7.5Ah / 8Ah are commercially interchangeable** in this form factor — vendors label the same brick differently.

**Equipment pairing:**
- **24V is a real and separate line** for certain garage/gate motors (DigiDoor 24V 2.4Ah, ET Nice 24V 3.5Ah, Forbatt 24V 3.5Ah). Getting voltage wrong is a live risk.
- Stafix markets one 12V 7Ah SKU for **gate motors, backup supplies, alarm panels, electric fence energisers and UPS** — five applications, one part.
- Red Pole Energy's 12Ah LiFePO4 is warranted for gate motors, alarms, access control, electric fences, CCTV.
- **Electric fence energisers scale much higher** — 28/50/100/105 Ah for larger perimeters.

### A5. Lifespan and failure causes

**Consensus: 1–3 years, with ~2 years the realistic residential replacement cycle.** Under frequent load shedding this drops to **18–24 months**; under heavy daily cycling, **under 18 months**.

**Verified failure mechanisms:**
1. **Deep discharge / sulfation.** Below ~50% SoC compromises lifespan; a fully flat lead acid battery is permanently degraded even after recharge. (Lance Dickerson, MD of REVOV, quoted in EngineerIT.)
2. **Discharging below 10.5V severely damages the battery.**
3. **Heat.** Every 8°C above 25°C halves life; damage is irreversible. Manufacturers advise reducing float voltage above 29°C ambient. **Directly relevant to SA** — gate motor and energiser enclosures sit in full sun.
4. **Positive grid corrosion** — the life-limiting mechanism in VRLA generally.
5. **Poor/undersized charging circuits** — gate motor chargers are designed for standby float duty, not cycling.
6. **Undersizing** — fitting 7Ah where duty demands 12Ah+.

**Early-warning signs (useful for copy):** slow gate movement during an outage; gate works on mains but fails on backup; motor completes only a few cycles then stops; **warm or swollen casing**.

### A6. Load shedding effect

The strongest, best-supported theme in the research.

- The mechanism is a **duty-cycle mismatch**: these are **standby/float** batteries used as **cycling** batteries. SLA gets 200–400 cycles; two outages a day burns that budget in well under two years.
- **Estates and complexes are worst-affected** — gates cycle constantly on battery, draining fully within one outage window.
- Batteries are frequently **not fully recharged between outages**, starting each cut at partial charge and being driven deeper.
- Reports range from the common 2–3 years down to replacement every few months under heavy load shedding.
- **Batteries fail far more often than the gate motors themselves** — useful framing for service copy.

### A7. Safety, disposal, regulatory

**Lead acid disposal:**
- **It is illegal to landfill batteries in South Africa** — NEM:WA prohibits landfills accepting hazardous waste.
- Lead acid batteries are a **Schedule 3 hazardous substance**; a cracked casing leaches sulfuric acid and soluble lead into soil within days.
- **Up to 90% of SA lead acid batteries are recycled.** Take-back points include Battery Centre and First National Battery.

**Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) — directly relevant to running an online battery store:**
- **Government Notice 3179, effective 23 March 2023**, imposes EPR for portable batteries.
- Obligated parties include **manufacturers, brand owners, retailers selling imported batteries, and any commercial battery seller**. A retailer importing stock can fall in scope.
- Producers must register with a Producer Responsibility Organisation (eWASA is the registered PRO) and implement collection, recycling, transport and reporting schemes.
- **Scope caveat:** the portable-battery definition covers sealed, hand-carryable batteries that are "neither automotive nor industrial", and **explicitly excludes UPS systems and power banks**. Whether a 12V 7Ah security SLA is portable, industrial, or out of scope is **unresolved** — see Part B.

**Lithium transport:**
- Standalone lithium-ion ships under **UN3480**; packed with/in equipment under **UN3481**. Air transport falls under IATA DGR.
- **FedEx no longer accepts UN3090/UN3480 as IATA Section II** — must be tendered as fully regulated Section I.
- Some UN3480 shipments are **Cargo Aircraft Only**.
- **Practical implication:** national courier of lithium stock is materially more constrained and costly than lead acid. Road is less restrictive than air, but confirm with the specific courier before promising nationwide next-day delivery on lithium SKUs.

**Product safety (Stafix):** do not short-circuit, observe polarity, charge regularly, do not discharge below 50%.

## Notes for copy and catalogue planning

- **The most defensible commercial story is the duty-cycle mismatch:** SLA is a standby battery being used as a cycling battery. Verified from multiple angles (Stafix's own "not suitable for deep-cycle" warning, the 200–400 vs 2000 cycle gap, the 50% DoD threshold). It justifies the lithium upsell honestly without overclaiming.
- **The lithium value case is arithmetic, not hype.** ~R800 LiFePO4 at 2000 cycles vs ~R280 SLA at 200–400 cycles replaced every 18–24 months. But real-world life depends on the charger — several SA vendors claim drop-in compatibility with existing gate-motor chargers. **Verify against the specific motors Sunlogic installs before putting it in writing**, since SLA float voltages and LiFePO4 charge profiles differ (LiFePO4 bulk 14.4V / float 13.8V per Red Pole Energy).
- **Heat is an underused angle for Cape Town.** The 8°C-halves-life rule is hard, citable engineering, and enclosures in summer sun are a genuine local failure driver — distinct from the load-shedding story everyone else tells.
- **Voltage confusion is a real support cost.** 24V garage/gate SKUs sit alongside 12V at similar prices. Filter the catalogue by voltage first, then capacity, then chemistry, to prevent wrong-part returns.
- **Capacity labelling should be tolerant.** List 7 / 7.2 / 7.5 / 8Ah as one interchangeable family with a note, not as separate SKUs the customer must choose between.
- **Two compliance items to resolve before launch:** (a) eWASA EPR scope for security SLAs, and (b) courier terms for lithium SKUs. Both are Part B items.
