# Competitive Landscape: Gas Installation & Servicing, Cape Town / Western Cape (as at October 2026)

> **Verification key used throughout**
> **[SELF]** = the company's own marketing claim, read directly off their site. Not independently verified.
> **[3P-AGG]** = third-party aggregator or directory (Snupit, Yellosa, iVote, Bizcommunity, Facebook, podcast profile). Ratings and review counts on these pages are *as displayed by the aggregator*, not read off Google/Hellopeter itself.
> **[CONFIRMED]** = I fetched the primary source page myself and can confirm the page says this (confirms the *claim exists*, not that the claim is true).
> **[GAP]** = could not verify.
>
> **Scope note on method:** all competitor pages below were fetched directly in October 2026. The available web-search tool is US-weighted and returned thin results for South African local businesses; Google Maps / Google Business Profile listings, Hellopeter business pages, and the SAQCC Gas and LPGas SA registers could not be read programmatically. Where this matters I have flagged it as a gap rather than inferring.

## Q1. Who are the main players? Categories and named companies

### Takeaway
Western Cape gas work is dominated by small owner-operator installers (one to three vans) competing on a near-identical website template: "registered gas installer, domestic and commercial, CoC provided, get a quote." National LPG suppliers (Afrox, Easigas) do **not** market installation to end consumers at all — they refer out to "approved installers" — which leaves the entire lead-capture layer to small independents, plumbing firms, and fireplace retailers. I found **no** Western Cape player that is both large and credibly multi-trade in gas.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The national LPG suppliers' refusal to own the installation relationship ("phone our Customer Service Centre") is the single biggest structural gift to a local contractor: there is no national brand soaking up household gas-install search demand in the Western Cape.
- The competitive set is almost entirely sub-scale. Several sites carry 0–11 reviews, placeholder phone numbers, spam injection, or 2019-vintage testimonials. A contractor with a maintained, current, properly credentialled site would outrank most of this field on trust signals alone.
- Fireplace showrooms are a channel, not a competitor: Italfire openly says a certified gas installer must do the gas line, and Progress/Fire Place Studio keep installation in-house only for the fireplace itself.

### Gaps
- No readable Oryx / TotalEnergies / KayaGas / Avedia Western Cape installer pages — cannot confirm whether they operate approved-installer networks in the province.
- Elite Gas Services' site blocked automated fetch (403), so its claims could not be read first-hand.
- Company sizes, headcounts, turnover and install volumes are unpublished across the board.

## Q3. Pricing and quoting practice

### Takeaway
Pricing is overwhelmingly hidden: six of seven sites read publish **no** numbers and route everything to "get a free quote." The two sources of public price data in this market are **The LpGas Man's** own rate card and **Snupit's** aggregated job averages — and they broadly agree, which makes the number range credible. Nobody publishes a CoC price, a callout fee, or a servicing price.

### Cited Findings

**The LpGas Man — the only published competitor rate card** — [pricing guide](https://thegasman.co.za/pages/gas-installation-pricing-guide) **[CONFIRMED]**

| Job | "From" price | Range given elsewhere on the same page |
|---|---|---|
| Gas stove / hob | from R2,500 | R2,000 – R7,500+ |
| Gas geyser | from R3,500 (R3,450 elsewhere) | R3,450 – R9,500+ |
| Gas fireplace | from R3,500 (R3,450 elsewhere) | R3,450 – R7,500+ |
| Gas braai / grill | from R3,000 | R3,000 – R7,500+ |
| Gas space heater | from R5,000 | R4,500 – R7,500+ |
| Gas generator | from R5,500 | R4,500 – R11,500+ |
| Cylinder cage | — | R1,450 – R3,750+ |

- **Excluded from every price:** "Appliance + Cylinders + Cage if required." **[CONFIRMED]**
- **CoC price: not published** — listed as a guide item but with no figure. Repairs/servicing: no price. Callout fee: none published. Fire-department approval (>100 kg): no price. **[CONFIRMED absence]**
- Cylinder sizing published as part of the price logic: geysers/generators/heaters need min 19–48 kg; fireplaces min 48 kg; two cylinders recommended for backup. **[SELF]**
- Quoting process described: free quote by phone (079-586-9947) or an **online estimate calculator**, plus "personalized consultations" to determine line layout and cylinder placement. **The page does not describe a site-visit process and does not say whether an inspection fee applies.** **[CONFIRMED]**
- Warranty: **two-year workmanship warranty** included with installations. **[SELF]**
- **Internal inconsistency flagged:** the same page gives two different prices for the same geyser and fireplace jobs. Confirm before citing a single figure.

**Snupit's aggregated averages (third-party, not an installer's own price)** — [Snupit Cape Town gas installers](https://www.snupit.co.za/cape-town/gas-installers) **[3P-AGG, CONFIRMED page content]**
- Inside / cupboard installation with 9 kg cylinder: **~R1,950**
- Outside installation, piping up to 5 m: **~R2,950**
- Outside installation with two or more cylinders: **~R3,900**
- The Goodwood page repeats "about R1,950 for an inside installation with a 9kg cylinder." **[3P-AGG]**

**Everyone else: quote-gated**
- Gas Viper, Cape Gas Installations, Core Gas, Kemp Jonker, Gas Line Solutions, Top Gas and Plumbing — **no prices at all**; CTA is a quote request or callout request. **[CONFIRMED on each site]**
- **Free quote** is offered explicitly by Kemp Jonker ("GET YOUR FREE QUOTE TODAY") and The LpGas Man. **[SELF]**
- **Free site visit vs paid inspection: no competitor states a policy either way.** Core Gas comes closest by saying geyser sizing is based on "an initial site inspection," without saying who pays. **[CONFIRMED]**
- **No CoC price is published by any competitor read.** A search specifically for Cape Town gas CoC pricing returned no rand figure from any provider. **[CONFIRMED absence]**

### Inferences
- The LpGas Man's "from" prices and Snupit's averages bracket the household market at roughly **R2,000–R4,000 for a straightforward single-appliance install**, rising to R7,500–R11,500 for generators and multi-appliance work, appliance excluded. That is a usable public benchmark.
- Publishing a clean, internally consistent rate card — including a **CoC-only price** and a **servicing price**, which nobody publishes — would be a genuine first in this market, and The LpGas Man's self-contradicting numbers show how low the bar currently is.
- "Free quote" is table stakes, not a differentiator. The unclaimed ground is stating up front whether a site visit is free, how fast it happens, and what the quote will and will not include.

### Gaps
- No published commercial or restaurant-kitchen pricing from anyone.
- No published CoC-only fee, callout fee, after-hours surcharge, or annual-service fee from any Western Cape competitor.
- Could not verify whether The LpGas Man's prices are honoured in practice (no independent quote data).

SAQCC registration is claimed almost universally and **numbered almost never**: of the companies whose sites I read, only Gas Viper (SAQCC ID 5389) and Cape Gas Installations (SAQCC 0366) publish a number. - **Appliance-warranty handling: no competitor read addresses it at all.** Nobody explains what happens to the appliance manufacturer's warranty, who registers it, or how a warranty claim is handled after their install. **[CONFIRMED absence across all seven]**

- The legal frame competitors are selling against: under the Pressure Equipment Regulations (OHS Act No. 85 of 1993) all gas installations must have a CoC confirming the installation has been scrutinised and is safe and leak-free, and for LPG it must be issued by a person registered with LPGAS — [The Citizen homeowner guide](https://www.citizen.co.za/alberton-record/uncategorized/2021/08/21/gas-installations-what-homeowners-need-to-know/) **[3P media]**
- The same guide reports SAQCC Gas's position that **the homeowner, not the installer, is responsible for holding a valid gas CoC**, and that an insurer may refuse a claim where a defective gas appliance caused damage and no valid certificate exists. **[3P media, attributed]**
- **Conflicting information on CoC validity — flagged, not resolved.** One source says gas CoCs are valid **five years** — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/alberton-record/uncategorized/2021/08/21/gas-installations-what-homeowners-need-to-know/); another says **two years**, required for any new installation, alteration or change of ownership — [MyProperty FAQ](https://myproperty.co.za/faq/legal/new-gas-compliance-certificates). A third source says that on sale a new certificate must be obtained regardless of the age of the existing one, and that the seller is responsible unless the parties agree otherwise — [STBB](https://stbb.co.za/thought-of-the-week-gas-certificate-of-conformity/) and [Chas Everitt](https://chaseveritt.co.za/news/which-compliance-certificates-may-be-required-when-buying-a-property-in-south-africa). **Do not publish a validity period without checking the current regulation directly.**
- What an inspection covers, per a Cape Town inspection provider: cylinder storage within safety zones, isolation and emergency valves, ventilation, and a pressure leak test for system continuity, against SANS 10087-1 — surfaced via [STBB / CoC provider material](https://stbb.co.za/thought-of-the-week-gas-certificate-of-conformity/) **[3P]**
- **Verifying a competitor's SAQCC claim is possible but manual:** an installer's own guide describes searching the SAQCCGas site by town or suburb, selecting LPG as the gas type and domestic or commercial as the skill — [Give It Gas](https://giveitgas.co.za/finding-a-qualified-gas-installer/) **[SELF/3P, and the guide is dated — current site layout may differ]**. I could **not** read the SAQCC Gas or LPGas SA registers programmatically, so **no competitor's registration claim in this document has been independently confirmed. [GAP]**

### Inferences
- Publishing a current, verifiable SAQCC number next to a live register link would put a new entrant immediately ahead of five of seven competitors on the single trust signal this market is built on.
- The combination **SAQCC number + current LPGas SA certificate + public liability/contractors-risk cover + a stated workmanship warranty length** is held by *no single company* in this set. Gas Viper holds three of four but shows a six-year-old certificate and no warranty; The LpGas Man holds the warranty but no number.
- Appliance-warranty handling is a completely vacant trust topic — and it is exactly the question a homeowner buying a R30,000 hob actually has.

### Gaps
- SAQCC Gas and LPGas SA public registers not machine-readable from here; no independent confirmation of any registration number, including Gas Viper's 5389 and Cape Gas Installations' 0366.
- Current statutory CoC validity period unresolved (two vs five years across sources).

## Cross-cutting cautions for the report writer

- **Every rating and review count in this document is second-hand.** No Google Business Profile, Hellopeter business page, SAQCC Gas register or LPGas SA register could be read directly with the tools available. Attribute accordingly: "Kemp Jonker *claims* 50+ five-star Google reviews on its own site"; "Snupit *displays* Gas Viper at 4.5 from 11 reviews."
- **The two SAQCC numbers found (Gas Viper 5389, Cape Gas Installations 0366) are published claims, not verified registrations.**
- **Do not assert the recurring-complaint patterns** (no-shows, quote vs final price, no certificate issued) — the review base was too thin and Hellopeter's gas category is empty and unclaimed. The absence of complaints here is a data gap, not a clean record.
- **CoC validity period is contradicted across sources** (two years vs five years). Check the regulation before publishing a number.
- **Prices from The LpGas Man contradict themselves within one page.** Quote the range, not a single figure, and say it is that company's published "from" pricing.
- **Several competitor sites carry visible defects or staleness** (2017 award references, 2019–2020 certificates and testimonials, a compromised site, broken internal links, a wrong postcode). Where a date is attached, cite it — it is itself a competitive finding.
- **Site content was read in October 2026**, but much of the *content* on these sites is years old. Read-date currency is not content currency.
