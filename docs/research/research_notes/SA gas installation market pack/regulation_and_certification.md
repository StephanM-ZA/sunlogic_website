# Legal, Regulatory and Certification Framework for LPG and Natural Gas Installation and Servicing in South Africa

> **Research date:** 15 October 2026. **Verification caveat:** I could reach secondary-source summaries and index pages of the primary regulations, but several primary documents would not load (City of Cape Town resource server refused connection; the SAQCC Gas registration-categories page 404'd; the current incorporation notice R.262 of 2017 404'd; SANS standards are paywalled). **The most recent independent source I could date on any substantive point is 2024.** I found **no evidence of any 2025 or 2026 amendment** to the Pressure Equipment Regulations — but I also could not affirmatively verify the 2026 position, because no primary government source would load. Treat every "current" claim below as "verified to the date stated, not re-verified to October 2026." Flagged individually throughout.
>
> **Copy-writing warning:** the single most commonly repeated error in South African gas marketing copy is treating SANS clause numbers and distances as verified law. Almost every distance figure circulating online traces back to secondary blog summaries, not to the standard. I could not verify a single numerical distance against SANS 10087-1 itself (the standard is paywalled). See the "Do not publish without verification" list at the end of Question 5.

## Q1. Who is legally permitted to install or service gas appliances in South Africa?

### Takeaway

Only an "authorised person" may install or remove a gas appliance, gas system or gas reticulation system — this is a hard legal prohibition in regulation 17(1)(c) of the Pressure Equipment Regulations, 2009, not industry practice. Critically, however, the regulation's own definition of "authorised person" is generic ("registered as competent within the scope of work") and does **not** name SAQCC Gas; SAQCC Gas derives its authority from a mandate/appointment by the Department of Employment and Labour rather than from being named in the regulation text.

### Cited Findings

- **The prohibition (national law).** Regulation 17(1)(c) of the Pressure Equipment Regulations, 2009 prohibits any person from installing or removing "a gas appliance, gas system, or gas reticulation system unless the person is an 'authorised person'" — [Pressure Equipment Regulations, 2009, reg 17, via Acts Online/source.acts.co.za](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_17_gas_reticulation_equipment_and_systems.php)
- **Regulation 17(1) also prohibits** (a) handling, storing or distributing gas (including filling containers) otherwise than in accordance with the relevant health and safety standard incorporated under section 44 of the OHS Act; (b) installing or removing an appliance, pressure equipment or gas system otherwise than in accordance with the relevant safety standard; and (d) using pressure equipment or systems for gas otherwise than in accordance with the relevant safety standard — [PER reg 17](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_17_gas_reticulation_equipment_and_systems.php)
- **Definition of "authorised person" in the PER** is, verbatim: "means a person who is registered as competent within the scope of work" — [PER reg 1 (Definitions)](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_1_definitions.php). **SAQCC is not mentioned anywhere in the definitions regulation**, and the regulation does not define "gas installation", "gas appliance" or "gas reticulation system"; "appliance" is defined by cross-reference to SANS 1539 — same source.
- **SAQCC Gas's mandate.** SAQCC Gas (South African Qualification and Certification Committee for Gas) states it "registers gas practitioners on behalf of the Department of Employment and Labour" in four industries: **Natural Gas; Liquefied Petroleum Gas; Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Gas; and Compressed Industrial and Medical Gases** — [saqccgas.co.za](https://www.saqccgas.co.za/)
- Earlier reporting confirms the same four-industry structure and adds the scope restriction: "gas practitioners may not install, repair or service gas equipment outside their designated speciality" — [Engineering News, 22 July 2021, "Why registration of persons working on gas matters"](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/why-registration-of-persons-working-on-gas-matters-2021-07-22) (I could not fetch this article directly — HTTP 403 — so the quotation is as returned in search results; treat as single-source.)
- **Number of registration categories is disputed between sources.** One HVAC&R trade article states the initiative "has culminated in the establishment of 14 distinct categories of registration, each with its own set of qualifications and stringent penalties for non-compliance", and that "practitioners must be registered within their specific category to legally operate in the field" — [Refrigeration & Air Conditioning, "Bridging the gap: navigating regulatory frameworks for the HVACR sector"](https://refrigerationandaircon.co.za/bridging-the-gap-navigating-regulatory-frameworks-for-the-hvacr-sector/). This 14-category figure appears in only one source and **could not be corroborated**; the four-industry grouping above is the only structure confirmed by SAQCC Gas itself.
- **Not every registered practitioner may sign a CoC.** In the refrigeration/air-conditioning category group (Category A), only "A2" practitioners (domestic, beverage coolers, light commercial refrigeration) and "A4" practitioners (room air-conditioning units) may sign a Certificate of Conformity; "others in that category 'A' group will not be able to sign a CoC as they generally work under supervision" — [Refrigeration & Air Conditioning (archived SARACCA-related pages)](https://refrigerationandaircon.co.za/registration-for-refrigeration-gas-practitioners/). **These are refrigeration categories, not LPG/natural gas categories** — do not transpose the A2/A4 codes to LPG work.
- **Registration validity:** "A gas practitioner is issued with a 3-year license"; renewal requires proof of ongoing competence or refresher training, and the relevant industry association's Registrations Committee assesses competence before SAQCC Gas approves — [Mining Weekly / Engineering News SAQCC Gas coverage, 2020–2021](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/how-gas-compliance-works-during-covid-19-2020-06-24)
- **How registration is verified (three routes).** (1) SAQCC Gas hosts a public "Find SAQCC Gas Authorized Practitioner" search, filterable by province, SAQCC Gas registration number, surname, town/suburb, company, gas name and skill; (2) practitioners hold a digital authorised-practitioner card viewable in the Electronic CoC App, showing photo, personal details, the Department of Employment and Labour logo, and qualifications on the reverse; (3) a National Database for Authorised Practitioners is published on the SAQCC Gas website — [saqccgas.co.za](https://www.saqccgas.co.za/); [Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/how-gas-compliance-works-during-covid-19-2020-06-24)
- Industry consumer guidance: "A registered LPGas installer will carry their SAQCC Gas registered license card. It details the installer's scope as well as the expiry date of the license card" — [Engineering News, "Safety tips for LPG cylinders for heating and cooking", 29 August 2024](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/safety-tips-for-lpg-cylinders-for-heating-and-cooking-2024-08-29)
- **The LPGSA's role is different from SAQCC Gas's.** The Liquefied Petroleum Gas Association of South Africa (lpgas.co.za) operates a **product**-side verification regime, not a practitioner registration regime: importers of appliances, hoses and regulators need a **Verification Permit** from LPGSA before selling in South Africa, and local manufacturers/importers of cylinders need a **Cylinder Verification Permit**. It also publishes a registered-installer listing, a "safe appliance database" searchable by appliance model number, and training routes to becoming a registered LPG installer, distinguishing domestic, commercial and industrial installers — [lpgas.co.za](https://www.lpgas.co.za/)
- **Naming conflict worth flagging for copy.** Sources use at least three names for the LPG trade body: "LP Gas Safety Association of Southern Africa (LPGAS)", "Liquefied Petroleum Gas Safety Association of Southern Africa", and "Liquefied Petroleum Gas Association of South Africa (LPGSA)". The organisation's own site presents itself as the **Liquefied Petroleum Gas Association of South Africa** — [lpgas.co.za](https://www.lpgas.co.za/). The "Safety Association" form appears in third-party sources such as [SAIAT CPD](https://cpd.saiat.org.za/lp-gas-installations). Use the name as it appears on lpgas.co.za.

### Inferences

- Because the PER defines "authorised person" generically rather than by naming SAQCC Gas, the strictly correct legal formulation for website copy is: *the law requires an authorised person (registered as competent within the scope of work); in practice the Department of Employment and Labour has mandated SAQCC Gas to perform that registration, so in practice this means a currently-registered SAQCC Gas practitioner holding the correct category for the work.* Copy that says "the law requires a SAQCC-registered installer" is substantively right but technically a compressed paraphrase.
- The "scope of work" wording in the definition is what gives legal force to category limits. A practitioner registered for domestic LPG who signs off a commercial reticulation job is arguably not an "authorised person" *for that work* — which is the strongest compliance argument a contractor can make in copy.
- The three-year card expiry means "SAQCC registered" is a perishable claim. Any copy asserting registration should be paired with a verifiable registration number so a client can check it on the SAQCC Gas database.

### Gaps

- **The authoritative list of LPG and natural gas registration category codes and names could not be obtained.** The SAQCC Gas registration-categories page returned HTTP 404 and no other source reproduced the list. I cannot tell you whether the categories are coded (e.g. "L1", "L2", "N1") or what the precise domestic/commercial/industrial/reticulation/autogas boundaries are. This must be obtained directly from SAQCC Gas before any copy names a category.
- Whether "autogas" (vehicle LPG conversion) is a separate SAQCC Gas category — unverified.
- Whether a *company* (as opposed to an individual practitioner) can or must be registered — unverified. All sources describe individual practitioner registration.
- Whether servicing/maintenance (as distinct from installation/removal) legally requires an authorised person. Regulation 17(1)(c) textually covers "install or remove", and 17(1)(b) covers installing or removing. **The word "service" or "maintain" does not appear in the reg 17(1) extracts I obtained.** Regulation 17(1)(a) and (d) do capture "handling" and "using" gas equipment contrary to the relevant standard, which is the likely hook, but I could not confirm this from the verbatim text.

## Q2. What is the legal basis? OHS Act, Pressure Equipment Regulations, and 2024–2026 amendments

### Takeaway

The chain is: Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993 → Pressure Equipment Regulations, 2009 (Government Notice R.734, published 15 July 2009) → health and safety standards incorporated under section 44 of the OHS Act by a separate notice (R.735 of 2009, later replaced by R.262 of 2017). I found no evidence of any 2024–2026 amendment to the PER, but I also could not verify the current incorporation notice's contents.

### Cited Findings

- **Parent Act:** Occupational Health and Safety Act, 1993 (Act No. 85 of 1993) — [Acts Online, OHS Act index](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_pressure_equipment_regulations.php)
- **The regulations:** Pressure Equipment Regulations, 2009, published under **Government Notice No. R.734**, dated **15 July 2009** — structure confirmed by the regulation index, which lists reg 1 (Definitions), reg 17 (Gas reticulation equipment and systems), reg 20 (Offences and penalties), Annexure 1 (Certificate of Conformity for Gas Installations), Annexure 2 (Registration of a Steam Generator) — [PER index](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_pressure_equipment_regulations.php)
- **Commencement:** secondary legal sources state regulation 17(3) "has applied since **1 October 2009**" — [Property Professional / conveyancing commentary](https://propertyprofessional.co.za/2023/07/03/a-new-gas-coc-is-now-required/) and [RE/MAX SA, compliance certificates](https://www.remax.co.za/blog/article/heres-what-you-need-to-know-about-compliance-certificates). The 1 October 2009 commencement date is from secondary sources only — I did not see it in the gazette text.
- **Standards are incorporated by a separate notice under section 44 of the OHS Act.** The incorporation notices are: **Notice No. R.735 of 2009** (dated 15 July 2009) — marked as **replaced by Notice No. R.262 of 2017**; and **Notice No. R.79 of 2012** — [Acts Online, Incorporation of Health and Safety Standards into the PER](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr79_incorporation_of_health_a.php)
- **Offences and penalties (reg 20).** A person who contravenes or fails to comply with any of regulations 3 to 19 (the text begins "Any person who contravenes or fails to comply with any of the provisions of regulations 3, 4, 5, 6, …") "shall be guilty of an offence and liable upon conviction to a fine or to imprisonment for a period not exceeding **12 months**"; for a continuing offence, "an additional fine of **R200,00** for each day on which the offence continues", or alternatively one additional day of imprisonment per day, "Provided that the period of such additional imprisonment shall not exceed **90 days**" — [PER reg 20](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_20_offences_and_penalties.php)
- **No South African PER amendment found for 2024–2026.** A targeted search for "Pressure Equipment Regulations amendment 2024 2025 draft gazette" returned **only United Kingdom, Irish and EU instruments** (UK Pressure Equipment (Safety) (Amendment) Regulations 2024; EU Directive 2014/68/EU amendments) — none of which apply in South Africa. **Do not confuse these with South African law.** Search returned no South African gazette results.
- **Separate regulatory regime that may apply in parallel:** Major Hazard Installation Regulations, 2022, assented to 13 November 2022, published as **Government Notice R2989 of 2023** — [Lawlibrary, MHI Regulations 2022](https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za/act/gn/2023/r2989/eng@2023-01-31). See Q6.

### Inferences

- Because standards are incorporated by a *separate* gazette notice rather than written into the PER, the technical requirements can change without the regulations changing. This means "current as of October 2026" compliance depends on which edition of SANS 10087-1 the live incorporation notice names — which is precisely the fact I could not verify (see Gaps).
- The R200/day continuing-offence fine is trivially small in 2026 terms and has evidently not been uprated since 2009. Copy should therefore **not** lean on the statutory fine as the deterrent; the real exposure is civil liability and insurance (see Q7).

### Gaps

- **The contents of Notice R.262 of 2017 — the operative incorporation notice — could not be retrieved** (404). I therefore cannot state which *editions* of SANS 10087 and the other standards currently have legal force. This is the single most important unverified item in this research.
- Notice R.79 of 2012's contents could not be retrieved (redirect loop on acts.co.za).
- Whether the PER has been amended at any point between 2017 and October 2026 — **unverified**. I found no amendment, but absence of a search result is not proof of absence. The Department of Employment and Labour site and the Government Gazette index should be checked directly.
- Whether the OHS Act itself has been replaced or substantially amended by 2026 (there has been long-standing talk of an OHS Amendment Bill) — not researched; outside the scope I could cover in the available calls.

## Q3. What exactly is a gas Certificate of Conformity?

### Takeaway

The gas CoC is created by regulation 17(3) of the PER, must be in the form of Annexure 1, and is triggered by four events: installation, modification, alteration, **or change of user or ownership**. The regulation itself prescribes **no validity period** — the widely-quoted "five years" is industry practice, not law. Since 2021–2023 the CoC has moved to a compulsory electronic format issued through the SAQCC Gas app; paper CoCs ceased to be valid.

### Cited Findings

- **The obligation.** Regulation 17(3) requires "an authorised person or approved inspection authority to issue a certificate of conformity, in the form set out in Annexure 1, after a gas installation, modification, alteration, or change of user or ownership" — [PER reg 17](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_17_gas_reticulation_equipment_and_systems.php)
- **Pre-commissioning inspection (reg 17(2)).** After installation or re-installation and **before commissioning**, "the user shall ensure that an external inspection and a leak test are performed by an authorised person or an approved inspection authority" — same source. Note the duty here falls on the **user/owner**, not the installer.
- **What the Annexure 1 form must contain** (six parts) — [PER Annexure 1, Certificate of Conformity for Gas Installations](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_annexure_1_certificate_of_conformity_for_gas_installations.php):
  1. **Authorised person's details:** full name, registration number, ID number, postal or physical address, telephone number.
  2. **Installation identification:** street, stand number, name of building, name of farm, number of farm, township/municipality/district.
  3. **Gas details:** name of gas supplier, type of gas, **amount of gas stored on the premises in kilograms**.
  4. **Compliance declaration:** the authorised person declares they inspected and tested the installation, and that in terms of regulation 17(3) the installation complies with regulation 17(2) and is safe.
  5. **Liability warning, verbatim on the form:** "I am aware that I am liable to prosecution in the case of a false declaration."
  6. **Sign-off:** signature and date.
- **Electronic CoC timeline.** The SAQCC Gas electronic CoC system "was launched on the 1st March 2021" — [Mining Weekly, "Moving into the Digital Space: Electronic COCs", 18 May 2021](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/moving-into-the-digital-space-electronic-cocs-2021-05-18). Paper CoCs were then phased out: "practitioners are required to start using the app to ensure that they meet legal requirements according to the Pressure Equipment Regulations" — [Engineering News, "Electronic certificate of conformity now compulsory", 9 September 2022](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/electronic-certificate-of-conformity-now-compulsory-2022-09-09). "As of April 1 this year, any Certificate of Conformity (CoC) issued by paper is no longer considered valid" — [Mining Weekly, "SAQCC Gas discusses new electronic CoCs at Securex 2023", 7 June 2023](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/saqcc-gas-discusses-new-electronic-cocs-at-securex-2023-2023-06-07). CoC books were withdrawn: "the online CoC for both Android and iOS has resulted in the book form of the paper CoC being discontinued … a CoC can only be issued via the online portal" — [Refrigeration & Air Conditioning (undated)](https://refrigerationandaircon.co.za/certificates-of-conformity-via-app/). SAQCC Gas's own site carries an announcement headed "End of the road for paper CoC's" — [saqccgas.co.za](https://www.saqccgas.co.za/)
- **Prescribed form from 1 May 2023, five pages.** "From 1 May 2023 all gas COCs must be in the prescribed form"; the certificate is a **five-page** document; "gas compliance companies may not use any other format" and "conveyancers can no longer accept a gas COC in any other format for a property transfer" — [Property Professional, "A new gas COC is now required", 3 July 2023](https://propertyprofessional.co.za/2023/07/03/a-new-gas-coc-is-now-required/); corroborated by [MyProperty, "What are the changes regarding Gas Compliance Certificates in South Africa?"](https://myproperty.co.za/faq/legal/new-gas-compliance-certificates)
- **Validity — sources conflict.** (a) One attorney position: "while there is no formal regulation on validity, the Gas Association typically recognises certificates as valid for five years" — [Maya on Money, "A buyer's and seller's guide to the five Certificates of Compliance"](https://mayaonmoney.co.za/a-buyers-and-sellers-guide-to-the-five-certificates-of-compliance/). (b) A stricter firm position: on a sale "a new certificate must be obtained, regardless of how old or recent the existing one may be" — [STBB, "Thought of the week: Gas Certificate of Conformity", 24 January 2017](https://stbb.co.za/thought-of-the-week-gas-certificate-of-conformity/). (c) Most estate-agency sources say "about five years" — [RE/MAX SA](https://www.remax.co.za/blog/article/heres-what-you-need-to-know-about-compliance-certificates). **The PER text I obtained contains no validity period at all.**
- **Who may issue — a source conflict worth noting.** The regulation says "an authorised person or an approved inspection authority" — [PER reg 17](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_17_gas_reticulation_equipment_and_systems.php). Several secondary sources instead say it "must be issued by an authorised person who is registered with the Liquefied Petroleum Gas Safety Association of Southern Africa (LPGAS)" — [SAIAT CPD, LP Gas Installations](https://cpd.saiat.org.za/lp-gas-installations); same claim repeated at [RE/MAX](https://www.remax.co.za/blog/article/heres-what-you-need-to-know-about-compliance-certificates). **This appears to be an error propagating through secondary sources**: the registration body mandated by the Department of Employment and Labour is SAQCC Gas, not the LPG trade association — [saqccgas.co.za](https://www.saqccgas.co.za/). Copy should say SAQCC Gas.
- **Who pays on a sale.** "A seller is responsible for obtaining this certificate, although the parties can agree to make it the purchaser's responsibility"; if the installation fails inspection the seller typically carries the repair cost before transfer — [Rawson, "What Documents and Compliance Certificates Do Sellers Need?"](https://blog.rawson.co.za/what-documents-and-compliance-certificates-do-sellers-need); [STBB](https://stbb.co.za/thought-of-the-week-gas-certificate-of-conformity/)

### Inferences

- **On the property-transfer question — the actual legal position.** Gas is *unlike* electrical compliance in an important way. For electricity, the Electrical Installation Regulations expressly bar a user or lessor from allowing a transfer without a valid certificate. For gas, regulation 17(3) instead imposes a duty on the **authorised person or AIA to issue** a CoC on "change of user or ownership". So: (a) there **is** a genuine regulatory trigger on change of ownership — this is not a myth; but (b) the regulation does not, on the text I obtained, make the *transfer itself* unlawful or make registration of transfer in the Deeds Office conditional on a CoC. In practice, the requirement bites because conveyancers and standard sale agreements make it a condition of transfer, and because the five-page prescribed form is the only one conveyancers will accept since 1 May 2023. **Correct copy formulation:** "Regulation 17(3) requires a Certificate of Conformity to be issued on change of ownership of a property with a gas installation, and conveyancers will not proceed to transfer without one." **Incorrect copy formulation:** "the law prohibits transfer of a property without a gas CoC" — I could not verify that stronger claim and the mechanism appears to be contractual/conveyancing practice rather than a statutory bar.
- **Provincial variation: none found.** The PER is national legislation applying uniformly. I found **no source suggesting the CoC-on-transfer requirement differs by province.** It does, however, differ by sale agreement (which party obtains it, and whether a fresh one is required). Copy should state the requirement is national.
- The "five-year validity" claim is the clearest example of (c) industry practice being repeated as (a) law. Safe copy: "there is no statutory expiry date for a gas CoC; the industry convention is to treat certificates as current for about five years, and a fresh certificate is required on each change of ownership."
- Because the CoC is now electronic and issued only through the SAQCC Gas portal/app, a CoC's authenticity is independently checkable — a genuine differentiator for a compliant contractor, and a reason a client should be suspicious of a PDF or paper certificate.

### Gaps

- **What the five additional pages of the 2023 prescribed form actually contain** — not found. No source reproduced the five-page form. The Annexure 1 fields listed above are from the 2009 regulation text; the 2023 form is evidently expanded (likely with an itemised inspection checklist) but I could not obtain it.
- Whether the five-page form was introduced by a gazette amendment or by SAQCC Gas administratively — **unverified and important.** If administratively, it is arguably (c) industry/registrar practice rather than (a) law, notwithstanding that it is practically mandatory.
- Whether a CoC is required when a single appliance is **replaced** (as distinct from "installation, modification, alteration"). Most secondary sources imply yes; the regulation text does not use the word "replacement". Unverified.
- Whether a *lease* (change of "user" without change of ownership) triggers a CoC. The regulation says "change of user **or** ownership", which textually suggests yes, but I found no source addressing tenancies.

## Q4. Which SANS standards apply?

### Takeaway

The operative list is set by the section 44 incorporation notice. From Notice R.735 of 2009 the gas-relevant incorporated standards are SANS 10087 (Parts 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 10), SANS 1539, SANS 1237, SANS 10019, SANS 347 and SANS 10227. **No edition years are stated in the notice I could read**, and the notice I could read has been replaced by one I could not.

### Cited Findings

- **Full list of standards incorporated by Notice R.735 of 2009 (15 July 2009) — twelve standards, no edition years stated in the notice** — [Notice No. R.735 of 2009, Acts Online](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr735_notice_no__r__735_of_200.php):

| SANS no. | Title as listed in the notice |
|---|---|
| SANS 347 | Categorisation and conformity assessment criteria for all pressure equipment |
| SANS 10227 | Criteria for the operation of inspection authorities performing inspections in terms of the Pressure Equipment Regulations |
| SANS 10019 | Transportable metal containers for compressed gas: Basic design, manufacture, use and maintenance |
| SANS 1475-1 | The production of reconditioned fire-fighting equipment: Part 1: Portable and wheeled (mobile) rechargeable fire extinguishers |
| **SANS 10087** | **The handling, storage, distribution and maintenance of liquefied petroleum gas in domestic, commercial and industrial installations — Parts 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 10** |
| SANS 10147 | Refrigeration systems including plants associated with air-conditioning systems |
| SANS 1539 | Appliances operating on liquefied petroleum gas: Portable and mobile appliances, Safety aspects |
| SANS 1237 | Single-stage low-pressure regulators for liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) |
| SANS 329 | Industrial thermal processing equipment: Safety requirements for combustion and fuel-handling systems |
| SANS 10105-1 | The use and control of fire-fighting equipment: Part 1: Portable and wheeled (mobile) fire extinguishers |
| SANS 1910 | Portable refillable fire extinguishers |
| SANS 1567 | Portable rechargeable fire extinguishers: CO2 type extinguishers |

- **Note which SANS 10087 parts are incorporated: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 10. Parts 5 and 9 are not in the list.** Same source.
- **SANS 10087-1 (domestic) scope.** The 2024 edition covers "LPG installations involving gas storage containers of individual water capacity not exceeding **500 L** and a combined water capacity not exceeding **3 000 L** per installation" — [SAIAT CPD, LP Gas Installations](https://cpd.saiat.org.za/lp-gas-installations). The identical scope wording appears in the superseded 2008 Edition 5 — [SANS 10087-1:2008 (archive copy, superseded)](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf), which also records that it supersedes SANS 10087-1:2004 (edition 4).
- **SANS 10087-1 current edition.** SAIAT states the standard "was reviewed in **2024**" and refers to "SANS 10087-1 (2024)". A SAIAT CPD course title advertises it as the **7th edition**, though the course body text does not itself state an edition number — [SAIAT CPD](https://cpd.saiat.org.za/lp-gas-installations). **Edition 7 / 2024 should be treated as probable but not confirmed.**
- **SANS 10087-2 scope:** "Installation of LPG systems in mobile units, including but not limited to caravans, motor homes, park homes and mobile kitchens" — [SANS 10087 listing](https://fliphtml5.com/psuxj/pbxe/SANS-10087/) (third-party flipbook host; low-reliability source, title/scope only).
- **SANS 827 — natural gas.** Not in the R.735 incorporation list above. A CSIR project specification describes SANS 827 as covering "the installation of pipes and appliances for use with natural gas", Part 1, and requires that "all piping, fittings and components of the Methane system shall comply with SANS 827", that "the gas container manifold and supply system shall be designed, installed, commissioned, inspected and maintained in accordance with ANNEX A of SANS 827", and that "the location of the installation shall comply with the requirements of SANS 827" — [CSIR, Annexure C: Gas Storage & Reticulation Layout](https://www.csir.co.za/sites/default/files/Documents/Annexure%20C-%20Gas%20Storage%20%26%20Reticulation%20Layout.pdf)
- **SANS 1539 in practice:** the same CSIR specification applies SANS 1539 to set "all applicable safety distances and maximum allowable capacities for appliances on the gas system" — same source. **SANS 1237:** "Low pressure regulators shall comply with SANS 1237" — same source.
- **SANS 10400 link.** A South African LP gas guide attributes the non-combustible-roof condition for reducing cylinder-to-window distance to **SANS 10400** (the National Building Regulations deemed-to-satisfy rules) rather than to SANS 10087-1 — [TWK Agri, "LP Gas Regulations"](https://www.twkagri.com/post/lp-gas-regulations). I could not verify which SANS 10400 part.

### Inferences

- **SANS 827 is the natural-gas analogue of SANS 10087 but does not appear in the 2009 incorporation list.** Either it was added by R.79 of 2012 or R.262 of 2017 (both of which I could not read), or natural-gas installations are caught by reg 17(1)'s general "relevant health and safety standard" wording with SANS 827 being the relevant standard by industry consensus rather than by incorporation. **This distinction matters a great deal for natural-gas copy** and must be resolved before publishing any claim that SANS 827 is legally mandated.
- SANS 10087-3 is named in the user's brief as "liquefied petroleum gas installations in commercial premises". Part 3 **is** in the incorporated list, but **I could not verify its title or scope from any source** — the one listing that covered the series was cut off at Part 3. Do not state Part 3's scope in copy without checking the SABS store listing.
- Because the incorporation notice names SANS 10087 without edition years, the legally binding edition is ambiguous on the face of the notice. The practical reading is that the current published edition applies. For a 2026 installation this means the 2024 edition of Part 1.

### Gaps

- **Edition years for every standard** are unverified — the notice does not state them and the SABS store (store.sabs.co.za) was not reachable in my searches.
- **SANS 10087-3 (commercial), -7 (storage and filling) and -10 scopes and current editions: not verified.** The user's brief assumes Part 7 is "storage and filling" and Part 3 is commercial; these assumptions are plausible and consistent with the series title but I found no source confirming them.

## Q5. Key technical compliance rules a homeowner or business owner would recognise

### Takeaway

I could verify two sets of figures from reasonably credible South African sources — the City of Cape Town's household cylinder limits and a trade guide's separation distances — but **I could not verify a single one against SANS 10087-1 itself**, and the circulating figures disagree with each other. Everything in this section must be checked against the standard before publication.

### Cited Findings

- **Indoor cylinder limits (City of Cape Town guidance).** The City's gas requirements guidance sets household indoor limits of **9 kg in a flat** and **19 kg in a house**, and states that containers "must never sit below ground level or on top of cooking or heating appliances". It also recommends using a properly accredited installer for gas stoves — [City of Cape Town, "Gas requirements" (PDF)](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Procedures%2c%20guidelines%20and%20regulations/Gas_requirements.pdf). **Reliability caveat: the City's resource server refused connection on two fetch attempts (ECONNREFUSED 165.25.1.32:443), so these figures come from a search engine's extraction of that PDF rather than from my own reading of it. Re-verify before publishing, and confirm the document is the current version.**
- **Insurer's version of the indoor limit.** Santam's Head of Non-Motor Claims advises "storing only bottles **under 19 kg** indoors" — [Insurance Biz, "With outages expected every third day, is it time to get gas?", 30 March 2022](https://insurancebiz.co.za/news/non-life/with-outages-expected-every-thrid-day,-is-it-time-to-get-gas.html). Note this is a general "under 19 kg" and does not distinguish flats from houses.
- **Outdoor separation distances (South African trade guide, secondary).** [TWK Agri, "LP Gas Regulations"](https://www.twkagri.com/post/lp-gas-regulations) lists:
  - "**1 m** away from any opening (into a building) that is below the level of the container valve, doors, windows and airbricks"
  - "**2 m** away from any drain, pit or manhole"
  - "**3 m** away from any opening (or window) directly above the containers"
  - "The 3 m distance from the top of the cylinder, valve connection or the manifold whichever is the higher, to the bottom of the window(s) may be reduced to a minimum of **300 mm**" — subject to a non-combustible roof extending over the cylinders to give a 1.5 m safety distance, or steel window frames with small non-openable glazing. The page attributes the roof conditions to **SANS 10400**, not SANS 10087-1.
- **The 1 m figure is corroborated by two independent South African sources.** Santam cites "SANS guidance on keeping gas bottles at least a metre from doors and windows" and on "sleeving copper pipes that pass through walls" — [Insurance Biz](https://insurancebiz.co.za/news/non-life/with-outages-expected-every-thrid-day,-is-it-time-to-get-gas.html). A property portal reports the same: "Gas bottles may not be installed less than 1 meter sideways from doors and windows; All copper pipes going through a wall must be sleeved", attributed to "the South African National Standards (SANS) guide under gas installation regulations" — [Property24, "How to safely and legally install gas appliances in your home"](https://www.property24.com/articles/how-to-safely-and-legally-install-gas-appliances-in-your-home/31165)
- **Ventilation.** Industry guidance: "Make sure that the area in which you use your LPGas appliance is well-ventilated"; "Do not detach cylinders, regulators or any connections while the LPGas appliance is in use" — [Engineering News / LPGas consumer guidance, 29 August 2024](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/safety-tips-for-lpg-cylinders-for-heating-and-cooking-2024-08-29). Santam adds "ensuring ventilation" and "not opening the flame too high" — [Insurance Biz](https://insurancebiz.co.za/news/non-life/with-outages-expected-every-thrid-day,-is-it-time-to-get-gas.html). **No source gave a quantified ventilation requirement (e.g. free area in mm² or air changes per hour).**
- **Container capacity ceiling for the domestic standard.** SANS 10087-1 applies to individual containers up to **500 L water capacity** and a combined **3 000 L** per installation — [SAIAT CPD](https://cpd.saiat.org.za/lp-gas-installations). Installations above this fall outside Part 1.
- **Appliance conformity.** Consumers can verify an appliance meets SABS standards by entering the model number (from the data badge) into the LPGSA "safe appliance database" at lpgas.co.za — [Engineering News, 29 August 2024](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/safety-tips-for-lpg-cylinders-for-heating-and-cooking-2024-08-29). Separately, importers of appliances, hoses and regulators require an LPGSA Verification Permit to sell in South Africa, and cylinder manufacturers/importers require a Cylinder Verification Permit — [lpgas.co.za](https://www.lpgas.co.za/)

### Inferences

- The commonly-stated rule set — *9 kg in a flat, 19 kg in a house, nothing below ground level, nothing in a cupboard, nothing above a stove, 1 m from openings, 2 m from drains, 3 m under a window* — is internally consistent across the South African sources I found, and the "below ground level" and "on top of cooking appliances" prohibitions are in a municipal document, which gives them at least local-authority standing in Cape Town.
- **"No cylinders in cupboards" could not be sourced at all.** No source I found stated it. It is plausible (it follows from ventilation and below-valve-level opening logic) but it is currently in category (d): commonly repeated, unverified.
- **The 48 kg cylinder rule the brief asks about could not be sourced.** No source I found mentioned 48 kg cylinders or any rule specific to them. Do not publish a 48 kg figure.
- The TWK Agri page's attribution of the roof condition to SANS 10400 rather than SANS 10087-1 is a useful signal that the circulating figures are a blend of two standards. Copy that attributes all of them to "SANS 10087" is likely to be partly wrong.

### Do not publish without verification against the standard itself

Every figure in this section. Specifically: the 9 kg / 19 kg flat-vs-house split; 1 m, 2 m, 3 m and 300 mm distances; the 1.5 m roof condition; and any clause number. **And do not publish at all** without a source: any 48 kg cylinder rule, any cupboard prohibition, any quantified ventilation figure, any boundary-line distance, any ignition-source distance, any pipe-material list, any bulk-tank separation distance. ### Gaps

- Pipe materials and routing rules beyond "copper pipes through walls must be sleeved": not found.

## Q6. Additional approvals for commercial installations

### Takeaway

Commercial work adds a municipal layer on top of the national PER: in Cape Town, any LPG installation over 100 kg (and any installation under 100 kg where pipe is embedded in a concrete floor) requires a Flammable Substance Certificate with approved plans submitted to Fire and Rescue via Building Development Management. Above certain quantities a separate national regime — the Major Hazard Installation Regulations, 2022 — also engages, but I could not obtain the LPG threshold.

### Cited Findings

- **Flammable Substance Certificate (FSC) triggers, City of Cape Town.** Fire and Life Safety officers inspect sites where an FSC applies, covering "typically gas in excess of **100 kilograms**, flammable materials in excess of **200 litres**, or cases where a gas plan has been submitted for approval" — [City of Cape Town, Gas requirements (PDF)](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Procedures%2c%20guidelines%20and%20regulations/Gas_requirements.pdf) *(search-extracted; server refused direct fetch — re-verify)*
- **When plans must be submitted.** "Anyone intending to install or use LP gas over 100 kg, or to install under 100 kg where the pipe is embedded in a concrete floor, must submit prescribed plans with the FSC application form to the Fire and Rescue Service via the Building Development and Planning department" — same source. Plan requirements include:
  - Scales: site plans 1:1000 to 1:100; layout plans 1:100, 1:50 or 1:20
  - Drawings clear, legible, dated and **signed by the owner**
  - Site plans to show property boundaries, north direction, the proposed installation, street name and nearest cross road
  - Layout drawings to show pipework and distances to other flammable installations, openings in walls, drains and flues; regulator and safety valve positions; fire extinguisher locations
  - A sectional drawing for any underground crossings
- **Annual registration with the fire department for >100 kg (secondary).** "For businesses, all LPG installations that exceed 100 kg must be registered with the local fire department on an annual basis" — [Property24](https://www.property24.com/articles/how-to-safely-and-legally-install-gas-appliances-in-your-home/31165). **Single secondary source; the annual-renewal element is not corroborated by the City document extract.**
- **Underlying by-law.** The City of Cape Town Community Fire Safety By-law (2002) is the instrument governing flammable substance certification in Cape Town — [City of Cape Town Community Fire Safety By-law, 2002, via Lawlibrary](https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za-cpt/act/by-law/2002/community-fire-safety). I did not read its gas provisions.
- **Occupancy certificate link.** When applying for a certificate of occupancy, the City may require a fire installation or fire protection compliance certificate — [City of Cape Town, Apply for a certificate of occupancy](https://www.capetown.gov.za/City-Connect/Apply/Planning-building-and-development/Building-plan-applications/Apply-for-a-certificate-of-occupancy). Note the City's page states the form is no longer available to download.
- **Separate application route for fuel-burning equipment.** Commercial pizza ovens, braais and fireplaces fall under an Air Quality Management By-law application, **not** the fire-safety FSC route — [City of Cape Town, Application to install pizza oven / commercial braai or fireplace (PDF)](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Forms,%20notices,%20tariffs%20and%20lists/ApplicationToInstallPizzaOvenCommBraaiOrFireplace.pdf); [Application to install fuel burning equipment (PDF)](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Forms,%20notices,%20tariffs%20and%20lists/Application%20to%20install%20fuel%20burning%20equipment.pdf). **This is directly relevant to restaurant work: a restaurant with both a gas reticulation system and a wood-fired or commercial oven needs two separate municipal applications to two different City departments.**
- **Major Hazard Installation Regulations, 2022 (national).** Assented 13 November 2022, published as Government Notice R2989 of 2023. They apply to "(a) major hazard installations; (b) establishments with the prescribed quantity of substances listed in Chapter 1 or 2; and (c) major pipeline establishments." Establishments are tiered as **lower hazard, medium hazard and high hazard**; qualifying quantities are in **Annexure A (pages 33–45)**, and a quantity at or above the qualifying quantity must be "verified by an Approved Inspection Authority (AIA)" to bring the establishment within the regulations — [MHI Regulations, 2022, Lawlibrary](https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za/act/gn/2023/r2989/eng@2023-01-31); explanatory notes referenced as Notice 2841 of 2024, Government Gazette 51628.

### Inferences

- The commercial compliance stack for a Western Cape restaurant or commercial kitchen is therefore **three-layered**: national (PER reg 17 — authorised person + CoC), municipal fire (FSC + approved plans + inspection, triggered at 100 kg), and municipal air quality (separate application for fuel-burning equipment). Plus potentially national MHI at bulk quantities. This is a strong, verifiable structure for commercial-page copy.
- The 100 kg threshold is low — roughly five or six 19 kg cylinders — so most restaurants with gas cooking will exceed it. This is a defensible and useful claim for copy, though the arithmetic should be presented as arithmetic, not as a quoted rule.
- The "pipe embedded in a concrete floor" sub-trigger below 100 kg is unusually specific and worth citing verbatim: it catches small installations that owners assume are exempt.
- Because the City requires layout drawings showing "distances to other flammable installations, openings in walls, drains, and flues", the municipal plan-approval process is in practice where the SANS separation distances get enforced in Cape Town.

### Gaps

- **The LPG qualifying quantity that triggers MHI classification could not be obtained.** Annexure A of the MHI Regulations, 2022 was not retrievable. **Do not publish a tonnage figure.** In particular, a search surfaced Australian figures (200 t threshold / 20 t notification, SafeWork SA) which **do not apply in South Africa** and must not be reused.
- Other Western Cape municipalities (Stellenbosch, Drakenstein, George, Overstrand, etc.): **not researched.** I only verified City of Cape Town. Copy should say "requirements vary by municipality; in the City of Cape Town …" rather than generalising to the Western Cape.
- Whether the Western Cape provincial government imposes any requirement above the municipal and national layers: not found — likely none, since fire safety is a municipal competence, but unverified.
- Restaurant/commercial-kitchen specific requirements beyond the FSC: not found. SANS 10087-3 is the likely home of these; paywalled.
- Whether the FSC must be renewed annually, and the fee: unverified (one secondary source says annual).
- Whether the City's Gas requirements PDF is current: unverified, since the server refused connection.

## Q7. Consequences of an uncertified installation

### Takeaway

The statutory penalty is weak (a fine or up to 12 months' imprisonment, plus R200/day for a continuing offence), so the real exposure is insurance and civil liability. Insurers do state that a claim can be declined where appliances were not fitted by a registered installer and there is no certificate — but I found **no South African ombudsman determination or reported case** confirming a gas-CoC-based repudiation.

### Cited Findings

- **Statutory penalty.** Contravention of regs 3–19 (which includes reg 17) is an offence: "liable upon conviction to a fine or to imprisonment for a period not exceeding 12 months"; continuing offence attracts "an additional fine of R200,00 for each day on which the offence continues" or additional imprisonment capped at 90 days — [PER reg 20](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_20_offences_and_penalties.php)
- **Personal criminal exposure of the signing practitioner.** The Annexure 1 CoC carries the declaration "I am aware that I am liable to prosecution in the case of a false declaration" — [PER Annexure 1](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_annexure_1_certificate_of_conformity_for_gas_installations.php). A trade article notes that the CoC "is a legal requirement that falls on the registered practitioner who completes the work" — [Refrigeration & Air Conditioning, "Issuing a certificate of conformity" (archived)](https://refrigerationandaircon.co.za/issuing-a-certificate-of-conformity/)
- **Insurance repudiation.** "A home insurance company can refuse a claim for damage caused by gas appliances not installed by a registered installer and without a Certificate of Compliance" — [Property24, "How to safely and legally install gas appliances in your home"](https://www.property24.com/articles/how-to-safely-and-legally-install-gas-appliances-in-your-home/31165)
- **Insurer's own framing.** Santam's Head of Non-Motor Claims, **Rowland Ramalingam**, states that "a certificate of conformity must be obtained for the gas system", that when a property with a gas system is sold "the seller must obtain one and give it to the buyer", and that gas is safe "when installed under the regulations", with explosion and fire the main hazards — [Insurance Biz, 30 March 2022](https://insurancebiz.co.za/news/non-life/with-outages-expected-every-thrid-day,-is-it-time-to-get-gas.html). **Note: this article does not mention repudiation or state grounds for declining claims** — Ramalingam's comments are risk-management advice, not a repudiation policy statement.
- **Analogous insurer behaviour on municipal approvals.** Press reporting on solar installations records insurers taking the position that installations lacking municipal approval can jeopardise cover — [Daily Maverick, "Make sure your solar installation has municipal approval to avoid getting burnt by insurers", 11 July 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-07-11-make-sure-your-solar-installation-has-municipal-approval-to-avoid-getting-burnt-by-insurers.md). This is an analogy, not a gas case.
- **Enforcement posture.** Trade reporting frames SAQCC Gas registration as carrying "stringent penalties for non-compliance" — [Refrigeration & Air Conditioning](https://refrigerationandaircon.co.za/bridging-the-gap-navigating-regulatory-frameworks-for-the-hvacr-sector/); and a Department of Labour-focused article is headlined "Gas practitioners to adhere to DoL regulations" — [Engineering News, 3 February 2017](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/gas-practitioners-to-adhere-to-dol-regulations-2017-02-03). Neither cites enforcement statistics.

### Inferences

- **Safe copy formulation on insurance:** "Insurers have stated that a claim for gas-related damage can be declined where the installation was not done by a registered installer and there is no certificate of conformity." **Unsafe:** "insurers will repudiate your claim" or any claim that cites a specific determination or statistic — I found neither.
- The strongest *verifiable* consequence chain for copy is not criminal and not insurance but **transactional**: no CoC in the prescribed form → conveyancer will not accept it → transfer stalls → seller carries repair costs under time pressure. That chain is documented by multiple conveyancing sources (Q3) and is far better evidenced than the insurance chain.
- The reg 17(2) duty — "the user shall ensure" the inspection and leak test are done — places a statutory duty on the **owner/occupier**, not only on the contractor. That is a legitimate and well-sourced point for homeowner and business-owner copy: the obligation is theirs, not just their plumber's.
- OHS Act section 37 vicarious liability and section 8 general employer duties would, as a matter of general principle, create additional employer exposure for a commercial premises with a non-compliant gas installation. **I did not verify this and it should not be published without checking the OHS Act text.**

### Gaps

- **No South African ombudsman determination or reported court judgment on gas-CoC-based claim repudiation was found.** A targeted search returned only UK Financial Ombudsman Service decisions about British Gas boiler policies and one Australian case — none applicable. The Ombudsman for Short-Term Insurance (now part of the National Financial Ombud Scheme) determinations database was not searched directly; that is the place to look.
- No SAIA (South African Insurance Association) guidance document on gas CoCs was found.
- No enforcement or prosecution statistics from the Department of Employment and Labour were found.
- Whether a policy wording actually makes a valid gas CoC a condition precedent to cover (as opposed to insurers merely recommending one): **unverified.** This is the crux of the insurance argument and should be checked against actual policy wordings before copy asserts it.
- Whether the R200/day continuing-offence fine has ever been uprated: no amendment found.

## Q8. Servicing and maintenance obligations

### Takeaway

The one clearly mandated inspection event in the PER is pre-commissioning: an external inspection and leak test by an authorised person or AIA after installation or re-installation, before the system is used. Beyond that, I found **no legally mandated periodic inspection interval or periodic CoC requirement** for either domestic or commercial LPG installations — periodic servicing appears in sources only as a trade-body recommendation.

### Cited Findings

- **Mandatory pre-commissioning inspection and leak test.** Reg 17(2): after installation or re-installation and before commissioning, "the user shall ensure that an external inspection and a leak test are performed by an authorised person or an approved inspection authority" — [PER reg 17](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr734_17_gas_reticulation_equipment_and_systems.php)
- **Maintenance is within the scope of the governing standard.** SANS 10087's title is "The handling, storage, distribution **and maintenance** of liquefied petroleum gas in domestic, commercial and industrial installations" — [Notice R.735 of 2009](https://source.acts.co.za/occupational-health-and-safety-act-1993/per_nr735_notice_no__r__735_of_200.php). SANS 10019's title similarly includes "use and maintenance" of transportable gas containers — same source. For natural gas, the CSIR specification requires the manifold and supply system to be "designed, installed, commissioned, inspected and maintained in accordance with ANNEX A of SANS 827" — [CSIR Annexure C](https://www.csir.co.za/sites/default/files/Documents/Annexure%20C-%20Gas%20Storage%20%26%20Reticulation%20Layout.pdf)
- **Trade-body recommendation, not a stated interval.** "Keep LPGas appliances and fittings in good condition via regular servicing"; watch for "poor performance, corrosion, or minor leaks"; "People should not modify or try to repair appliances themselves" — [Engineering News / LPGas guidance, 29 August 2024](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/safety-tips-for-lpg-cylinders-for-heating-and-cooking-2024-08-29). The LPG body also runs a seasonal "winter appliance safety checks" campaign — [Engineering News, "LPGas winter appliance safety checks", 10 May 2021](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/lpgas-winter-appliance-safety-checks-2021-05-10). **Neither gives a numeric interval.**
- **Who must do it.** "LPGas appliances should only be serviced and installed by licensed LPGas installers or service companies" — [Engineering News, 29 August 2024](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/safety-tips-for-lpg-cylinders-for-heating-and-cooking-2024-08-29). Santam advises choosing "a reputable gas installer" — [Insurance Biz](https://insurancebiz.co.za/news/non-life/with-outages-expected-every-thrid-day,-is-it-time-to-get-gas.html)
- **Leak response procedure (industry guidance, not law).** "If you smell gas, close the cylinder valve and make sure the area is ventilated. Check all the connections and if in a sound state try again. If the smell persists, close the cylinder valve and call your LPGas dealer"; if there is fire or imminent danger, call the fire department — [Engineering News, 29 August 2024](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/safety-tips-for-lpg-cylinders-for-heating-and-cooking-2024-08-29)
- **Commercial periodic re-inspection exists, but as a municipal mechanism rather than a periodic CoC.** In Cape Town, Fire and Life Safety officers inspect sites where an FSC applies — [City of Cape Town, Gas requirements](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Procedures%2c%20guidelines%20and%20regulations/Gas_requirements.pdf) *(search-extracted)*; one secondary source states >100 kg installations "must be registered with the local fire department on an annual basis" — [Property24](https://www.property24.com/articles/how-to-safely-and-legally-install-gas-appliances-in-your-home/31165)

### Inferences

- **The honest position for copy: there is no legally mandated service interval for a South African LPG installation.** What the law mandates is (i) an authorised person for the work, (ii) an external inspection and leak test before commissioning, and (iii) a CoC on installation, modification, alteration or change of user/ownership. A contractor selling annual servicing should market it as best practice and as insurance/risk protection, **not** as a legal requirement — the latter would be a category (d) claim.
- The industry's de facto five-year CoC convention (Q3) functions as a soft re-inspection cycle, and a change of ownership forces a fresh inspection. For commercial premises in Cape Town, the FSC inspection regime is the closest thing to a mandated periodic check.
- Because SANS 10087's own title covers maintenance, it is likely that the standard contains maintenance provisions and possibly intervals. **This is the highest-value single thing to check in the purchased standard** — if SANS 10087-1:2024 does specify an interval, that converts the servicing pitch from category (c) to category (b).

### Gaps

- **Whether SANS 10087 (any part) specifies a maintenance or re-inspection interval: unverified** (paywalled). This is the key open question for Q8.
- Whether a periodic CoC is mandated for commercial premises: **no source found stating one is.** The absence of such a source is itself a finding, but it is not proof.
- Cylinder revalidation/retest intervals (SANS 10019 territory — cylinders are normally the supplier's property and the supplier's responsibility): not researched.
- Whether any insurer contractually requires periodic gas servicing as a condition of cover: not found.

## Appendix: the four-way classification the brief asks for, applied

**(a) What the law requires** (OHS Act 85 of 1993 + Pressure Equipment Regulations 2009, national):
- Only an "authorised person" may install or remove a gas appliance, gas system or gas reticulation system — reg 17(1)(c)
- Handling, storing, distributing, installing, removing and using gas equipment must comply with the incorporated health and safety standard — reg 17(1)(a), (b), (d)
- The user must ensure an external inspection and leak test by an authorised person or AIA after installation/re-installation and before commissioning — reg 17(2)
- A CoC in the form of Annexure 1 must be issued after installation, modification, alteration, or change of user or ownership — reg 17(3)
- Contravention is an offence: fine or up to 12 months' imprisonment; R200/day continuing, additional imprisonment capped at 90 days — reg 20
- **Municipal (City of Cape Town):** a Flammable Substance Certificate with approved plans for LPG over 100 kg, or under 100 kg with pipe embedded in a concrete floor — Community Fire Safety By-law, 2002
- **National, bulk quantities only:** Major Hazard Installation Regulations, 2022 (threshold unverified)

**(b) What a SANS standard requires** (binding only to the extent incorporated under s44 of the OHS Act):
- SANS 10087 Parts 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 10 — LPG handling, storage, distribution and maintenance in domestic, commercial and industrial installations. Part 1 applies to containers up to 500 L individual / 3 000 L combined water capacity
- SANS 1539 (portable/mobile LPG appliance safety), SANS 1237 (single-stage low-pressure LPG regulators), SANS 10019 (transportable gas containers), SANS 347, SANS 10227
- SANS 827 (natural gas pipes and appliances) — governs natural gas work in practice; **whether it is legally incorporated is unverified**
- SANS 10400 — National Building Regulations link; relevant part unverified
- **All specific distances, limits and clause numbers in circulation are unverified against the standards themselves**

**(c) Industry practice / trade body recommendation (not law):**
- Five-year CoC validity convention (no statutory expiry exists)
- The five-page prescribed CoC form and electronic-only issuance (practically mandatory via SAQCC Gas, but possibly administrative rather than gazetted)
- Regular appliance servicing; LPGSA winter safety checks
- LPGSA appliance and cylinder Verification Permits (a trade-association product regime)
- Conveyancers requiring a fresh CoC on every transfer
- Seller rather than buyer obtaining the CoC (contractual, variable by sale agreement)

**(d) Commonly repeated online but NOT verified as a legal requirement:**
- "A CoC must be issued by a person registered with the LP Gas Safety Association" — **wrong body**; the Department of Employment and Labour mandated SAQCC Gas
- "A gas CoC is legally required before a property can be transferred" — overstated; reg 17(3) requires issuance on change of ownership, but the transfer bar appears to be conveyancing practice and contract, not a statutory prohibition
- "A gas CoC is valid for five years" — no statutory basis found
- "No cylinders in cupboards" — no source found at all
