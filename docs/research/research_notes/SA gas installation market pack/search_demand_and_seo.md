# Search Demand, SERP Landscape and Content Opportunities — Gas Installation & Servicing, Cape Town / Western Cape

> **Methodology and limitation statement (read first).**
> All SERP-like observations in these notes were gathered on **15 October 2026** using a **US-biased web search index**, not a geo-located `google.co.za` session. That means:
> - I can establish **which pages and page types exist, what they contain, and which domains compete** for these queries, and I verified competitor markup and content by fetching the live pages directly (curl, 15 Oct 2026).
> - I **cannot** verify true `google.co.za` ranking order, Local Pack composition, or AI Overview presence for Cape Town users. Every statement about "what ranks" is therefore framed as **"what is competing / what the index surfaces"**, not a verified rank order. The client must re-check live positions from a Cape Town IP or a rank tracker set to Cape Town.
> - **No search volume figures were obtainable.** Google Keyword Planner requires an authenticated Ads account; no published South African volume dataset for these terms surfaced. Per the brief, clusters below are ranked by **reasoned priority** and all volumes are explicitly **unverified**. No numbers have been invented.

## Q1. What do South Africans actually search for? (keyword clusters + intent)

### Takeaway
No verifiable volume data exists for these terms, so clusters are prioritised by reasoning from observable supply-side signals: the headings, URL slugs and page titles that Cape Town installers and SA cost-guide publishers have actually built. Those signals point to three dominant demand centres — **compliance/CoC**, **appliance-specific installation (geyser, hob/stove, braai, fireplace)**, and **cost** — with suburb-level geography as a thin but real secondary layer.

### Inferences
- The absence of any Afrikaans gas-installer page in the Western Cape — a province with a very large Afrikaans first-language population — is more likely a genuine demand signal (Afrikaans speakers search in English for trade services) than a universal competitor oversight. **Treat Afrikaans as unverified and low priority; do not invest in a parallel Afrikaans site on this evidence.**
- "Convert electric geyser to gas" was not found as a built-out page on any competitor site, yet the economic case for it is heavily documented by the cost publishers (running-cost savings, load shedding). This looks like a **structural gap**, not a no-demand signal.
- Because no competitor page found carries real reviewed pricing *and* local proof *and* clean markup simultaneously, the realistic winning strategy is a **hub-and-spoke build (main service page + appliance spokes + CoC page + cost pillar)**, not one long page.

### Gaps
- **No verified search volumes for any term.** Keyword Planner needs an authenticated Google Ads account; no free SA volume dataset surfaced. The client should pull Keyword Planner (geo = Cape Town / Western Cape), Google Search Console query data, and ideally a paid tool's SA database before committing budget.
- Relative demand *between* appliance types (geyser vs hob vs braai vs fireplace) is unknown. Supply-side signals suggest geyser and hob/stove lead, but this is inference, not data.
- No data on mobile vs desktop split, or on "near me" share of local queries in SA.
- Afrikaans demand could not be quantified at all — only shown to be unserved.

## Q2. Intent classification and page-vs-section-vs-post decisions

### Takeaway
The intent split is cleanly observable from competitor architecture: transactional/local clusters have been built as standalone pages by the better competitors, cost and comparison clusters have been ceded to national publishers, and compliance sits uniquely in both camps — it is informational in phrasing but transactional in outcome, because the CoC is itself a billable job.

### Cited Findings
- Competitors that split transactional clusters into their own URLs: Core Gas runs `/gas-stove-installation/` and `/residential-gas-installation/` as separate pages — [coregasinstallations.co.za/gas-stove-installation/](https://www.coregasinstallations.co.za/gas-stove-installation/), [/residential-gas-installation/](https://www.coregasinstallations.co.za/residential-gas-installation/); National Gas Installers runs a standalone `/gas-coc-compliance-certificate/` page — [nationalgasinstallers.co.za/gas-coc-compliance-certificate/](https://nationalgasinstallers.co.za/gas-coc-compliance-certificate/)
- Competitors that keep everything on one long page: The LpGas Man's single Cape Town page (2,496 words, measured 15 Oct 2026) covers geyser, hob, braai, fireplace, CoC, repairs, generators, heaters, cages and fire-department approval as H2/H3 sections of one URL — [thegasman.co.za/pages/gas-installations-cape-town](https://thegasman.co.za/pages/gas-installations-cape-town)
- The compliance cluster is contested by **non-installers** — attorneys, bond originators, property-tech and electrical contractors — which changes the competitive calculus for that cluster: [esilaw.co.za PDF "Gas Compliance Certificate Requirements"](https://esilaw.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/The-Gas-Certificate-Revised-2.pdf), [ooba.co.za](https://www.ooba.co.za/resources/certificates-compliance/), [prospr.realestate](https://prospr.realestate/blog/compliance-certificates-selling-property), [cnaelectrical.co.za/gas-certificate-cape-town/](https://cnaelectrical.co.za/gas-certificate-cape-town/)
- Measured word counts of pages competing in this space (curl, 15 Oct 2026):
  - Kandua cost guide — **3,089 words** (longest; informational pillar) — [kandua.com](https://kandua.com/cost-guides/gas-installation-prices-in-south-africa-what-are-all-the-costs)
  - The LpGas Man Cape Town page — **2,496 words** — [thegasman.co.za](https://thegasman.co.za/pages/gas-installations-cape-town)
  - Core Gas gas-stove page — **1,356 words** — [coregasinstallations.co.za](https://www.coregasinstallations.co.za/gas-stove-installation/)
  - National Gas Installers Cape Town page — **966 words** — [nationalgasinstallers.co.za/cape-town/](https://nationalgasinstallers.co.za/cape-town/)
  - Cape Gas Installations homepage — ~70 KB of HTML and only five headings total ("Your safety is our prime concern.", a name, a phone number, "Gas Installations", "Maintenance", "Enquiry form") — a notably thin page for a business ranking on brand — [capegasinstallations.co.za](https://www.capegasinstallations.co.za/)
  - Gas Viper homepage — 35 KB, **no H1 at all**, only five H2s, zero structured data — [gasviper.co.za](https://www.gasviper.co.za/)

### Inferences
- The observable word-count band for a competitive **commercial service page** in this niche is roughly **1,300–2,500 words**; the informational **cost pillar** band is roughly **3,000+ words**. Two different targets for two different jobs.
- Several ranking sites are technically weak (no H1, no schema, five headings). That means **the bar to beat on-page is low** and a well-structured 1,500-word service page with real proof and clean markup is likely sufficient to compete on-page — the harder battle will be Local Pack signals and reviews (Q5).
- **Dedicated pages:** core service + metro; CoC; each of geyser/hob-stove/braai/fireplace; servicing & repairs; commercial. **Sections on the main page:** safety, process/how-we-install, certifications, areas served, pricing block, FAQ. **Blog posts:** cost guides, gas-vs-electric comparisons, regulation explainers, convert-electric-to-gas.
- Because non-installers (attorneys, originators) own much of the compliance cluster with purely informational content, an installer page that is informational *and* has a "book your CoC inspection" CTA can out-serve all of them on intent.

### Gaps
- Word count is correlation, not a ranking factor; I could not measure engagement, conversion or actual rank for any of these pages.
- I could not establish whether Core Gas's split-page architecture actually outperforms The LpGas Man's single-page architecture, since no rank or traffic data was available.

## Q4. Content gaps — real questions people ask that nobody answers well

### Takeaway
The richest gap is **contradiction**: the single most-asked practical question — how long a gas CoC stays valid — has at least four mutually incompatible answers across installer sites, forums and property publishers, and no page found resolves it against the actual regulation. A page that settles the contradictions with cited primary sources would be both a ranking and an AI-citation asset.

### Cited Findings — documented contradictions (each is a content opportunity)

**CoC validity — four conflicting answers, no authoritative resolution found:**
- "No set period of validity; banks and conveyancers usually require new certificates for transfer" — [mortgagecalculator.co.za](https://mortgagecalculator.co.za/compliance-certificate-calculator/)
- "2 years validity for the gas CoC" — [sapropertytools.co.za](https://sapropertytools.co.za/blog/compliance-certificates-property-sale-south-africa.html)
- Some installer sites say "valid for five years"; others say there is no fixed legislated validity and it lasts until ownership changes or the installation is altered — multiple installer sources, surfaced 15 Oct 2026
- MyBroadband forum members disagree with each other in the same threads: one says "you need to certify it annually", another says "re-certify every 5 years for a gas system" — [MyBroadband "Gas installation" thread](https://mybroadband.co.za/forum/threads/gas-installation.850720/), [MyBroadband "Gas Geyser" thread](https://mybroadband.co.za/forum/threads/gas-geyser.1239745/)

**Which body actually registers the person who signs your CoC — three competing answers:**
- **SAQCC Gas** — described as "mandated by the Department of Employment and Labour (DEL) to register competent Gas Practitioners"; the body most installer sites point to; also states the "Certificate of Conformity (CoC) is now issued digital" — [labour.gov.za SAQCC Gas presentation, OHS Conference, 27 Feb 2025 (PDF)](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Publications/Occupational%20Health%20and%20Safety/OHS%20Conference%202025%20-/Day%202%20Presentations/SAQCCGas_OHS%20DoEL%2027%20Feb%2025%20Eddie%20Cooke.pdf)
- **LPGSA / LPGSASA** — other sources say the CoC "can only be issued by an authorised and registered Liquefied Petroleum Gas Safety Association of Southern Africa (LPGSAS)" — surfaced 15 Oct 2026; see [gastrek.co.za hosted LPGSA safety tips (PDF)](https://gastrek.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/lpgsa-safety-tips-final.pdf)
- **SAGA** — for certain combustion systems, "only the Southern African Gas Association (SAGA) appointed TPE licenced Gas Practitioners can audit the system and issue a Certificate of Conformity (CoC)" — [SAGA Directive No. 2-2024 (PDF)](https://www.sagas.co.za/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/SAGA-Directive-No-2-2024.pdf)

**Terminology confusion nobody has cleaned up:** sources use "Certificate of **Compliance**" and "Certificate of **Conformity**" interchangeably for the same document, and both abbreviate to CoC — compare [coc-certificate.co.za](https://www.coc-certificate.co.za/gas-certificate-of-compliance-cape-town/) with the SAQCC and LPGSA documents above. **No page found explains that these are the same thing**, yet both phrasings are used as search queries.

**Which SANS edition applies to *my* installation — partially answered, poorly:**

- Installers are still advertising compliance to the **2013** edition in 2026 — [capegasinstallations.co.za](https://www.capegasinstallations.co.za/), [facebook.com/capegasinstallations](https://www.facebook.com/capegasinstallations/)

**Insurance consequences — strong, under-exploited angle:**

**Legal trigger events — stated but scattered:**

## Q6. Structured data — what fits, what still earns a rich result, and what competitors actually implement

### Takeaway

### Inferences
**What makes a page citable for South African gas work — a practical checklist derived from the above:**
1. **Answer first, in one sentence, directly under the question as a heading.** "Do I need a gas certificate?" → "Yes. A Certificate of Conformity is required for any new installation, alteration, or change of property ownership." Then the detail.
2. **Name the registering body explicitly and in full on first use** — "SAQCC Gas (South African Qualification and Certification Committee for Gas)" — because the acronym alone is ambiguous across SAQCC/LPGSA/SAGA, and the ambiguity is exactly what AI answers currently stumble on.
3. **Cite the regulation with its number, parent act and effective date** — "Regulation 17(3) of the Pressure Equipment Regulations under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, effective 1 October 2009." This exact string demonstrably propagates.
4. **Publish real numbers as explicit ranges, with the date they were quoted** — "R950 for the first appliance, R150 per additional appliance (quoted October 2026)". Vague "affordable rates" copy is uncitable.
5. **Name the individual practitioner, their SAQCC ID, and the expiry/renewal date**, and link to their public page on `saqccgas.co.za`. This is first-party, third-party-verifiable E-E-A-T that competitors mostly omit — and the one competitor who does it got cited for it.
6. **Resolve the known contradictions explicitly** (CoC validity; Compliance vs Conformity terminology; which body registers whom; SANS 10087-1:2013 vs :2024 vs -2) with primary citations. Being the arbiter is the highest-leverage GEO play available in this niche.
7. **Stamp dates everywhere** — "Updated October 2026", prices "as at October 2026", standard edition named. Then actually keep them current; a stale year is visibly penalised in this niche.
8. **Keep passages self-contained.** Each answer should make sense lifted out of the page, with the city and the credential inside the sentence ("A SAQCC-registered installer in Cape Town must…"), because that is how it gets quoted.
9. **Link out to labour.gov.za, SAQCC Gas, SAGA and LPGSA.** Generative engines corroborate against those domains; being co-cited with them helps.
10. **Keep `FAQPage` markup but expect no rich result from it.** Its value in 2026 is machine comprehension, not SERP display.
