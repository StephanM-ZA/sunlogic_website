# Practical scope of gas installation and servicing work in South Africa — residential and commercial (as at October 2026)

**Research date: 15 October 2026.** Time-sensitive items are date-stamped inline.

**Critical sourcing note for the report writer.** The single richest technical source available without paywall is the **full text of SANS 10087-1 Edition 5 (2008)**, which is on archive.org and which I extracted and read directly ([SANS 10087-1:2008 full PDF](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)). Nearly every hard number below (distances, vent areas, test pressures, hose life, vaporisation rates, appliance kJ/h ratings, pipe sizing) comes from that document. **The current edition is Edition 7, approved August 2024, superseding SANS 10087-1:2013 (Edition 6)** — confirmed by a South African CPD course listing ([SAIAT CPD](https://cpd.saiat.org.za/lp-gas-installations)) and by the foreword of a (dubiously hosted, licence-restricted) online copy ([fliphtml5 copy of SANS 10087-1:2024](https://fliphtml5.com/psuxj/pbxe/SANS-10087/)). **I could not obtain a clause-by-clause list of 2024 changes.** So: the 2008 numbers are the best-sourced figures available to us and most are long-standing industry practice, but **any number quoted on a public website should be hedged ("current standard requires…", "typically") rather than presented as a 2024-verified clause.** Where a website-ready claim is safe, I flag it.

## Who may legally do the work, and what a "service" or "COC" legally is

### Takeaway
All gas installation, modification and repair work in South Africa must be done by a registered installer authorised in the correct grade (domestic / commercial / industrial), registered through SAQCC Gas on behalf of the Department of Employment and Labour, and every installation must carry a Certificate of Conformity/Compliance. SANS 10087-1 requires re-inspection of the whole installation at intervals **not exceeding five years**, which is the real technical basis for the "COC valid 5 years" claim used throughout the market.

### Cited Findings
- SANS 10087-1 clause 5.2.1.1: "The installation of a container(s), complete with all associated equipment and appliances and any subsequent repair or modifications to the installation, shall be carried out by a registered installer qualified to the appropriate grade (domestic or commercial grade)." Replacement of cylinders is explicitly excluded from this requirement — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Clause 10.3: incorrect repair or adjustment "can result in hazardous conditions. Only a registered installer or a registered appliance technician, as appropriate to the appliance being installed, shall therefore carry out such repair. This also includes the **removal of appliances** from a gas system." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Clause 10.1 (inspection): "The user shall ensure that appliances, components of the installation and the distributing system are inspected at regular intervals (**not exceeding five years**) to ensure that all components are still operating effectively and that the system is leak free. All hoses used on outdoor installations shall be **replaced five years after the date of installation**." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- SAQCC Gas registers practitioners across four gas industries: **Natural Gas, Liquefied Petroleum Gas, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Gas, and Compressed Industrial and Medical Gases** — [SAQCC Gas](https://www.saqccgas.co.za/)
- SAQCC Gas is "officially appointed and mandated by the Department of Labour (DoL) to register gas practitioners, as competent within a specific scope of work"; registration is tied to the Pressure Equipment Regulations under the OHS Act, is issued for **three years**, and renewal requires proof of competence — [Engineering News, 28 Sep 2021](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/training-and-development-for-a-gas-safe-future-2021-09-28); [Mining Weekly, 28 Aug 2015](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/industry-body-sees-uptake-in-registered-gas-practitioners-2015-08-28-1)
- LPGSA runs LPG installer training split into **domestic, commercial and industrial** installer categories, and operates the Permitted Appliances Database and Permitted Cylinders Database that consumers can check; importers of appliances, hoses and regulators need an LPGSA **Verification Permit** before selling in South Africa, and cylinder manufacturers/importers need a **Cylinder Verification Permit** — [LPGSA](https://www.lpgas.co.za/)
- Under the Pressure Equipment Regulations of the OHS Act (No. 85 of 1993), all gas installations must have a Certificate of Compliance stating the installation "has been scrutinized and confirmed to be safe and leak-free", issued by an authorised person registered with LPGSA; secondary SA sources state **gas COCs are valid for five years** — [TWK Agri](https://www.twkagri.com/post/lp-gas-regulations); [Kimberley.org.za](https://www.kimberley.org.za/lp-gas-installations-legislation-safety/)
- "A plumber is not qualified to install gas unless he can present to you his SAQCC Card." — [TWK Agri](https://www.twkagri.com/post/lp-gas-regulations)
- Insurance consequence: without a valid certificate from a registered person, "if your home is damaged or destroyed, as a result of a defective gas appliance … An insurance company would be well within their rights to repudiate a claim" — [TWK Agri](https://www.twkagri.com/post/lp-gas-regulations)
- SAQCC Gas is phasing out paper COCs in favour of a **digital COC issued through an Electronic COC App** — [SAQCC Gas](https://www.saqccgas.co.za/)
- Clause 8.2 note: "Users should contact the local authority to find out if they need a **Flammable Liquids Permit**. This permit is usually required when storing gas on the premises." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Clause 8.3.1: "The local fire department shall be **consulted at an early stage** when LPG storage is planned on a site." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "5-year COC" convention almost certainly derives from clause 10.1's five-year maximum inspection interval plus the five-year automatic replacement of outdoor hoses — i.e. after five years the installation can no longer be assumed leak-free with original consumables. For website copy, "re-inspection and re-certification every five years" is safer and more accurate than "your COC expires".
- Because clause 10.3 covers **removal** of appliances, the common homeowner job of "take the old hob out so the kitchen fitter can work" is itself gas work requiring a registered person. That is a good, concrete, recognisable service-page item.
- The three installer grades (domestic / commercial / industrial) are the natural spine of a services section: it maps directly to what a given contractor is legally allowed to quote on.

### Gaps
- **I could not retrieve the actual per-category scope-of-work definitions from SAQCC Gas.** The scope-of-work page returns 404 and the "Registration Categories per Association" page only links out to further per-association pages that the fetch could not resolve ([SAQCC Gas categories page](https://www.saqccgas.co.za/gas-practioners/registration-categories-per-assoc/)). The boundary between domestic / commercial / industrial authorisation (in kg of storage, kW, or vessel size) is therefore **unverified** and should not be stated numerically in copy without checking the SAQCC/LPGSA source directly.
- No source found confirming whether a COC legally "expires" at five years or is simply superseded by the re-inspection duty.
- Current (2026) SAQCC/LPGSA registration fees and renewal mechanics not retrieved.

## Residential installation jobs, appliance by appliance

### Takeaway
The residential menu divides into cooking (hobs, freestanding stoves), hot water (instant/continuous-flow geysers, internal with balanced flue vs external), space heating (fireplaces, vent-free and flued heaters, patio heaters), outdoor/leisure (built-in braais, pool heating), laundry (gas tumble dryers) and whole-house reticulation. The technical work differs far more by **ventilation and flue obligation** than by appliance brand: the standard's dividing line is whether the appliance is high-consumption or long-running (must be flued) versus short-burst cooking (may be exempt with a minimum airbrick).

### Cited Findings — appliance gas ratings (the basis for all sizing)
SANS 10087-1 Table H.1 appliance ratings, used to size pipe and cylinders — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf):

| Appliance | Rating (kJ/h) |
|---|---|
| Gas stove, normal domestic | 42 000 |
| Gas stove, large domestic | 63 000 |
| Portable hotplate (2-burner) | 16 000 |
| Instantaneous water heater — multipoint / bath heater | 74 000 |
| Instantaneous water heater — single-point / sink heater | 37 000 |
| Space heater, large, with flue | 20 000 (Table H.1) |
| Space heater, small portable | 5 000 |
| Gaslight | 2 000 |
| Gas refrigerator | 2 000 |
| Gas iron | 3 000 |

- **Source conflict to flag:** Table 2 of the same standard rates "Space heater, large, with flue" at **37 000 kJ/h** while Table H.1 rates it at **20 000 kJ/h**. Do not quote a single figure for flued space heaters without noting the range — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Cited Findings — hobs, cooktops and freestanding stoves
- Hobs and hotplates that are open underneath "shall not be placed on any combustible surface" unless the surface is protected from burner heat (clause 6.2.12) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Built-in appliances must not be installed in small, poorly ventilated confined spaces; where an appliance requires back ventilation and is installed against a wall there must be a gap of **at least 50 mm** between appliance and wall (clause 6.2.5) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Kitchen exemption from flueing:** "Gas stoves, hotplates and sink water heaters installed in kitchens may be exempted from the requirements of 7.6.3 and 7.6.4 if they are to be used at maximum rating for short periods only. However, kitchens shall be adequately ventilated by the use of at least one airbrick (in an external wall) that has a ventilating area of **at least 160 cm²**" (clause 7.6.6) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Appliances must be installed so draughts cannot blow out burners on "low" flame (clause 6.2.4); stoves must not sit under combustible shelves or where curtains come near the cooking top (clause 6.2.8) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **The electrical interlock question — this is the real "electrical" requirement in a residential hob job.** Clause 9.1: "Electrical equipment, such as light switches and plugs, shall be at least **200 mm** away from any burner and potential point of gas release. Where it is necessary to install a gas hob together with an electric oven, **a three-point plug socket shall not be used** to connect the electric oven. A proper **isolator switch** shall be in place above the level of the hob and it shall have at least 200 mm clearance from the hob. **No electrical connection shall be made below the hob.**" — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Appliances needing to be moved for cleaning must be connected by flexible hose plus a **restraining mechanism shorter than the hose** so the hose cannot be ruptured or torn from its mountings (clause 6.2.3) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Market practice / cost signal: a basic gas stove installation is quoted as "anywhere from R2,000 to R9,000", depending on "the complexity of the installation, the type of gas stove, the length of piping required, and the need for any additional safety devices" — [Defy](https://www.defy.co.za/blog/news/faqs-gas-installation-hobs-south-africa)

### Cited Findings — gas water heating (instant / continuous-flow geysers)
- **Indoor units need a balanced flue.** Paloma's internal continuous-flow units are "installed indoors with a balanced flue"; "A balanced flue system is used to safely disperse the combusted air out and bring fresh air in. The flue can be installed horizontally or vertically." The 28 L internal commercial unit "requires Paloma-specific coaxial 'balance flue' system" — [Paloma 27L internal](https://palomagas.co.za/products/27-l-min-paloma-gas-geyser/); [Paloma 28L commercial](https://palomagas.co.za/gas-water-heater/paloma-28-l-commercial/)
- **External units** are wall-mounted outdoors in "areas with good free ventilation"; Paloma's spec sheet gives a "recommended height from ground to flue outlet of **1800 mm**" for the 20 L/min external unit — [Paloma 20L domestic](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-20-l-domestic/); [Paloma 28L commercial](https://palomagas.co.za/gas-water-heater/paloma-28-l-commercial/)
- Flow-rate ladder in the SA market: **20 L/min at 25 °C rise** ("ideal for smaller homes, flats, guest houses, home offices or outbuildings"), **26 L/min** ("medium to larger-sized homes… larger outbuildings or offices"), **27 L/min internal** (discontinued), **28 L/min internal and external commercial range, launched 2024**. Manufacturer states a 20 or 26 L/min unit handles "roughly up to 3 taps/showers used at any one time" — [Paloma range](https://palomagas.co.za/gas-water-heater/); [Paloma 20L](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-20-l-domestic/); [Paloma 26L](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-26-l-domestic/)
- **Manifolding water heaters:** "two Paloma 20 l per minute water heaters can be installed in parallel" via an **EZ-Link** system to supply one piping system — [Paloma](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-20-l-domestic/)
- **Dual-fuel capability:** "All Paloma appliances are available for use with either LPG or Natural Gas." Bathroom max temperature is limited to **50 °C** on the 26 L domestic unit — [Paloma 20L](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-20-l-domestic/); [Paloma 26L](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-26-l-domestic/)
- **Bathroom rule (safety-critical, good copy):** clause 6.2.6 — "In any bathroom in which a conventional gas water heater (geyser) is installed, the heater shall be flued to the outside and provision shall be made to ensure permanent ventilation." And the standard's own WARNING: "If instantaneous water heaters (geysers) **without a balanced flue system are fitted in bathrooms, permanent ventilation is of the utmost importance** because of the high gas consumption rate of the appliance, the small size of normal bathrooms, and the tendency of most users to keep air entry into a bathroom to a minimum." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Clause 7.6.7: "Appliances that have a high rate of gas consumption or that operate for long periods shall be equipped with flue pipes that lead to the outside air. Typical examples of such appliances are **multipoint and bath-size water heaters, showers and high-output space heaters**." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- A gas geyser's placement "must meet strict safety regulations, including being installed by a qualified gas installer who is registered with the Liquefied Petroleum Gas Safety Association of Southern Africa" — [Looksee (Standard Bank) gas geyser FAQs](https://www.looksee.co.za/looksee/home/knowledge-hub/articles/faqs-gas-geysers)
- Gas units "heat water on demand without a storage tank, so they avoid standing losses"; useful "where electricity supply is unreliable, where a fast on-demand solution is needed, or where a point-of-use application makes more sense than a centralised storage system" — [Kwikot](https://www.kwikot.com/the-complete-geyser-guide-for-south-africans)
- Market price signal (treat as unverified — the page is an unbranded website template): gas geyser installation R4,500; gas hob R2,500; gas fireplace R5,500; COC for existing installation R1,200 — [Stanley Gas Evolution listing](https://stanley-gas-evolution.ensiglo.com/)

### Cited Findings — fireplaces, heaters and patio heaters
- **Vent-free heaters need no flue** (clause 7.6.4) but must be installed per the manufacturer and per the ventilation rules; **"Vent-free space heaters shall not be installed in separately enclosed bathrooms"** (clause 6.2.7) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **The 0,37 MJ/m³ rule** (clause 6.3.2 / Annex E.5): if the total heat input of the vent-free heater plus any other gas, solid-fuel or paraffin appliance in the room exceeds **0,37 MJ per m³ of room volume**, permanent ventilation must be provided. Worked example from the standard: "A 25 MJ appliance requires a minimum room volume of 25 ÷ 0,37 = **67,57 m³**" — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Vent-free heaters in bedrooms always require permanent ventilation to outside, regardless of room size** (clause 6.3.5 / E.5.1) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Adjoining rooms may be added to the room volume **only if the opening between them is permanently open and cannot be closed by a door** (clause 6.3.4 / E.5.4). Worked example: 81 m³ + 60,75 m³ = 141,75 m³ → max installable heat input without permanent ventilation = 141,75 × 0,37 = **52,45 MJ** — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Flue connection into an existing chimney is allowed only if the bottom of the chimney is **permanently sealed** (clause 7.6.8) — the classic "convert my old wood fireplace to gas" job — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Flue geometry rules: cross-sectional area never less than the appliance's flue collar; flue run as near vertical as possible, with horizontal sections limited so their total length does not exceed **75 % of the total vertical run**, rising slightly toward the outlet so condensate drains back; **45° bends preferred**, and where a 90° change is unavoidable the bend radius must be **at least three times the flue diameter**; clearance between any heat-insulating screen and the flue pipe at least **10 mm**; rainproof terminal/cowl that does not restrict the flue; flues run indoors as far as possible and thermally insulated to limit condensation (clauses 7.6.9–7.6.18) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Where two or more appliances vent into a common flue, the common flue's cross-sectional area must be at least the combined area of the individual flues (clause 7.6.11) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Flue provision generally is governed by **SANS 1539**, as are tubing and hoses (clauses 7.6.1, 6.2.3) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- A Cape Town installer's advertised menu confirms fireplaces as a standard residential line item alongside hobs and geysers: "Hobs, gas geysers, fireplaces. Installed to SANS 10087 and certified", plus "gas fires and braais, gas stoves and ovens, as well as hot water systems" — [Atlas Plumbing & Gas / Citizen, 21 Aug 2021](https://www.citizen.co.za/randburg-sun/uncategorized/2021/08/21/gas-installations-what-homeowners-need-to-know/)

### Cited Findings — whole-house reticulation (pipe work, the actual engineering)
- **Permitted pipe and material rules (clause 7.3.3.1.1):** HDPE "shall be used for the conveyance of LPG vapour only, and shall be buried". Composite (multilayer) pipe: allowed above ground only if protected from direct sunlight; **no joints** in sections embedded in walls or floors or passing through cavity walls; restricted in ceilings; avoid solvent contact; **not closer than 150 mm to any heat source**; not for liquid supply; **maximum supply pressure 150 kPa**; not to be used as a pigtail or connected directly to a cylinder or to appliances that must be moved regularly; "The preferred type of crimp style for use in South Africa is the **'U' type**." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Pipes must not pass through the cavity of cavity walls, lift shafts, flues, ceiling voids or air ducts (clause 7.3.3.4.2); where a duct system is used, ventilation to open air must be provided at the lowest point (clause 7.3.3.3.4) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Gas vs electrical separation:** gas piping must be at least **150 mm** from parallel electrical cables, may cross them only without contact and with no joints in either line within 150 mm of the crossing, and "shall not be laid in the same service as 'Electrical Bus-Bars'" (clauses 7.3.3.4.5–7.3.3.4.6) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Each branch line in a multi-branch installation must have its own **shut-off valve** so it can be repaired without shutting the whole installation (clause 7.4.7); **hemp must not be used** as a jointing compound (clause 7.4.5) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Copper pipe sizing (Table H.2, OD in mm, at 3 kPa):** 5 000 kJ/h over <3 m → 8 mm; 40 000 kJ/h over 3–6 m → 15 mm; 40 000 kJ/h over 16–30 m → 15 mm; 80 000 kJ/h over 7–15 m → 18 mm; 120 000 kJ/h over 10–30 m → 20 mm. **Steel pipe (Table H.3, ID in mm):** 20 000 kJ/h up to 30 m → 10 mm; 100 000–120 000 kJ/h over 26–30 m → 20 mm — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Fitting allowances for sizing:** elbow or tee = **600 mm** equivalent pipe length; connector or 90° bend = **800 mm** (clause H.2) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Worked whole-house example from the standard: single-point water heater (37 000) + normal stove (42 000) + fridge (2 000) + two lights (4 000) = **85 000 kJ/h total**, carried by the main section; a 3,2 m branch at 46 000 kJ/h needs 15 mm OD copper, a 7 m branch at 4 000 kJ/h reduces to 8 mm — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Pipe-sizing adequacy test (Annex C.2):** connect a water gauge near the highest-consumption burner with all appliances at maximum, then repeat at the burner farthest from the regulator; "The piping can be regarded as adequate in size if in neither … the gas pressure is less than **2,5 kPa**." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Cited Findings — ventilation sizing (applies across all of the above)
- **Venting to outside: two vents, one high level and one low level, with a total free cross-sectional area of not less than 13 cm²/MJ of total rated heat input** (clause 6.3.3 / E.3.2) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Venting room-to-room: two vents of 300 cm² per 50 MJ/h heat input, one high and one low, provided one of the rooms is permanently ventilated to outside** (clause E.3.3) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Free area is the sum of the open holes, not the vent's outside dimensions: "There are 12 holes in a vent. If the size of each hole is 1 cm × 1 cm … then the total effective area of free air is 12 × (1 × 1) cm²." Vents "shall be installed in such a way that they cannot be blocked." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Why it matters, in the standard's own words: incorrect installation or use of LPG appliances in buildings "can give rise to … a build-up of unburnt gas, a high concentration of **carbon monoxide** and the **depletion of oxygen**"; hazard onset is when the normal 21 % oxygen content "is reduced by about a third, i.e. to ± 14 %", at which point "the flames will start to smoke and eventually go out and the people will lapse into unconsciousness and eventually suffocate" — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Flue/no-flue is the single best organising idea for a residential services page.** Hob/hotplate/sink heater in a ventilated kitchen = no flue, 160 cm² airbrick. Bath/multipoint geyser, shower heater, high-output space heater = flue mandatory. Vent-free heater = no flue but a hard room-volume calculation. A reader recognises their own case immediately from that.
- The 200 mm electrical clearance and the ban on a plug socket for the electric oven under a gas hob is the closest thing to an "electrical interlock" in SA residential practice. True combustion-air interlocks (as in UK BS 6173) appear to be a commercial-kitchen matter, not residential — see the commercial section and its gap.
- Gas tumble dryers, built-in gas braais, patio heaters and pool heating are not named as categories in SANS 10087-1 at all; they are governed by the generic appliance clauses (6.2.x), the ventilation annex, and the manufacturer's installation instructions. A contractor can honestly describe them as "appliance-specific: installed to the manufacturer's requirements within the SANS 10087 framework."

### Gaps
- **Gas tumble dryers:** found no South African source on installation requirements, typical ratings, or market availability. Cannot cite anything. (Inference only: SANS 10087-1 clause 7.6.7's "high consumption or long operating period" test would make a gas dryer a flued appliance, and dryers also need their own lint/moisture exhaust — but this is unsourced reasoning.)
- **Built-in gas braais:** no SA standard-level or supplier-level source retrieved. Only evidence found is that installers advertise "gas fires and braais" as a service line ([Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/randburg-sun/uncategorized/2021/08/21/gas-installations-what-homeowners-need-to-know/)). No figures on typical burner ratings or cylinder sizing.
- **Patio heaters:** no SA source found. The standard's "space heater, small portable type, 5 000 kJ/h" is the closest analogue but is not the same appliance.
- **Residential pool heating by gas:** I found **no usable South African source** — no sizing guidance, no kW/kJ ratings, no pricing. The only pool-heater cost guide surfaced was a US dollar-denominated page and is unusable. This is a real gap; do not invent numbers.
- Typical **residential monthly LPG consumption in kg** for a cooking-only vs cooking-plus-hot-water household: not found in any citable SA source.

## Residential supply options: 9 kg, 19 kg, 48 kg, twin-cylinder changeover, bulk tanks

### Takeaway
SANS 10087-1 governs installations up to 500 L individual / 3 000 L combined water capacity and gives explicit vaporisation rates per cylinder size, which is what actually determines whether a cylinder can keep up with demand. The indoor mass limits (9 kg in a flat, 19 kg total in a house) are the hard compliance constraints homeowners most often breach, and the standard's own recommendation for an ordinary permanent dwelling is **one 48 kg supply cylinder plus one 48 kg reserve on changeover**.

### Cited Findings — scope split
- SANS 10087-1 covers "installations involving gas storage containers of individual water capacity **not exceeding 500 L** and a combined water capacity **not exceeding 3 000 L** per installation"; **SANS 10087-3** covers installations with storage vessels of individual water capacity **exceeding 500 L**. The 2008 edition's scope line also references "containers of gas capacity not exceeding 9 kg and the storage of individual gas containers not exceeding 48 kg" in a separate context — [SANS 10087-1:2024 foreword via fliphtml5](https://fliphtml5.com/psuxj/pbxe/SANS-10087/); [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Cited Findings — vaporisation rates (Table 1), the real sizing driver
| Water capacity (L) | Nominal mass of gas (kg) | Approx. vaporisation rate (L/h) | (g/h) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | 9 | 100 | 230 |
| 34 | 13–14 | 140 | 320 |
| 45 | 19 | 200 | 460 |
| 113 | 48 | 400 | 920 |
| 454 | 196 | 1 000 | 2 300 |

— [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

- The standard is explicit that vaporisation, not capacity, is the failure mode: "The number and the size of containers recommended for an LPG installation depend on the maximum hourly consumption of the appliances served **and the lowest ambient temperature expected**. When an installation is designed, care shall be taken to guard against possible failure of the gas supply due to **vaporization problems**." The discharge of gas "diminishes slowly until it reaches a state of equilibrium with the vaporization rate", which depends on container size, remaining LPG and ambient temperature — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Cylinder-count table (Table 2)** — number of cylinders of each size needed per appliance, "based on results that are typical for **cold-winter conditions in South Africa**; in warmer conditions the requirements will be less":

| Gas stove, normal domestic | 1,50 | 0,65 | 0,32 |
| Gas stove, large domestic | 2,25 | 1,00 | 0,50 |
| Hotplate (2-burner) | 1,00 | 0,25 | 0,12 |
| Instantaneous water heater, multipoint | 4,60 | 2,00 | 1,00 |
| Instantaneous water heater, single point | 2,00 | 0,84 | 0,42 |
| Space heater, large, with flue | 2,00 | 0,84 | 0,42 |
| Space heater, small, portable | 0,40 | 0,16 | 0,08 |

— [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- The standard's note that 19 kg approximations "can usually also be used for 34 L (13/14 kg) containers" — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **The standard's default recommendation:** "One supply container (48 kg) and one reserve container of the same size are normally enough for ordinary installations in permanent dwellings." For a worked house (normal stove + single-point instantaneous heater + fridge + 8 gaslights) the total is 1,85 × 19 kg or 0,92 × 48 kg, so "Two 45 L (19 kg) containers coupled in parallel, or one 113 L (48 kg) container would … be acceptable. **The best arrangement, however, would be one 113 L (48 kg) container, with a second one in reserve.**" — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Mobile units: "A maximum combined capacity of 38 kg is allowed for installations on mobile units."** — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Cited Findings — indoor mass limits by occupancy (clause 5.2.2.1)
- **Flats (H3): maximum 9 kg per flat.**
- **Houses (including cluster and group housing not exceeding two storeys): total maximum 19 kg.**
- **Commercial premises (occupancies not otherwise named): total maximum 19 kg per unit**, provided there is a separating element complying with SANS 10400.
- **Industrial premises (D1–D4): maximum 19 kg per 600 m³ of building space, total maximum 100 kg.**
- **Special events: consult the local fire authority.**
— [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Market practice confirms the same numbers in plain language: "you are not permitted to store more than 19kg of LP gas inside your house; for a flat or apartment the maximum is 9kg" — [Citizen / Cape Town installer, 21 Aug 2021](https://www.citizen.co.za/randburg-sun/uncategorized/2021/08/21/gas-installations-what-homeowners-need-to-know/)

### Cited Findings — indoor siting rules
- **Containers shall never be located below ground level in a building, for example in cellars or basements** (5.2.2.2); never above or on top of any cooking or heating appliance (5.2.2.3); **containers inside a building shall not be manifolded** (5.2.2.7) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Floor-level ventilation to outside air is required; "An airbrick situated near the bottom of an external wall or a normal gap of **at least 6 mm underneath an external door** (or a combination of these) usually provides sufficient ventilation" (5.2.2.4) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **At least 300 mm from a gas stove**; **at least 2 m from any coal stove, reducible to 1 m if a non-combustible insulating guard is fitted** (5.2.2.5–5.2.2.6) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Cylinder-in-cupboard rules (Annex E.4):** ventilation to the outside atmosphere; if not in a cupboard, place the cylinder as close as possible to the door leading outside; otherwise provide cross ventilation via **a minimum of two airbricks in close proximity on the external wall, as low as possible to floor level** (need not be staggered, may be at the same level); a cupboard door must have ventilation (lattice door or slots) and the cupboard back should preferably vent outside; **no electrical connections or plug points in the same section as the cylinder**; the cupboard must be sealed so gas cannot leak from one section to another — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Cited Findings — outdoor siting and twin-cylinder/manifold arrangements
- **Minimum distances (clause 5.2.3.2):** at least **1 m** from any opening into a building below the level of the container valve (doors, windows, airbricks); at least **2 m** from any drain, pit or manhole; at least **3 m** from the property boundary — **unless the boundary has a firewall, in which case the containers may be next to the firewall, and where there are only two containers the boundary distance can be reduced to 1 m**; at least **3 m** from any opening or window directly above the containers, unless a non-combustible roof is installed between them — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **The glazing exception (5.2.3.7):** glazing within the safety distance is allowed only where window frames are metal, total glazing does not exceed **1 m²**, panes are **wire-woven glass not larger than 450 mm × 450 mm**, and the windows are **not openable** — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Thatch:** where cylinder installations are adjacent to or directly under a thatched or other combustible roof covering, "the local authority concerned shall be consulted in terms of the location and placement of the gas containers" (5.2.3.4) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Single-cylinder connection detail (5.2.3.5):** regulator fitted into the cylinder valve, flexible hose on the regulator outlet, free end on an approved hose nozzle which is itself affixed to the wall adjacent to the cylinder **by a holder bat**. Where the regulator is wall-bracket-mounted instead, the cylinder-to-regulator link must be an **LPG pigtail with a bull-nose fitting at one end and a threaded connection at the other** — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Twin-cylinder changeover (5.2.3.6):** "where cylinders are connected by means of a changeover valve, the **regulator and changeover switch shall be wall mounted**, with the gas supply from each cylinder being by means of individual pigtails" — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Changeover is not a manifold:** "Where more than two containers are used in an installation, the containers shall be connected into a manifold system. A changeover device connected to one container on each side shall **not** be deemed to be a manifold, however, the safety distances shall still apply." (5.2.3.8) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Manifold limits (5.2.3.9):** on a single branch, up to **ten** containers of size up to and including 113 L (48 kg), with a **maximum of 20 cylinders per installation**; or on a single branch, up to **three** containers of size up to and including 500 L (196 kg), with a **maximum of 6 containers per installation**. Clause 5.3.3 repeats: not more than ten containers per manifold branch, and **"dumpies" shall not exceed six in total**. Pigtails must not exceed **1 m** in length — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Roof-mounted cylinders (5.2.4):** permitted on fire-resistive or non-combustible construction (or sprinklered) provided total capacity connected to a manifold is **not greater than 480 kg**, groups are **at least 15 m apart**, containers are **at least 3 m from building openings** and **at least 6 m from air intakes and air-conditioning/ventilation systems**, hoses comply with **SANS 252**, parapets higher than **0,45 m** are breached with low-level ventilation openings **not more than 6 m apart** (or all interior-communicating openings are at/above parapet top), **the local fire department is advised of each installation by means of approved plans**, and cylinders are never refilled on the roof — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Larger domestic/estate bulk:** Sasol's residential reticulation product uses bulk tanks "ranging from 6 200 to 22 500 litres", and the system "can be installed to meet up to 70% of household energy requirements" — [Brand South Africa / Sasol Homegas](https://www.brandsouthafrica.com/stay-updated/quick-facts/2010/sasol-introduces-lp-gas-for-homes/)

### Cited Findings — fire protection and signage at residential installations
- **Signage (5.2.3.3): all installations must carry the no-smoking, no-naked-lights, no-unauthorised-entry and no-cellphone pictograms — PV 1, PV 2, PV 3 and PV 27 of SANS 1186-1.** — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf); corroborated by [Kimberley.org.za](https://www.kimberley.org.za/lp-gas-installations-legislation-safety/)
- **Extinguisher threshold (8.3.2): a dry powder fire extinguisher of at least 9 kg complying with SANS 1910 is required for installations of combined capacity in excess of 100 kg of gas**, installed near the installation, securely mounted and signposted — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- No loose or piled combustible material, weeds or long grass within **3 m** of any container (8.3.3); containers at least **3 m** from the centre line of the wall of bunded areas containing flammable liquids (8.3.5); where LPG is closer than 3 m to any other compressed gas, special precautions such as a firewall must be considered (8.3.6) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Overhead power lines:** "No part of an LPG container shall be located in the area **1,8 m horizontally** from a vertical plane beneath overhead electric power lines that are over 600 V." (8.3.7) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Enclosure limit (8.3.8):** "Structures such as firewalls, concrete barriers, and other similar structures shall be avoided around or over installed containers. **A maximum of two walls joined perpendicularly shall be allowed.**" — directly relevant to the popular "build me a gas cage/box" request — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Domestic installations do not require cylinders to be caged "but shall be secured in such a manner so as not to accidentally fall over"; where public access is possible, cylinders and manifold must be in a **locked cage or fenced area** — [Kimberley.org.za](https://www.kimberley.org.za/lp-gas-installations-legislation-safety/)
- Each container must stand upright, valve uppermost, on a firm level base with no danger of tilting, be accessible for swapping full/empty cylinders, be removable quickly in an emergency, and have an easily operated valve (5.2.1.2–5.2.1.3) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The trade-off story for a website is clean and defensible from the standard alone:** 9 kg is a flat/single-appliance solution capped by both the indoor mass limit and a 230 g/h vaporisation ceiling; 19 kg suits cooking-only houses; 48 kg is the standard's recommended default for a permanent dwelling and the only single cylinder that can carry a multipoint water heater (exactly 1,00 cylinder at 48 kg vs 2,00 at 19 kg); twin 48 kg on changeover removes the run-out risk; bulk is for reticulation or high simultaneous demand.
- **Cold-weather undersizing is the most underexplained residential failure.** The Table 2 note says the figures assume cold SA winters, and the vaporisation discussion explains that a cylinder with little remaining liquid in cold weather cannot maintain off-take. This is the technical cause of the "cylinder freezing / flame dying mid-shower in winter" complaint, and it is a genuinely differentiating thing for a contractor to explain.
- The 3 m boundary rule relaxing to 1 m "where there are only two containers" is a strong practical argument for twin-48 kg changeover on tight suburban plots — more capacity without triggering manifold rules or the full 3 m setback.

### Gaps
- **Household bulk tank sizes in kg (100 kg, 200 kg, 250 kg+):** I could not source these as named South African residential product tiers. The only citable residential bulk figures are Sasol Homegas's 6 200–22 500 L tanks, which are estate-scale, and SANS 10087-1's 196 kg / 454 L container and the Part 1 / Part 3 boundary at 500 L individual water capacity. **Do not state "100 kg / 200 kg / 250 kg tanks" as standard SA residential offerings without a supplier source.**
- **Refill logistics, cost per kg, bulk vs cylinder price differentials, and tank rental vs purchase economics:** no citable 2026 SA figures found. TotalEnergies says its bulk tanks are "supplied on loan to our customers" and Oryx says it offers "FREE on-loan LPG installation" with metered billing, which is the only concrete commercial-model evidence ([TotalEnergies Totalgaz LPG in Bulk](https://totalenergies.co.za/our-products/gaz/products/lpg-bulk); [Oryx Energies](https://www.oryxenergies.com/en/products-services/businesses/businesses-lpg/lpg-solutions)) — but both are single-supplier marketing and should be labelled as such.
- Whether the 2024 Edition 7 changed any of the indoor mass limits or setback distances: **unverified.**

## Commercial and industrial installation jobs

### Takeaway
Commercial LPG work in SA divides into kitchen/hospitality (restaurant ranges, fryers, grills, combi ovens, wok burners, bulk hot water), process and plant (laundries, bakeries, manufacturing heat), transport (autogas and forklift fuelling), agriculture (poultry brooding, crop drying, farm hot water), and multi-user reticulation (estates, apartment blocks, retirement villages) with sub-metering. Documentary evidence of SA commercial practice is much thinner on the open web than residential; most of what I can cite is supplier marketing plus the manifold and bulk-vessel clauses of SANS 10087-1/-3.

- US grower figure: about **300 kg of LPG per broiler batch** for brooding in a 300 ft × 42 ft insulated house using forced-air space heaters and infrared gas brooders — [Avian Advice, University of Arkansas](https://poultry-science.uark.edu/_resources/pdf/avian_advice_wi2001.pdf)
- Infrared brooder manufacturer rating: **128–225 g LPG/hour** (regulator min to max), capacity 750–1 000 chicks per unit — [Medion (Indonesia)](https://www.medion.co.id/?p=9262)
- **Fuel-switching consequence relevant to the LPG↔natural gas question:** a house running on natural gas "needs larger pipes and orifices than one on propane" — [Avian Advice](https://poultry-science.uark.edu/_resources/pdf/avian_advice_wi2001.pdf)
- Control-setting efficiency point: a narrow heater activation differential limits gas use because "chicks are not fully feathered, heaters will quickly attain the required temperature and shut off" — [Avian Advice](https://poultry-science.uark.edu/_resources/pdf/avian_advice_wi2001.pdf)

### Inferences
- The chain of SANS limits produces a clean commercial decision tree a contractor can present: 19 kg indoor cap in commercial occupancy → cylinders must go outside → >2 cylinders triggers manifolding → >20 cylinders (or >6 at 196 kg) or >500 L individual vessel triggers SANS 10087-3 bulk design → >100 kg triggers the 9 kg extinguisher and (per the standard's own instruction) early fire-department consultation → electrical equipment inside the safety distance triggers SANS 10087-3 hazardous-area zoning.
- Two-stage regulation is effectively mandatory for any bulk or long-run commercial system, because the standard's intermediate-pressure stage (>5 to ≤150 kPa) exists precisely to carry gas at higher pressure in smaller pipe from a remote tank to a second-stage regulator near the appliances, and the standard specifies a dedicated 200 kPa/10 min test for it.
- Metering/sub-metering is commercially established in SA (Oryx bills on metered readings; the Pretoria development uses pre-paid gas metering alongside water and electricity), so a contractor can legitimately offer tenant sub-metering as a service — but the regulatory basis for reselling gas to tenants is unverified (see gaps).

### Gaps
- **Restaurant appliance specifics — ranges, fryers, grills, combi ovens, wok burners — have no South African citable source in my results.** No kW/kJ ratings, no typical gas consumption, no SA sizing guidance. This is a significant hole for a commercial-kitchen services page.
- **Commercial kitchen gas interlocks:** I found **no South African standard or authority** requiring extraction interlocks. The only detailed guidance located is UK: suppliers state "all extraction systems in commercial catering establishments must have a gas interlocking system in place to comply with **BS6173 and IGEM/UP/19**", functioning to prevent gas being turned on until ventilation is fully operational, and treating canopy extraction as a power-operated flue requiring an interlock that shuts off gas on air-movement failure ([Gas Products UK](https://gasproducts.co.uk/catering/gas-interlock-extracting-systems.html?limit=24); [Caterkwik](https://caterkwik.co.uk/shop/39/129/2283/index.htm)). **Do not present interlocks as a South African legal requirement on the strength of these.** Whether SANS 10087-2 or a SANS commercial-kitchen ventilation standard imposes an equivalent duty is unverified.
- **Laundries and commercial tumble dryers, bakeries, retail/food courts, manufacturing process heat:** no SA-specific sources found describing the installation work or typical loads. Supplier pages list industries generically; the Easigas industries page returned 404 and the Oryx LPG solutions page carried only a one-line activity mention.
- **Bulk tank sizing bands and siting distances for >500 L vessels** (the SANS 10087-3 numbers): not obtained. Part 3 is paywalled and no open copy was found. **All above-ground/below-ground setback distances for bulk tanks are therefore unsourced** — do not publish numbers.
- **LPG forklift cylinder exchange and on-site decanting in SA:** nothing found. The only forklift refuelling result was a hydrogen project (Implats/HySa at Springs). SANS 10019 governs cylinder filling (see safety section) but I could not source forklift-specific practice.
- **Metering/sub-metering regulation:** no SA source on whether a body corporate or landlord may resell gas, how sub-meters must be certified, or whether NERSA/municipal approval is needed. Unresolved.
- **Gas supply contracts** (take-or-pay, minimum offtake, tank rental terms, pricing mechanisms): nothing citable beyond "tanks supplied on loan" and "billing based on metered readings".
- **Crop drying and farm hot water in SA:** no sources found.

## Natural gas vs LPG, and the Western Cape position

### Takeaway
South Africa's piped natural gas is confined to the Sasol/ROMPCO corridor serving Gauteng, Mpumalanga and KwaZulu-Natal, and that supply is in managed decline — the "gas cliff". As of September 2026 the operative cut-off for industrial third-party supply has moved from June 2026 to **July 2028**, no LNG import terminal has been built, and **the Western Cape has no reticulated natural gas at all**, so Cape Town gas work is LPG work, full stop.

### Cited Findings — the gas cliff timeline (date-sensitive; current as at Sept–Oct 2026)
- Sasol announced in **August 2023** that it would stop supplying natural gas and methane-rich gas to industrial users in **Mpumalanga, Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal at the end of June 2026** — [The Conversation, Mar 2026](https://theconversation.com/south-africa-will-run-out-of-industrial-gas-by-2028-and-70-000-jobs-could-be-lost-why-government-fixes-are-lacking)

### Cited Findings — fuel conversion work (LPG ↔ natural gas)

### Inferences
- **For a Western Cape contractor, "natural gas conversion" is a non-service.** There is no network to convert to. The honest and more useful framing is the reverse: appliances arriving in the Western Cape set up for natural gas (imports, relocations from Gauteng/KZN, second-hand equipment from a Highveld restaurant) must be **converted to LPG** — smaller injectors, different regulator pressure, re-set simmer screws, new data plate. That is a real, specific, recognisable job and it is defensible from the sources above.
- The gas cliff is, if anything, a tailwind for LPG contractors nationally: as piped supply to roughly 300 industrial users winds down from 2028, LPG and LNG-derived supply are the only near-term substitutes, and those require on-site storage, vaporisation and reticulation — i.e. installation work.
- The Western Cape's historic import dependence plus winter demand spikes is a genuine argument for twin-cylinder changeover and larger bulk storage in the region (buffer against delivery and stock interruptions) rather than purely a sizing argument.

## Servicing and maintenance: what a gas service actually consists of

### Takeaway
SANS 10087-1 defines the service content far more precisely than most SA installer websites do: a staged leak test (high, intermediate, low pressure) with specified test pressures and durations, a regulator performance check against three numerical pressure limits, and a consumables regime with hard ages — hoses and pigtails at five years, hydrostatic relief valves at five years, regulators at seven years — all inside a maximum five-year re-inspection interval.

### Cited Findings — the leak and pressure test sequence (clause 8.1)
- Installations must be tested in sequence: **(a) high-pressure stage, (b) intermediate-pressure stage (when used), (c) low-pressure stage** — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **High-pressure stage (Annex J, twin-cylinder method):** close off the low-pressure line; break the pigtail joint of container A and couple in a pressure gauge (**range 0–600 kPa, dial diameter at least 100 mm**); close container B's valve and charge the system from A; close A and **let the system stand 5 minutes for temperature equalisation**; note the pressure; **stand a further 10 minutes — if the gauge pressure does not drop, the installation is sound**. If it drops, test individual joints with **soapy water**. With pigtails disconnected, test the container valves themselves for leakage with soapy water — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Intermediate-pressure stage:** tested with inert gas, air or nitrogen at **200 kPa for a minimum of 10 minutes**, with the secondary pressure regulator outlet closed beforehand — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Low-pressure stage (Annex K, U-gauge method):** close all taps; admit gas; purge each appliance tap in turn (with a flame held near enough to ignite the issuing stream when the mixture is rich enough) and close; confirm the regulator operates at the appliances' design pressure; open tap A; close the container valve; **allow 10 minutes for temperature stabilisation**; note the U-gauge reading; close tap A; **after 10 minutes reopen tap A and re-read — a drop of more than 25 mm water gauge means there is a leak**, to be located with soapy water — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Buried/embedded pipework in critical locations:** "a pressure test of at least **400 kPa** shall be done… The pressure shall be maintained for at least **15 min** to ensure that no pressure drop occurs." Welding in these areas must be done by a competent person (welder), and no mechanical joints are allowed (clauses 7.3.3.4.7–7.3.3.4.8) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Cited Findings — regulator and pressure performance check (Annex G)
- Connect a **water manometer** near the regulator outlet, purge, then check pressure with (a) all burners alight, (b) only the smallest burner alight, (c) all burners off. The system is satisfactory if there is **no fluctuation** in (a) and (b) and if pressure:
  - **does not fall below 2,7 kPa** with all burners alight;
  - **does not exceed 3,4 kPa** with only the smallest burner alight;
  - **does not exceed 4,0 kPa** with all burners off.
— [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Pipe-sizing tables are stated "at **3 kPa** pressure", and the pipe-adequacy check requires pressure not less than **2,5 kPa** at the highest-consumption and farthest burners under full load (Tables H.2/H.3; Annex C.2) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Cited Findings — consumables with a defined life
| Component | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Flexible tubing / hose (general) | Check for rupture, cracking, perishing; replace if necessary. **"If over five years old and exposed to UV light, flexible tubing and hose shall be replaced automatically."** (7.5.9) | [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf) |
| Hoses on outdoor installations | **"shall be replaced five years after the date of installation"** (10.1) | same |
| Pigtails (manifold) | Inspect for rupture, cracking, chafing, perishing; **"If over five years old (see the date mark on the hose/brass fitting), they shall be replaced automatically."** Annex I also states pigtails "require replacement every five years" | same |
| Hydrostatic relief valve | **"shall be replaced if it is more than five years old"** (I.6.6) | same |
| Regulator | Check setting, performance, corrosion, mechanical damage; **"shall be replaced or refurbished if it is more than seven years old"** (I.6.7) | same |
| Whole installation | Inspection at intervals **not exceeding five years** (10.1); manifold systems "maintained on a planned inspection basis at intervals not exceeding five years. **If conditions demand, this period should be reduced.**" (I.6 note) | same |

### Cited Findings — the rest of the manifold/commercial service checklist (Annex I.5–I.6)
- Pre-operation: check the system for rigidity and that cylinders are correctly assembled to the manifold by their flexible pigtails; open individual cylinder valves slowly and fully and leak-check **both banks** of the manifold with an approved leak detection solution; check regulator delivery pressure setting and performance; examine any **vaporiser** against manufacturer's requirements before start-up — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Service: faulty parts may only be replaced on site; check manifold securing brackets; check pipework for **corrosion**; check all valves for free and full range of movement, positive shut-off, corrosion and mechanical damage; check gauges for correct operation and clean gauge faces; leak-test all joints with an approved leak detection solution, tightening or remaking any joint that leaks — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Hose installation and inspectability: hose must be installed "away from any position of mechanical damage and in a position where it can be inspected at appropriate intervals (see 10.1)" (7.5.4) — i.e. accessibility is itself a compliance requirement — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Cited Findings — water-side servicing (hot water)
- Budget Insurance / SA market guidance: "Experts recommend that geysers are serviced by a qualified plumber **every three years**", covering draining the tank, checking the anode, element and thermostat, removing limescale and sludge, and checking the whole system for leaks; thermostat set to about **60 °C** — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/699809/winter-insurance-warning-for-south-africans/)
- "Most South African insurers require proof of regular geyser servicing"; Kwikot's warranties "require correct installation and servicing by qualified professionals" — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/699809/winter-insurance-warning-for-south-africans/); [Kwikot](https://www.kwikot.com/kwiktok-series/episode-8/water-heater-maintenance-part-1)

### Cited Findings — the user's and supplier's duties
- "The **supplier** of gas and gas equipment shall inform the user of his responsibilities in respect of safe operation, use and maintenance" (10.2), with reference to the OHS Act duties of the supplier — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- All installation details "shall be discussed with the user to ensure that he fully understands all the details", and the user's attention drawn to the warnings in the product brochures (clause 8.2) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Market framing: "the homeowner, not the installer, carries the onus to hold the certificate" — forum practitioner report, not an authority — [4x4Community forum](https://www.4x4community.co.za/forum/showthread.php?p=2530619)

### Inferences
- A truthful, differentiated "what's in a gas service" list is directly derivable from the standard: staged leak test with stated hold times; regulator performance test against the 2,7 / 3,4 / 4,0 kPa limits; hose, pigtail, relief-valve and regulator age check against the 5/5/5/7-year rules; ventilation and vent-blockage check; flue integrity and obstruction check; valve operation and corrosion check; appliance burner and flame check. Few SA installer sites state the numbers; a contractor that does will read as competent.
- **Coastal corrosion makes the Western Cape case for a shorter interval, and the standard explicitly allows it:** the manifold inspection note says "If conditions demand, this period should be reduced", and the service checklist repeatedly centres corrosion (pipework, valves, relief valve, regulator). That is a sourced, non-marketing argument for annual or biennial servicing on a coastal site rather than riding the five-year maximum.
- The three-year electric-geyser servicing convention is water-side, not gas-side. For a gas geyser the combustion-side checks (burner condition, flame colour, flue clearance, ventilation adequacy, CO) are the part that needs a registered gas person. Presenting these as two separate maintenance streams on one appliance is accurate.

### Gaps
- **Carbon monoxide testing:** SANS 10087-1 repeatedly names CO as the hazard that ventilation and flueing exist to prevent, but **I found no South African requirement, threshold in ppm, or specified procedure for CO measurement at a gas service.** No SA source prescribes a CO test. Do not state a ppm limit.
- **Combustion analysis / flue gas analysis:** no SA requirement or procedure sourced.
- **Burner cleaning and gas-appliance servicing intervals per appliance type** (e.g. "clean the geyser heat exchanger annually"): no SA source. The only descaling interval found (annual, sooner in hard water) is from an Indian maintenance guide and is not SA guidance — [Studio Matrx (India)](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/water-heater-maintenance-india). Flag clearly if used at all.
- Whether SANS 10087-1:2024 changed any consumable intervals: **unverified.**
- Annex F ("testing of adjustment of burners") is referenced by clause 6.2.5 but I did not extract its content.

## Repairs and fault work: symptoms and causes

### Takeaway
I was able to source the *mechanisms* behind most common complaints from the standard itself, but **I found no South African troubleshooting source that lists customer-described symptoms and their causes.** The symptom-to-cause mapping below is therefore largely **inference from sourced technical clauses**, and must be labelled as such rather than presented as a cited authority.

### Cited Findings (mechanisms, from the standard)
- **Low flame / flame dying under load — undersized pipe or inadequate vaporisation.** Pipe is adequate only if pressure at the highest-consumption and farthest burners stays **≥2,5 kPa** under full load (Annex C.2); and the regulator check fails if pressure falls **below 2,7 kPa** with all burners alight (Annex G). Separately, "care shall be taken to guard against possible failure of the gas supply due to **vaporization problems**", since off-take "diminishes slowly until it reaches a state of equilibrium with the vaporization rate", which depends on **container size, remaining LPG and ambient temperature** — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Cylinder freezing / sweating / frosting — off-take exceeding the vaporisation rate.** Table 1 caps vaporisation at 230 g/h (9 kg), 460 g/h (19 kg), 920 g/h (48 kg), 2 300 g/h (196 kg) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Flame blowing out on "low" — draughts.** "Appliances shall be installed in such a way as to avoid draughts that are strong enough to extinguish the burners when they are set on 'low' flame" (6.2.4) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Smoking / sooting / yellow flame and eventual flame-out — oxygen depletion.** "If sufficient ventilation is not provided, the existing oxygen in the air will be used up, **the flames will start to smoke and eventually go out** and the people will lapse into unconsciousness and eventually suffocate" — onset when oxygen falls from 21 % to about **14 %** (Annex E.1) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Poor draught, condensation inside flue pipes — overcooled or badly routed flue.** "Overcooling of flue pipes might cause reduction of draught or heavy condensation inside the pipes (or both), therefore flues shall be run indoors as far as possible and thermally insulated"; a drain may be fitted low down in the flue. Downdraughts are prevented by correct outlet positioning and a rainproof terminal/cowl that does not restrict the flue (7.6.14–7.6.15) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Flue blockage / restriction.** "A flue shall be clear of all obstructions that might impede the flow of exhaust gases, and the cross-sectional area of the flue shall nowhere be less than that of the flue collar on the appliance. A flue pipe shall not enter into a draught diverter far enough to cause an obstruction." (7.6.9) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Leaks and where gas collects.** Avoid any position "adjacent to cellars, drains, hollows, etc., where escaping gas might collect" (5.2.1.4(d)); and the standard notes leakage can be detected "a considerable distance from the source of leakage, therefore **low-level ventilation of buildings** shall…" (clause around 4.x) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Hose failure causes.** Rupture, cracking, perishing; UV exposure; heat over **50 °C**; being torn from mountings when an appliance is moved without a restraint shorter than the hose; incorrect routing near the oven vent or flue outlet or above burner level (7.5.7–7.5.9, 6.2.3) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Corroded cylinders leak or worse.** Easigas, on finding mostly visibly corroded cylinders at 46 Gauteng retail sites: corroded cylinders "suggest improper to no maintenance at all. This presents a safety concern as they may be susceptible to **leaks, rupture or explosion**" — [Easigas investigation, June 2025 (Fuels Industry Association of SA)](https://fuelsindustry.org.za/download/292/illegal-lpg-activities/15239/fuels-industry-learnings-easigas-investigations-june-2025.pdf)

### Inferences (label as reasoning, not citation)
- **"Smell of gas"** → most likely a perished or out-of-date hose, a pigtail past five years, a loose or re-used joint, or a leaking cylinder valve. The standard's own diagnostic order is: stage the test (HP → intermediate → LP), then localise with soapy water, including testing container valves with pigtails disconnected.
- **"Pilot won't stay lit"** → draught (6.2.4), inadequate ventilation causing oxygen depletion (Annex E.1), or flue downdraught (7.6.14). I found **no SA source on thermocouple/flame-failure-device faults**, which is the other usual cause; do not assert it as sourced.
- **"Yellow flame"** → in standard terms, the oxygen-depletion/smoking pathway (Annex E.1) and/or inadequate combustion air for a built-in appliance (6.2.5, 50 mm back gap). Aeration/injector blockage is the conventional explanation but is **unsourced here**.
- **"Geyser not firing or cycling / hot-cold-hot"** → undersized pipe (<2,5 kPa at the appliance under load), vaporisation shortfall in cold weather, or simultaneous-demand exceedance (Paloma rates a 20–26 L/min unit at roughly three simultaneous outlets). Water-side scale and flow-switch faults are the other usual causes but are **unsourced for SA**.
- **"High consumption"** → undersized cylinder forcing frequent swaps rather than true overuse; or a genuine slow leak, which the staged pressure test will find; or a short-cycling appliance. No SA source quantifies this.
- **"Cylinder freezing"** → almost always off-take above the Table 1 vaporisation rate for that cylinder size at that ambient temperature, which is a *sizing* fault, not an appliance fault. This is the single most satisfying fault to explain to a customer and it is fully sourced.

### Gaps
- **No South African fault-and-symptom troubleshooting source was found at all.** Searches returned installer listings and hobbyist forum threads, not technical guidance. Everything in the Inferences block above should be presented on a website as the contractor's own diagnostic experience, not as "according to SANS".
- Thermocouple / flame failure device (FFD) / oxygen depletion sensor (ODS) faults: no SA source.
- Injector blockage, aeration adjustment, spark/piezo ignition faults: no SA source.
- Water-side faults in gas geysers (scale in the heat exchanger, flow switch, water filter screens): no SA source.
- No SA data on frequency of fault types or typical repair costs.

## Conversions and crossover work

### Takeaway
Electric-to-gas conversion of hobs and water heating is the dominant crossover job and is well supported by the installation clauses already cited (particularly the 200 mm electrical clearance and the ban on a plug socket for the oven under a gas hob). The solar/heat-pump + gas hybrid question is where the evidence is weakest: **I found no South African source describing a gas booster or gas pre-heat stage paired with solar thermal or a heat pump.**

### Cited Findings
- **Electric-to-gas hob conversion — the electrical work it forces.** Where a gas hob is installed together with an electric oven, "a three-point plug socket shall not be used to connect the electric oven. A proper **isolator switch** shall be in place above the level of the hob and it shall have at least **200 mm** clearance from the hob. **No electrical connection shall be made below the hob.**" Light switches and plugs at least 200 mm from any burner or potential point of gas release (clause 9.1) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **LPG ↔ natural gas appliance conversion** is an injector/orifice job plus simmer-screw adjustment plus a new data label, with injector orifice sizes marked on the parts; LPG→NG means **larger** injectors (see the Natural gas vs LPG section for full citations) — [Gorenje](https://partners.gorenje.com/fts/htmlNavodila/567972en.html); [Electricians Forum](https://electricianforum.co.uk/threads/gas-hob-jet-conversion-for-lpg.37894)
- **Dual-fuel appliance availability in SA** makes conversion viable: "All Paloma appliances are available for use with either LPG or Natural Gas." — [Paloma](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-20-l-domestic/)
- **Why gas water heating is chosen in SA (the conversion driver):** gas units "heat water on demand without a storage tank, so they avoid standing losses"; useful "where electricity supply is unreliable, where a fast on-demand solution is needed, or where a point-of-use application makes more sense than a centralised storage system" — [Kwikot](https://www.kwikot.com/the-complete-geyser-guide-for-south-africans)
- **Solar thermal's backup is normally electric, not gas:** "Most solar geysers also come with an **electrical backup element** to ensure you still have hot water during extended periods of no sun or high demand" — [Kwikot solar geyser guide](https://www.kwikot.com/your-complete-guide-to-solar-geysers-in-south-africa)
- Kwikot's own hybrid recommendation pairs solar with a **heat pump**, not with gas: pairing a solar geyser with a Kwikpump heat pump "ensures efficiency even during prolonged cloudy weeks"; "larger systems often justify heat pump or hybrid solutions" — [Kwikot](https://www.kwikot.com/your-complete-guide-to-solar-geysers-in-south-africa)
- Comparative efficiency claims (manufacturer, treat as marketing): heat pump water heaters "typically use 60% less electricity than conventional electric geysers" — [Kwikot, heat pump vs solar geyser](https://www.kwikot.com/heat-pump-vs-solar-geyser-which-saves-you-more-money)
- Solar savings claims conflict and should be reported as a range with the dissent: one source says solar can save "40%-60% on water heating costs"; another argues most installs are undersized and that "a high-efficiency, properly sized solar water heater saves 50% on your water heating bill, this will not result in a 50% saving on your total electrical bill" — [Kwikot](https://www.kwikot.com/your-complete-guide-to-solar-geysers-in-south-africa); [SolarStep ZA](https://solarstepza.co.za/blogs/news/solar-geysers-vs-gas-geysers-the-ultimate-guide-for-south-african-homes)
- **Cascading gas water heaters** is the sourced SA way to meet larger simultaneous demand: two 20 L/min Paloma units in parallel via EZ-Link on one piping system — [Paloma](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-20-l-domestic/)
- **Inherent technical constraint on any pre-heat scheme:** Paloma's 26 L domestic unit limits bathroom outlet temperature to a **max 50 °C**, and the 20 L unit is rated at a **25 °C temperature rise** — i.e. a continuous-flow unit's output is defined by the rise it can add to the incoming water temperature — [Paloma 26L](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-26-l-domestic/); [Paloma 20L](https://palomagas.co.za/paloma-20-l-domestic/)

### Inferences
- **The honest technical reality of "solar plus gas" in SA, reasoning from the rated temperature rise:** a continuous-flow gas heater rated at 25 °C rise is specified against cold mains input. Feed it solar-preheated water and the unit must either modulate down or it will overshoot; many units also have a **minimum activation flow and a minimum rise before they fire**, which is why a solar-preheated supply can cause a gas unit to fail to fire or to short-cycle. A correctly engineered hybrid therefore needs either a modulating unit explicitly rated for pre-heated inlet, or a thermostatic blending/bypass arrangement. **This is reasoning from the rated-rise and max-temperature specs above, not a cited SA design rule** — present it as the contractor's engineering view.
- **Gas pairs much more naturally with solar PV than with solar thermal.** PV displaces electricity in daylight; a gas geyser removes the single largest electrical load (water heating) entirely, including at night and in winter, without the tank, element, standing losses or circuit capacity that a PV-plus-electric-geyser system needs. This is consistent with Kwikot's own framing of gas as a no-standing-loss, unreliable-grid solution, but the comparison itself is inference.
- Heat-pump-plus-gas is the least likely pairing to make sense: both are efficiency plays on the same load, with a heat pump's capital cost justified by continuous use that a gas booster would then undercut. No SA source supports or rejects it.

### Gaps
- **No South African source describes a gas booster / gas pre-heat / solar-plus-gas hybrid water heating configuration at all.** This was searched directly and returned only electric-backup solar and solar-plus-heat-pump material. Any claim about combining gas with solar thermal or a heat pump must be presented as the contractor's own engineering position, not as market or standards practice.
- **Gas-to-electric conversion:** no source found; no SA guidance on decommissioning a gas installation (capping, purging, COC implications of removing gas).
- Cost comparisons for electric-to-gas water heating conversion in SA rands: not sourced.
- Whether SANS 10400-XA (energy usage in buildings) creates any obligation or incentive relevant to gas water heating: not verified — a search result mentioned it only as something to check.

## Safety: the real hazards, and illegal filling

### Takeaway
The hazard set is leaks, carbon monoxide and oxygen depletion, cylinder failure under fire or corrosion, and ventilation that has been blocked or never provided. The most acute current SA problem is the illegal filling, decanting and re-sealing of cylinders: an Easigas investigation of 46 Gauteng retail sites in May 2025 found **all of them** distributing cylinders with unauthorised seals, mostly visibly corroded, and industry sources put the illegal share of the cylinder sector at over 25 %.

### Cited Findings — illegal filling and decanting (2025–2026)
- **Easigas investigation, May 2025:** 46 retail sites visited in Gauteng; **all found to be distributing LPG cylinders sealed with unauthorised seals** (at least in the case of Easigas cylinders), and **most of the cylinders were visibly corroded**. Corroded cylinders "suggest improper to no maintenance at all… they may be susceptible to leaks, rupture or explosion" — [Easigas investigation report, June 2025](https://fuelsindustry.org.za/download/292/illegal-lpg-activities/15239/fuels-industry-learnings-easigas-investigations-june-2025.pdf)
- "Most retail site managers are aware of the unlawful activities, but they choose to persist regardless due to cheaper prices from rogues." — [Easigas, June 2025](https://fuelsindustry.org.za/download/292/illegal-lpg-activities/15239/fuels-industry-learnings-easigas-investigations-june-2025.pdf)
- **Definition of illegal refilling:** cylinders "filled with LPGas outside of the company's filling plants or its official distributors' locations", and filling by an unlicensed company. A legal operator needs a **DMRE licence, zoning certificates and a valid fire permit** — [Easigas, June 2025](https://fuelsindustry.org.za/download/292/illegal-lpg-activities/15239/fuels-industry-learnings-easigas-investigations-june-2025.pdf)
- **Legal basis:** **SANS 10019** requires that all LPG cylinders be handled and filled by competent and authorised persons; LPGSA also points to the OHS Act's Pressure Equipment Regulations as the standard for legally filled cylinders — [TimesLive, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2025-03-26-how-to-spot-unsafe-gas-cylinders-amid-rising-lpg-demand)
- **Scale claim (industry-sourced, unverified independently):** "over 25% [of the gas cylinder sector is] dominated by illegal businesses and individuals" — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/energy/864207/new-winter-mafia-taking-hold-in-south-africa/). **Note:** the search result reported this article as dated **23 June 2026**, which conflicts with the 2025 framing of surrounding coverage; verify the date before citing.
- **Two offender profiles (PayGas CEO):** large illegal refilling plants operating as syndicates, "usually under the responsibility of the South African Police Service (SAPS) and law enforcement to regulate"; and the **seasonal dealer who refills 48 kg bottles by hand, mainly in winter** — [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/energy/864207/new-winter-mafia-taking-hold-in-south-africa/)
- **Enforcement intent, not confirmed outcome:** Easigas said it planned to bring in the Fire Department and the Department of Employment and Labour for site inspections of the identified retail sites — [Easigas, June 2025](https://fuelsindustry.org.za/download/292/illegal-lpg-activities/15239/fuels-industry-learnings-easigas-investigations-june-2025.pdf). **I found no confirmation these inspections took place, and no record of SA state prosecutions or seizures.**
- Continuing local-press warnings through 2025: — [Citizen (Benoni City Times), 5 Apr 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/benoni-city-times/news-headlines/2025/04/05/beware-of-illegally-refilled-lpg-cylinders/); [Citizen (Springs Advertiser), 6 Jul 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/springs-advertiser/news-headlines/2025/07/06/illegal-gas-refills-threaten-safety/); [Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/beware-illegally-filled-lpgas-cylinders)
- Consumer verification route: LPGSA operates a **Permitted Cylinders Database** (is this cylinder verified?) and a **Permitted Appliances Database** (is this appliance safe?), plus a registered-installers listing via SAQCC Gas — [LPGSA](https://www.lpgas.co.za/)

### Cited Findings — the hazards a compliant install mitigates
- **CO and oxygen depletion:** incorrect installation or use "can give rise to a variety of hazardous conditions, such as a build-up of unburnt gas, a high concentration of **carbon monoxide** and the depletion of oxygen. The provision of ventilation is therefore of **vital importance**" (6.3.1). Annex E: hazard onset at about 14 % oxygen, with flames smoking then going out and occupants lapsing into unconsciousness — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- The standard's standing WARNING on enclosed spaces: "LPG appliances consume oxygen and, in enclosed or restricted spaces, this will cause…"; and that "too much carbon dioxide in the [room]… inside a room if the ventilation is inadequate" (clause 7.6.7 warnings) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Vents must be unblockable:** "Vents shall be installed in such a way that they cannot be blocked." (E.2) — the single most common real-world ventilation failure — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Gas collects low and travels:** containers must avoid positions "adjacent to cellars, drains, hollows, etc., where escaping gas might collect" (5.2.1.4(d)); cylinders must never be below ground level in a building (5.2.2.2); 2 m minimum from drains, pits and manholes (5.2.3.2(b)) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Fire behaviour of LPG and why you don't put it out:** "Those training to fight LPG fires shall be aware that these fires **should not normally be extinguished unless the source of LPG can be isolated**" (8.3.12) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Fire separation and access:** no combustible material, weeds or long grass within 3 m; access to and around the installation kept free of obstacles at all times for firefighting; in extreme fire risk situations the local fire department may require a **fire hose reel**; fire protection in major installations may be done by rational design per SANS 10400 (8.3.3, 8.3.10–8.3.13) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Ignition sources:** electrical equipment within the safety distance triggers SANS 10087-3 zone requirements; no part of a container within 1,8 m horizontally beneath >600 V overhead power lines (9.1, 8.3.7) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Insurance is the practical enforcement mechanism in SA residential:** without a valid certificate from a registered person, an insurer "would be well within their rights to repudiate a claim" after a gas-related loss — [TWK Agri](https://www.twkagri.com/post/lp-gas-regulations)
- Commercial/industrial sites must display emergency procedure notices near the LPG store; domestic users must be given full emergency instructions (8.3.12) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The DIY/non-compliant-install hazard profile maps almost exactly onto the clauses that get ignored:** hose longer than 2 m or run through a wall (7.5.3); hose over 5 years old or sun-exposed (7.5.9); cylinder inside above the 9/19 kg limit (5.2.2.1); cylinder in a sealed cupboard with a plug point in the same section (E.4(e)); a vent blocked or never fitted (E.2, 6.3.3); a cylinder in a basement or near a drain (5.2.2.2, 5.2.3.2(b)); two cylinders in a fully boxed-in brick enclosure rather than the permitted maximum of two perpendicular walls (8.3.8). Each is a specific, checkable thing a contractor can offer to inspect — a genuinely useful "is my existing install legal?" checklist.
- **Second-hand cylinders and illegal filling connect directly to the corrosion findings:** the Easigas evidence is that cylinders circulating through illegal channels are visibly corroded and improperly or never maintained. A contractor can legitimately advise checking a cylinder against the LPGSA Permitted Cylinders Database and buying only from licensed outlets.
- **BLEVE:** the standard does not use the term but its fire clauses (do not extinguish unless the source can be isolated; 3 m clearance of combustibles; 9 kg extinguisher above 100 kg; fire department early consultation) are precisely the mitigations for a cylinder heated by an external fire. Explaining the mechanism in plain language is fine; attributing the term to SANS 10087-1 is not.

### Gaps
- **No South African 2024–2026 news coverage of specific gas-related fires or fatalities was located**, despite being requested. Searches on illegal filling returned industry warnings and consumer advice, not incident reporting. **I found no SA casualty or incident statistics for LPG.** Do not state a number of deaths, injuries or fires.
- **No evidence of actual SA state enforcement action** — no SAPS raids, DMRE/DEL prosecutions, plant closures or seizure figures for 2025–2026. The Kenyan and Indian crackdowns that surfaced in search results are **not South African** and must not be used ([Goa, India](https://www.thegoan.net/goa-news/underweight-lpg-cylinder-racket-busted-in-sancoale/132666.html); [Kenya](https://capitalfm.africa/wandayi-tells-gachagua-to-stop-attacking-public-officers-over-lpg-crackdown/)).
- The ">25% illegal" figure is industry-sourced and the reporting date is internally inconsistent (search reported 23 June 2026 in a 2025-focused context). **Verify before publishing.**
- No SA source on CO alarm/detector requirements or recommendations for homes with gas appliances.
- Decanting specifically (small-cylinder filling from a large one) is named by the PayGas CEO only as hand-refilling of 48 kg bottles; no SA standard clause on decanting was located beyond the SANS 10019 reference.

## Cross-cutting: Western Cape / Cape Town specifics worth calling out

### Takeaway
Four things differ in the Western Cape: there is no reticulated natural gas (so everything is LPG), supply has a history of import dependence and winter tightness, coastal corrosion justifies shortening the standard's five-year inspection interval, and the Cape's thatch and estate building stock triggers specific clauses.

### Cited Findings
- **No reticulated natural gas**; only a government pre-feasibility study on importing gas via the Saldanha Bay–Cape Town corridor — [WCG study](https://stocks.metabolismofcities.org/library/43523/download/43526); see also [Business Day, 2017](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/opinion/2017-07-27-gas-shipment-heralds-a-new-energy-era)
- **Import dependence and winter demand spikes:** Afrox importing LPG through the Port of Cape Town; "demand for Handigas skyrockets in winter when stocks and availability of LPG locally have tended to run low" — [Afrox, 30 Jan 2017](https://afrox.co.za/en/news_and_media/press_releases/News%202017-01-30%20Afrox%20secures%20Western%20Cape%20LPG%20stocks.html)
- Historic Western Cape shortages driven by Milnerton and Mossel Bay refinery/plant outages — [Business Report, 2015](https://businessreport.co.za/economy/2015-07-12-shortage-sees-lpg-prices-soar/); [City Press, 2015](https://city-press.news24.com/Business/Cape-Town-is-out-of-gas-20150614)
- **Corrosion is central to the service checklist** (pipework, valves, relief valve, regulator, cylinder condition) and the standard permits a **shorter than five-year** inspection interval where "conditions demand" (I.6 note, I.6.4–I.6.7) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- Avoid "any position near corrosive … substances" when siting containers (5.2.1.4(c)) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Thatch:** where cylinders are adjacent to or directly under a thatched or other combustible roof covering, the local authority must be consulted on location and placement (5.2.3.4) — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Cold-winter sizing assumption:** the cylinder-requirement table is explicitly "based on results that are typical for **cold-winter conditions in South Africa**; in warmer conditions the requirements will be less. With experience, a registered installer will learn how the values can be modified to suit local conditions." — [SANS 10087-1:2008](https://archive.org/download/za.sans.10087.1.2008/za.sans.10087.1.2008.pdf)
- **Cape Town installers' advertised menus** (market practice evidence, not authority): Gas Viper, Sunningdale — "Gas Installation, Gas COC, Gas Cages, Gas leaks, Gas hob work", stating LPGSA membership and SAQCC registration ([Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/Company/GasViper)); Atlas Plumbing & Gas, Woodstock — "Hobs, gas geysers, fireplaces. Installed to SANS 10087 and certified", plus "Plumbing and gas compliance certificates for property sales", describing itself as "PIRB licensed · SAQCC Gas" ([via Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/randburg-sun/uncategorized/2021/08/21/gas-installations-what-homeowners-need-to-know/))
- **Property transfer is a live demand driver:** compliance certificates for property sales are an advertised service line, and a COC must still be valid when a sale closes — [Citizen / Atlas listing](https://www.citizen.co.za/randburg-sun/uncategorized/2021/08/21/gas-installations-what-homeowners-need-to-know/)
- Western Cape demand for registered compliance work is visible in recruitment: a "SAQCC licensed installer / subcontractor for risk management gas compliance, Western Cape" post — [Careers24](https://careers24.com/jobs/adverts/2240212-saqcc-licensed-installer-subcontractor-for-risk-management-gas-compliance-western-cape/)

### Inferences
- Cape Town's mild winters relative to the Highveld mean the standard's cylinder-count table is **conservative** for the Cape; the standard itself invites local adjustment. That is a legitimate, sourced basis for a Cape contractor to size differently from a Gauteng rule of thumb — in either direction, and worth saying explicitly rather than copying national guidance.
- Coastal corrosion plus the five-year consumable ages make a **two- or three-year service interval** a defensible recommendation for seafront properties, grounded in the standard's own "if conditions demand, this period should be reduced".
- Estate and body-corporate rules (cylinder cages, screening, placement) interact directly with clause 8.3.8's two-perpendicular-walls maximum and the locked-cage requirement where the public has access. A contractor who can resolve an estate aesthetic requirement against those clauses has a real, specific competence to advertise.

### Gaps
- **No source found for Western Cape / Cape Town estate or body-corporate gas rules**, or for City of Cape Town fire by-law specifics on LPG quantities, permit thresholds or plan submission. This was requested and is unresolved — the only threshold evidence is SANS 10087-1's own 100 kg extinguisher trigger and an unverified forum claim about a 100 kg fire-department-approval threshold.
- Current (2026) Western Cape LPG supply position: all supply-tightness sources are 2015–2017. **I found nothing current on 2026 Western Cape LPG availability or pricing.** Do not imply current shortages on 2015 evidence.
- No SA data on the size of the Western Cape gas contracting market, number of registered installers in the province, or typical job volumes.
