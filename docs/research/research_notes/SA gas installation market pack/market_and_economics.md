## 1. LPG pricing: how it works, and what it costs in the Western Cape right now

### Takeaway

South African LPG sold to domestic customers is subject to a **regulated maximum retail price (MRP) per kilogram**, set monthly by the Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources (DMPR, formerly DMRE) and gazetted by magisterial district zone. For the period **7 October 2026 to 3 November 2026** the MRP is **R34.56/kg at the coast (Zone 1A)**, **R37.81/kg inland (Zone 9C, Gauteng)** and **R37.08/kg for LPG imported through Saldanha Bay (Western Cape)** — the price that applies to Western Cape supply, because the province is served almost entirely by the Saldanha import terminal. Critically for website copy: **the regulated coastal LPG price is essentially unchanged in nominal terms since January 2024 (3457 c/kg → 3456 c/kg), while Eskom tariffs rose ~22.6% compounded over the same window.** That is the honest, verifiable core of the gas argument — not load-shedding.

### Cited Findings — the mechanism

- LPG is **not** priced like petrol. There is no pump price; instead the DMPR sets (a) a **Maximum Refinery Gate Price (MRGP)** per metric ton excluding VAT, and (b) a **Maximum Retail Price (MRP)** in cents per kilogram, zoned by magisterial district. For 7 Oct–3 Nov 2026 the MRGP is **R13,618.97/metric ton (755.853 c/l) ex-VAT**, except for LPG imported through the Port of Saldanha Bay, where the MRGP is **R15,525.62/metric ton (861.672 c/l)** — [CEF/DMPR Media Statement, 2 October 2026, §6](https://cefgroup.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Press-Release-02-October-2026-Change-07-October-2026.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`
- Prices are **adjusted monthly**, effective the first Wednesday of each month, under the same "agreed mechanism" that governs liquid fuels: over- or under-recovery in the prior month is corrected in the following month, rounded to the nearest full cent — [CEF/DMPR Media Statement, 2 October 2026, §7](https://cefgroup.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Press-Release-02-October-2026-Change-07-October-2026.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`
- The LPG MRP is **built up from published cost elements**, which is why the price is defensible and auditable. Full composition for 7 Oct–3 Nov 2026, in c/kg — [CEF/DMPR Media Statement, 2 October 2026, §11](https://cefgroup.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Press-Release-02-October-2026-Change-07-October-2026.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`:

| Price element | Coast (Zone 1A) | Inland (Zone 9C) | Saldanha / Western Cape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum refinery gate price | 1,361.897 | 1,361.897 | 1,552.562 |
| Primary transport costs | 65.809 | 311.955 | 65.809 |
| Operating expenses | 593.508 | 593.508 | 593.508 |
| Working capital | 44.988 | 44.988 | 44.988 |
| Depreciation | 240.135 | 240.135 | 240.135 |
| Gross margin: cylinder-filling plant | 306.838 | 306.838 | 306.838 |
| **Sub-total (1)** | **2,613.175** | **2,859.321** | **2,803.840** |
| Retail margin (15% of sub-total 1) | 391.976 | 428.898 | 420.576 |
| **Sub-total (2)** | **3,005.151** | **3,288.219** | **3,224.416** |
| VAT (15%) | 450.773 | 493.233 | 483.662 |
| **MAXIMUM RETAIL PRICE (c/kg)** | **3,456.00** | **3,781.00** | **3,708.00** |

- **The retail margin is a fixed 15% of the cost build-up, and VAT is 15% on top.** This is the single most useful structural fact for honest copy: roughly **26% of what a household pays per kg of LPG is retail margin plus VAT**, and the refinery gate price (the commodity) is only ~39–42% of the MRP — [CEF/DMPR Media Statement, 2 October 2026, §11](https://cefgroup.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Press-Release-02-October-2026-Change-07-October-2026.pdf) `[PRIMARY]` + `[MY ARITHMETIC]` (1361.897/3456.00 = 39.4% coast; 1552.562/3708.00 = 41.9% Saldanha)
- For October 2026 the increase was **42.00 c/kg nationally and 48.00 c/kg in the Western Cape** — the Western Cape gets its own adjustment because it is priced off the Saldanha import MRGP — [Minister Gwede Mantashe media statement, 5 October 2026](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/minister-gwede-mantashe-announces-adjustment-fuel-prices-effective-7-0) `[PRIMARY]`; increase of 42 c/kg confirmed in [CEF/DMPR Media Statement §1 and §9](https://cefgroup.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Press-Release-02-October-2026-Change-07-October-2026.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`
- The **Magisterial District Zone (MDZ) fuel price schedule**, which gives the zone-by-zone LPG price for a specific town, was due for publication on **6 October 2026** — [Minister's media statement, 5 October 2026](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/minister-gwede-mantashe-announces-adjustment-fuel-prices-effective-7-0) `[PRIMARY]`

### Cited Findings — coastal vs inland, and MRP vs in-store price

- The **coast-to-inland differential is small and narrowing in relative terms**: it is almost entirely primary transport cost (65.809 c/kg coast vs 311.955 c/kg inland = 246 c/kg), amplified by the 15% retail margin and 15% VAT to a 325 c/kg gap. In October 2026 the inland price is **9.4% higher** than the coastal price — [CEF/DMPR Media Statement, 2 October 2026, §11](https://cefgroup.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Press-Release-02-October-2026-Change-07-October-2026.pdf) `[PRIMARY]` + `[MY ARITHMETIC]`
- Press analysis of the 3 June 2026 zone schedule found the 9kg cylinder maximum was **R365.85 in Zone 1A (Cape Town and Durban)**, **R378.00 in Zone 9B (Johannesburg)** and **R379.35 in Zone 8B (Bloemfontein)** — a spread of only R13.50 between the cheapest and most expensive zones. The article's point is that the zoning system deliberately compresses the coastal advantage people expect — [IOL, 10 June 2026](https://iol.co.za/business/economy/2026-06-10-the-truth-about-south-africas-lpg-prices-at-the-coast-versus-inland/) `[SECONDARY]`
  - **Flag a discrepancy:** 9 × the Zone 1A coastal MRP for June 2026 (3769 c/kg) is R339.21, not R365.85. The R365.85 is closer to 9 × the Saldanha/Western Cape price. Either the article is quoting a different zone number, or the MDZ schedule's Zone 1A differs from the press release's "Coast Zone 1A" reference zone. **Do not quote the R365.85 figure as a Cape Town price without checking the MDZ schedule directly.**
- The MRP is a **ceiling, not a floor**, and real retail prices scatter around it on local competition. A June 2026 survey of advertised 9kg exchange prices found: **Durban R339; Cape Town R350–R375; Johannesburg/Pretoria R365–R369; Mbombela R345** — with more than R30 variation between retailers selling the identical quantity of gas. The article notes it could not explain why some Cape Town advertised prices sat *above* the regulated maximum — [IOL, 10 June 2026](https://iol.co.za/business/economy/2026-06-10-the-truth-about-south-africas-lpg-prices-at-the-coast-versus-inland/) `[SECONDARY]`
- The gap between the regulated gas price and the final invoice is made up of **delivery fees and connection/safety services**, which sit outside the MRP. One Cape Town supplier charges a **R35 standard delivery fee** and a one-off cylinder deposit for new installations — [Gas2Home, Cape Town, 2026](https://gas2home.co.za/blogs/news/gas-bottle-prices-2026-your-complete-guide-to-lpg-costs-in-cape-town) `[MARKET EVIDENCE]`
- The same supplier states the **Western Cape (Saldanha-import) MRP was R40.84/kg effective 1 July 2026**, following a 19.00 c/kg Western Cape increase on that date — [Gas2Home, 2026](https://gas2home.co.za/blogs/news/gas-bottle-prices-2026-your-complete-guide-to-lpg-costs-in-cape-town) `[MARKET EVIDENCE]`. Cross-check: the coastal Zone 1A price for 1 July 2026 was 3786 c/kg; R40.84 is 298 c/kg above that, which is in the right order as the Saldanha premium (252 c/kg in October 2026). **Treat R40.84 as plausible but unconfirmed against the gazette.**

- **Nominal flat over nearly three years.** Coastal LPG was **3,457 c/kg in January 2024** (per the Fuels Industry Association table) and **3,456 c/kg in October 2026** — a change of **−0.03% over 33 months**. (For reference, January 2025 coastal was 3,514 c/kg, so Jan 2025 → Oct 2026 is **−1.65%**.) Against SA CPI either window is a meaningful **real** decline.

### Inferences

- For Cape Town / Western Cape copy, **R37.08/kg (7 Oct – 3 Nov 2026)** is the defensible headline number, because Western Cape LPG is priced off the Saldanha import MRGP and received its own 48 c/kg adjustment. Using the generic "coastal" R34.56/kg would understate Cape Town's cost by 7.3%.
- The cost-element table is the best available defence against an accusation of cherry-picking: the price is a published, audited build-up, not a market quote. The independent auditors' report on the price adjustment is available on request — [CEF/DMPR Media Statement §10](https://cefgroup.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Press-Release-02-October-2026-Change-07-October-2026.pdf).
- Because retail prices scatter both below and (apparently) above the MRP, copy should say "regulated maximum" rather than "the price of gas", and should name the month.

### Gaps

- **I could not verify the exact scope of the MRP** — i.e. which cylinder sizes and which customer classes the maximum retail price legally binds (domestic cylinder sales only, or also 48kg and bulk?). Bulk LPG supplied to commercial customers appears to be commercially negotiated rather than MRP-bound, but I have no primary source for that. **This matters for restaurant/commercial copy and must be checked in the Petroleum Products Act regulations before publication.**
- **I could not retrieve the 6 October 2026 MDZ zone schedule** giving the exact gazetted maximum for Cape Town's magisterial districts. The press release's three reference zones are confirmed; the town-level schedule is not.
- **No verified Western Cape / Saldanha MRP time series.** I have only two Saldanha data points (R40.84/kg at 1 July 2026, unconfirmed; R37.08/kg at 7 October 2026, primary). The 2024–2026 series above is coastal Zone 1A and Gauteng Zone 9C only.
- **No verified cylinder deposit amounts.** No source gave an actual rand figure for a new-cylinder deposit.
- The CEF press release's §5 figure of 3,182.00 c/l is the **illuminating paraffin** SMNRP, not an LPG figure. Earlier web-search snippets conflated the two — do not repeat that error.

## 2. Electricity pricing: Eskom and City of Cape Town, 2025/26 and 2026/27

### Takeaway

Eskom's approved increases are **12.74% for 2025/26** and **8.76% for 2026/27** for direct customers (**9.01%** for municipalities from 1 July 2026) — a compounded **+22.6% over two years**. The 2026/27 figure was revised *upward* mid-stream from 5.36% after NERSA admitted a R54.7bn calculation error in Eskom's regulatory asset base, and **8.83% is already approved for 2027/28**. Cape Town's 2026/27 Domestic tariff is **400.63 c/kWh for the first 600 kWh and 480.73 c/kWh above that (VAT incl.)**, but the City is reportedly billing above the approved rates pending a dispute with the regulator — flag this.

## 3. Gas vs electricity on a like-for-like energy basis — with the working shown

### Takeaway

At the **Western Cape regulated maximum of R37.08/kg (7 Oct – 3 Nov 2026)**, LPG delivers useful water heat at roughly **R3.05–R3.45 per useful kWh**. That beats a City of Cape Town electric element geyser (**R4.45–R5.34/useful kWh**) by **~25–40%**, roughly ties Eskom Homelight 60A and Homepower, and **loses outright to Eskom Homelight 20A, to the CoCT Lifeline tariff, and very heavily to a heat pump (R1.34–R1.60/useful kWh)**. For **cooking**, the arithmetic goes the other way: on standard pan-efficiency assumptions **gas is more expensive per useful kWh than electric cooking at Cape Town tariffs**, and much more expensive than induction. Gas hobs are a performance and resilience choice, not a savings choice.

### The inputs

**Conclusion: at October 2026 Cape Town prices a gas hob is roughly 17–27% more expensive per useful kWh than a radiant electric hob and 25–50% more expensive than induction.** The absolute amounts are small (cooking is typically a minor share of a household's energy), so this is not a reason to avoid gas — but it is a reason **not to claim gas cooking saves money.**

### Honest statement of where gas loses

1. **Against a heat pump** for water heating. A heat pump at COP 3.0 runs at roughly **half the cost of gas per useful kWh** at Cape Town tariffs, and about a quarter of an electric element's. Gas only wins on capital cost (heat pump installed R27,500–R37,500 vs gas R3,000–R8,000 — [EnergyBee, 2 July 2026](https://energybee.co.za/news/cheapest-way-to-heat-water-south-africa-winter-2026)).
2. **Against solar water heating** on running cost — R200–R350/month even in winter, at an installed cost of R22,500–R42,500 — [EnergyBee, 2 July 2026](https://energybee.co.za/news/cheapest-way-to-heat-water-south-africa-winter-2026) `[SECONDARY]`.
3. **Against low-tariff electricity.** On Eskom Homelight 20A (270.30 c/kWh) or the CoCT Lifeline tariff (269.87 c/kWh), gas water heating is **at or above** the cost of an electric element. Eskom-supplied low-consumption households should not be told gas is cheaper.
4. **For cooking**, on the efficiency assumptions above.
5. **For low-usage households.** Gas's advantage is per-kWh, so a household that uses little hot water will take many years to recover install cost.
6. **Historical warning:** a controlled 2012 test by Stiebel Eltron found gas **~7% more expensive** than electricity for water heating at the prices of the time — [PowerSaving.co.za](https://powersaving.co.za/appliance-tests/water-heating/gas-vs-electric-geyser-test/) `[SECONDARY, 2012 — out of date, cite only to show the comparison is price-dependent]`. A 2018 forum calculation found a 9 kg bottle holds about 115 kWh and that gas and electricity then cost about the same per unit of energy — [PowerForum, 2018](https://powerforum.co.za/topic/2201-cost-to-heat-a-litre-of-water-using-gas-versus-eskom/) `[SECONDARY, 2018]`. (Note 115 kWh/9 kg = 12.8 kWh/kg, consistent with the 46 MJ/kg figure used above.)
7. **The fixed-charge ratchet.** Eskom shifted the fixed portion of the Homepower service and administration charge from 33.3% to **66.6%** for FY2027 and raised the Generation Capacity Charge to 30% of RTP value — [Eskom Tariffs and Charges Booklet 2026/2027, p.5](https://www.eskom.co.za/distribution/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Eskom-Tariff-booklet_Final.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`. Cape Town Domestic carries R74.77/month and Home User R424.30/month regardless of consumption — [Cape Town ETC](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/cape-town-electricity-tariffs-rise/) `[SECONDARY]`. **Gas reduces kWh, not the connection charge** — so the saving on the bill is smaller than the saving on energy, and this gap widens every year.

### Inferences

- The **defensible claim** is narrow and specific: *"On the City of Cape Town Domestic tariff in 2026/27, heating water with LPG at the regulated Western Cape maximum costs roughly 25–40% less per useful kilowatt-hour than an electric element geyser."* Anything broader is not supportable.
- The **strongest honest gas cases** are (a) City of Cape Town Domestic Block 2 and Home User Block 2 customers, (b) anyone on Residential Time-of-Use with winter peak draw, (c) rural Eskom Landlight customers, (d) small businesses on Businessrate 4 (R4.60/kWh) or CoCT SPU 2/SPU 3 (R6.40 and R7.81/kWh), and (e) anyone whose alternative is a *new* electric geyser rather than an existing one.
- The **weakest** are Eskom Homelight 20A / Lifeline households, low-usage households, and anyone who could afford a heat pump.

### Gaps

- **I could not verify a South African published calorific value for LPG** from SAPIA, LPGas SA, the DMPR or a cylinder filler. The 46–50 MJ/kg range is taken from the brief and is standard for propane/butane, and the 2018 forum figure of 115 kWh per 9 kg bottle (12.8 kWh/kg ≈ 46 MJ/kg) is consistent with it — but **this is the single most load-bearing unsourced input in the whole comparison.** Get it from a cylinder filler's product data sheet or an LPGas SA technical note before publishing.
- **I could not verify appliance efficiency figures for South African conditions** — instant gas water heater efficiency, electric geyser standing losses, or hob pan efficiencies. All are `[ASSUMPTION]`. The hob efficiencies in particular drive the "gas cooking is more expensive" conclusion; if gas pan efficiency is actually 50% rather than 40%, gas cooking becomes roughly cost-neutral with radiant electric.
- **No verified SA heat pump COP under Cape Town winter conditions.** COP 3.0 is a reasonable mid-range figure but unsourced.
- **I could not find an independent (non-vendor) 2026 SA gas-vs-electric water heating study.** Every comparison I located was published by a party selling gas, solar or heat pumps.

## 4. Market size, growth and household penetration

### Takeaway

South Africa's LPG market is small — **roughly 500,000 tonnes a year** — and has flipped from domestically supplied to **import-dependent**: local output nearly halved from 235,000 t (2020) to 123,000 t (2024) after refinery closures near Durban, while imports rose more than 140% to **589,000 t (2024)**. Household penetration is real and rising: gas rose from 2.2% to 6.8% of households as a *primary* cooking fuel, and **29.0% of households use LPG/gas at all — 46.0% in the Western Cape**, the highest province in the country. I found **no rand-value figure for the SA LPG market that I would publish.**

### Cited Findings — volume and supply

- **Market size:** PetroSA's interim CEO described the South African LPG market as **under 500,000 t/yr**, constrained by refinery outages and a fragmented transport network — [Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2757746-africa-s-lpg-growth-faces-supply-and-logistics-hurdles) `[SECONDARY, industry primary-adjacent]`
- **Domestic production collapse:** Argus Consulting data show local LPG output fell from **235,000 t in 2020 to 123,000 t in 2024** — a 48% decline — following the closure of two refineries near Durban — [Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2757746-africa-s-lpg-growth-faces-supply-and-logistics-hurdles) `[SECONDARY]`
- **Import surge:** imports rose **more than 140% from 2020 to 589,000 t in 2024** — [Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2757746-africa-s-lpg-growth-faces-supply-and-logistics-hurdles) `[SECONDARY]`
- **Consumption:** one source reports LPG consumption rose to **500,000 t in 2024 from 425,000 t** the prior year (+17.6%) — [Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2757746-africa-s-lpg-growth-faces-supply-and-logistics-hurdles) `[SECONDARY]`. **Contradicted by** IndexBox, which puts SA 2024 consumption at **167,000 t** and production at 244,000 t — figures that cannot be reconciled with 589,000 t of imports — [IndexBox SADC LPG report](https://www.indexbox.io/store/sadc-liquefied-petroleum-gas-lpg-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/) `[SECONDARY — I would not use]`. IndexBox's 2026 South Africa report carries placeholder "X" values for key figures — [IndexBox SA LPG report](https://www.indexbox.io/store/south-africa-liquefied-petroleum-gas-lpg-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/) `[unusable]`
- **Growth forecasts (treat as commercial estimates, not statistics):** Mordor Intelligence projects the LPG segment to be the fastest-growing part of SA's refined products market at approximately **12.48% over 2026–2031**, against a 1.22% CAGR for the overall petroleum market — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/south-africa-refined-petroleum-products-market) `[SECONDARY]`. A 2024 commentary expects residential and commercial demand to exceed **600,000 t/yr by the late 2030s** — [Tanzania Petroleum, 15 May 2024](https://tanzaniapetroleum.com/2024/05/15/south-africas-lpg-market-growth-potential/) `[SECONDARY]`. Africa-wide, IndexBox forecasts only **+0.5% CAGR in volume and +1.0% in value to 2035**, reaching 16M tonnes and $10.8bn — [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/liquefied-petroleum-gas-lpg-africa-market-overview-2024-2/) `[SECONDARY]`. **These three forecasts are mutually inconsistent; do not average them.**

### Cited Findings — household penetration (Stats SA)

- **Primary cooking fuel, 2024:** about **22.7% of households did not use mains electricity for cooking**. Among all households, **gas was 7.2%**, behind wood at 7.7% — [Stats SA General Household Survey 2024 (P0318)](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0318/P03182024.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`
- **Trend:** gas as a primary cooking source rose **from 2.2% to 6.8%** of households over the GHS series; electricity as primary cooking source rose from 57.5% to 76.9% over the same period — [Stats SA, "The state of South African households in 2023"](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=17283) `[PRIMARY]`
- **Any use of LPG/gas, 2023: 29.0% of households nationally**, against 23.1% relying on open fires — [Stats SA, "The state of South African households in 2023"](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=17283) `[PRIMARY]`
- **Western Cape is the most gas-penetrated province: 46.0%**, ahead of Gauteng at 36.3% — [Stats SA, "The state of South African households in 2023"](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=17283) `[PRIMARY]`. **This is the single most useful market-size statistic for a Western Cape gas business.**

- Stated aims: reduce grid pressure, reduce consumer dependence on coal and paraffin for cooking and heating, expand refinery capacity to reduce imports, regulate pricing across the value chain, and support local LPG cylinder manufacture — [DMRE draft LPG Rollout Strategy](https://www.dmre.gov.za/Portals/0/Energy_Website/files/policies/Gazette44384-for-comments-Draft-LPG-rollout-strategy.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`; [Prospect Intelligence](https://prospect-intel.com/south-africa-lpg-energy-market-strategy/) `[SECONDARY]`

### Inferences

- The honest market framing: **a small, import-dependent, policy-favoured market with high and rising Western Cape penetration.** The 46.0% Western Cape figure plus the 2.2%→6.8% national primary-fuel trajectory is enough to support "gas is mainstream in the Western Cape and growing" without any invented numbers.

## 5. Demand drivers in 2026 — and why load-shedding is no longer one of them

### Takeaway

**Load-shedding is over as a selling point and must not be used.** South Africa passed **504 consecutive days without Eskom load-shedding as at early October 2026**, with the streak starting 16 May 2025 and the energy availability factor at **67.79%, its highest since 2020**. The honest 2026 drivers are: the **22.6% two-year electricity tariff increase with 8.83% more approved for April 2027**, the **shift of electricity cost into unavoidable fixed charges**, water-heating cost specifically, cooking performance, and commercial kitchen economics.

### Cited Findings — Eskom supply status (the thing not to overstate)

- Eskom reached **one full year without a single second of load-shedding on 16 May 2026** — the first such run since September 2018 — [Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/eskom-delivers-365-days-without-loadshedding-advancing-the-economy-competition-and-the-integration-of-renewable-energy/) `[PRIMARY]`
- A later update reported **504 consecutive days without Eskom load-shedding**, streak beginning 16 May 2025 — [FurtherAfrica, 6 October 2026](https://furtherafrica.com/2026/10/06/eskom-load-shedding-ends-for-504-straight-days/) `[SECONDARY, citing Eskom]`. Intermediate milestones: 441 days and 476 days — [SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/eskom-reports-441-days-without-load-shedding) `[PRIMARY, government news service]`; [Businessfront](https://businessfront.com/energy/news/south-africa-load-shedding-eskom/) `[SECONDARY]`
- Eskom delivered a **load-shedding-free winter 2026** and its **energy availability factor rose to 67.79%, the highest since 2020** — [SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/eskom-carries-south-africa-through-load-shedding-free-winter) `[PRIMARY]`; [Africa Updates, 6 September 2026](https://www.africaupdates.com/news/south-africa-powers-through-winter-2026-without-load-shedding-n1579/2026/09/06) `[SECONDARY]`
- **The residual, and the only defensible resilience claim:** targeted **load reduction** (distinct from national load-shedding) still affects **202,590 customers, 2.8% of Eskom's base**, primarily in Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal, with full elimination targeted by **March 2027**. Eskom's own August statement described a programme covering **971 feeders**, on track to eliminate load reduction in a seventh province by October 2026 and nationwide by 2027 — [Businessfront](https://businessfront.com/energy/news/south-africa-load-shedding-eskom/) `[SECONDARY]`. **The two sources use different scope and timing; the exact figures are unresolved. This barely touches the Western Cape and should not be used as a Cape Town driver.**
- **Flag:** one commercial outage site reports Eskom issued a formal legal notice on 15 May 2026 and could cut bulk supply to parts of Johannesburg from 8 July 2026 over City Power debt. **I could not confirm this on any official source — do not use it.**

### Cited Findings — the drivers that are real

1. **Tariff escalation, verified and forward-looking.** 12.74% (1 Apr 2025) → 8.76% (1 Apr 2026) → **8.83% already approved for 1 Apr 2027**; municipalities 9.01% from 1 Jul 2026 — [Eskom Tariffs and Charges Booklet 2026/2027](https://www.eskom.co.za/distribution/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Eskom-Tariff-booklet_Final.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`; [NERSA media statement, March 2026](https://www.nersa.org.za/files/files/2026/03/MediaStatement-NERSAapprovesEskomRetailTariffsandStructuralAdjustmentapplicationfor2026-27.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`; [Engineering News, 10 March 2026](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/nersa-approves-higher-electricity-hikes-for-202627-after-r547bn-error-2026-03-10) `[SECONDARY]`. And the **R54.7bn error recovery is not finished** — R12bn in 2026/27, R23bn in later years. That is a documented, named reason to expect further above-inflation increases.
2. **Both 2026/27 and 2027/28 increases are above inflation** (~3.5%) — [Newcastillian, 9 February 2026](https://newcastillian.com/2026/02/09/eskom-tariff-increase-2026-2027-nersa/) `[SECONDARY]`

### Inferences

- **Required copy discipline:** do not write "load-shedding" in present tense. ## 7. Payback, and the risks to the economics

### Takeaway

At October 2026 Cape Town prices, **electric geyser → instant gas water heating pays back in roughly 14 to 44 months (central ~24 months)** for a City of Cape Town Domestic household using ~200 L/day — but **does not pay back at all** for an Eskom Homelight 20A or CoCT Lifeline household, and is beaten on lifetime cost by a heat pump. **Electric hob → gas hob does not pay back on energy cost at all.** The principal risks are LPG price volatility (a verified 15.5% single-month increase in May 2026), near-total import dependence in the Western Cape through a single terminal, rand and freight exposure, and a documented history of Western Cape wholesalers paying premiums above the regulated MRGP.

### Payback — electric geyser → instant gas water heater `[MY ARITHMETIC]`

Assumptions: 200 L/day, 15 °C → 55 °C (279 useful kWh/month); electric geyser 90% system efficiency; gas instant heater 78–88%; LPG at R37.08/kg; 2026/27 City of Cape Town Domestic tariff; install cost R8,000 – R17,000 from §6.

| | Monthly energy cost |
|---|---|
| Electric, CoCT Domestic Block 1 (400.63 c/kWh) | R1,242 |
| Electric, CoCT Domestic Block 2 (480.73 c/kWh) | R1,490 |
| LPG instant at R37.08/kg | R851 – R963 |

- **Monthly saving: R279 (Block 1, worst gas efficiency) to R639 (Block 2, best gas efficiency).** Central ~R450.
- **Payback: R8,000 ÷ R639 = 12.5 months (best case) to R17,000 ÷ R279 = 61 months (worst case).** Central: **R11,000 ÷ R450 ≈ 24 months.**
- **Range to publish: roughly 1 to 5 years, with ~2 years typical** — and only for a household on the City of Cape Town Domestic tariff with meaningful hot-water use.
- **The payback improves every April and July** as tariffs rise and LPG does not. At 8.83% on electricity in April 2027 with LPG flat, the Block 2 saving rises from R639 to ~R770/month `[MY ARITHMETIC]`.
- **Where it fails:** at Eskom Homelight 20A (270.30 c/kWh → R837/month electric) the saving against gas at R851–R963 is **negative**. There is no payback. Same for CoCT Lifeline (R836/month).
- **Against a heat pump:** heat pump at CoCT Block 2 costs R446/month vs electric R1,490 — a R1,044/month saving at R27,500–R37,500 installed → payback **26 to 36 months** `[MY ARITHMETIC]`, and a **lower running cost than gas thereafter (R446 vs R851–963)**. So a heat pump has a comparable payback period on 3–4× the capital, and wins on lifetime cost. **Gas's genuine advantage over a heat pump is capital cost, instantaneous unlimited hot water, and operation without electricity — not running cost.**

### Payback — electric hob → gas hob `[MY ARITHMETIC]`

- From §4, gas cooking costs **R6.68 – R7.25 per useful kWh** against radiant electric at **R5.72 – R6.87** and induction at **R4.77 – R5.72** at CoCT 2026/27 tariffs.
- **The energy saving is zero or negative. There is no payback period.** A gas hob conversion at R2,000 – R7,500 plus appliance plus R950 CoC never repays itself from energy cost at 2026 prices.
- **This is contingent on the 40% gas pan-efficiency assumption** (see §4 Gaps). If actual gas hob efficiency is 50%, gas cooking costs R5.34–R5.80/useful kWh and roughly ties radiant electric — still not a saving.
- **Honest copy position:** sell the gas hob on instant heat control, wok/flame cooking, working through a power outage, and professional-kitchen preference. **Do not sell it on running cost.**

### Risks — LPG price volatility

- **Verified single-month move: coastal LPG rose 508 c/kg (+15.5%) between 1 April 2026 and 6 May 2026**, then fell 443 c/kg (−11.7%) between 1 July and 5 August 2026 — [CEF/DMPR Media Statement, 2 October 2026, Annexure 1](https://cefgroup.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Press-Release-02-October-2026-Change-07-October-2026.pdf) `[PRIMARY]` + `[MY ARITHMETIC]`
- **Peak-to-trough spread of 22.9% within 7 months** (3,082 c/kg Nov 2025 → 3,787 c/kg May 2026) `[MY ARITHMETIC]` on `[PRIMARY]` data
- **The driver is international, not local.** October 2026's increase was attributed to average Brent rising from **87.89 to 101 USD** on US/Iran tensions, uncertainty over oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz, higher shipping costs and falling inventories; **propane and butane prices rose specifically due to "supply uncertainties"** — [Minister's media statement, 5 October 2026](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/minister-gwede-mantashe-announces-adjustment-fuel-prices-effective-7-0) `[PRIMARY]`
- **Monthly resets mean the household carries the risk.** Unlike electricity, which steps once a year on an announced percentage, LPG changes every month in both directions.

### Risks — import dependence

- **Domestic production nearly halved** (235,000 t in 2020 → 123,000 t in 2024) while **imports rose >140% to 589,000 t** — [Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2757746-africa-s-lpg-growth-faces-supply-and-logistics-hurdles) `[SECONDARY]`. On those numbers **imports are roughly 83% of supply** `[MY ARITHMETIC]` — but see the gap note, since consumption and import figures across sources do not reconcile.
- **The Western Cape depends on a single terminal.** Sunrise Energy operates a **210,000 t/yr** LPG import facility at Saldanha Bay, described as **the Western Cape's only LPG import terminal**, with the province **almost entirely reliant on imports** because local refineries cannot meet demand — [Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2636043-s-africa-eml-gets-2-yr-contract-at-sunrise-lpg-terminal) `[SECONDARY]`
- Nationally, South Africa has propane import terminals at **Saldanha and Richards Bay**; Richards Bay is operated by Petredec-Bidvest. A March 2026 analysis argues this existing LPG capacity could support a national gas strategy without a large new LNG terminal — [The Conversation, 2026](https://theconversation.com/south-africa-will-run-out-of-industrial-gas-by-2028-and-70-000-jobs-could-be-lost-why-government-fixes-are-lacking-277839) `[SECONDARY]`
- **A documented pricing dispute specific to the Western Cape:** Western Cape wholesalers reported paying **significant premiums since EML took over as importer**, pushing regional prices **above the government-regulated MRGP** and prompting the department to review its pricing formula. EML argued the formula excludes demurrage and freight costs specific to the Saldanha Bay terminal; the department **acknowledged that Saldanha is more expensive than Richards Bay** — [Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2636043-s-africa-eml-gets-2-yr-contract-at-sunrise-lpg-terminal) `[SECONDARY, c. early 2025]`. **This is the structural explanation for the R37.08 vs R34.56 Western Cape premium and should be disclosed in Cape Town copy.**
- **SA has no storage facility able to fully discharge a Very Large Gas Carrier (VLGC)** — a structural cost penalty on imports — [DMRE draft LPG Rollout Strategy](https://www.dmre.gov.za/Portals/0/Energy_Website/files/policies/Gazette44384-for-comments-Draft-LPG-rollout-strategy.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`
- **Supply constraints are being officially acknowledged:** supply tightness from refinery outages and a fragmented transport network has constrained the market; PetroSA's interim CEO called restarting refineries a priority — [Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2757746-africa-s-lpg-growth-faces-supply-and-logistics-hurdles) `[SECONDARY]`
- **Mitigation under way:** Richards Bay has added large LPG storage (four 5,650-tonne bullets as part of a national LPG supply plan) — [Freight News](https://www.freightnews.co.za/article/sas-lpg-supply-plans-take-a-giant-leap-forward) `[SECONDARY, ~7 years old]`; an LPG rail freight project is advancing with operation from 2028, and the Tianlong LPG cylinder manufacturing plant was inaugurated in October 2025 — [African Energy Week](https://aecweek.com/news/lpg-reshaping-regional-clean-cooking-dynamics-sadc-region) `[SECONDARY]`

### Risks — rand exposure

- The LPG MRGP is derived from imported product prices and therefore carries direct rand exposure. For October 2026 the average rand/USD rate used was **16.2123** for 28 August – 1 October 2026, against 16.2139 in the prior period — a marginal 0.01% appreciation that offset the international increase by only 0.17–0.22 c/l on liquid fuels — [CEF/DMPR Media Statement, 2 October 2026, §3](https://cefgroup.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Press-Release-02-October-2026-Change-07-October-2026.pdf) `[PRIMARY]`
- **Honest framing:** in October 2026 the rand was essentially neutral; the entire increase was international product price. But the mechanism means a rand depreciation passes through to the LPG MRP within one month.

### Inferences

- The **safest payback claim** for the website is specific and conditional: *"For a City of Cape Town household on the Domestic tariff using about 200 litres of hot water a day, switching from an electric element geyser to an instant gas water heater saves roughly R280–R640 a month at October 2026 prices, paying back a R8,000–R17,000 installation in about one to five years. It does not pay back for low-usage households, for Eskom Homelight or Lifeline customers, or where a heat pump is affordable."*
- The risk section should be published, not hidden. A business that discloses the May 2026 15.5% spike and the Saldanha single-terminal dependence is far more credible than one that claims gas is simply cheaper.

### Gaps

- **I could not verify the current national LPG import share.** The 83% figure is my arithmetic on two numbers from the same source, and the consumption data across sources do not reconcile (500,000 t vs 167,000 t for 2024). **Do not publish an import-share percentage.**
- **No 2025 or 2026 import/production volumes.** All supply data stops at 2024.
- **No confirmation of whether the Western Cape MRGP premium dispute has been resolved** or whether the department changed the pricing formula. The Argus report dates to approximately early 2025; the October 2026 press release still shows a separate, higher Saldanha MRGP, which suggests the premium was formalised rather than removed — **but that is an inference, not a verified finding.**
- **No verified Western Cape or Saldanha supply interruption record** for 2025–2026. I found commentary about national supply tightness but no specific Western Cape outage events.
- **I could not verify Sunrise Energy's current capacity or its 2026 operator.** The 210,000 t/yr and EML operator details come from a c. 2025 report.
- **The LNG picture is unresolved and should be left out of copy.** TNPA signed a 25-year terminal operator agreement with Zululand Energy Terminals in February 2025 for SA's first LNG import terminal at Richards Bay, commercial operation 2028 — [Engineering News, 10 February 2025](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/tnpa-signs-25-year-operator-agreement-for-south-africas-first-lng-import-terminal-2025-02-10) `[SECONDARY]` — and ExxonMobil signed an LNG supply deal for it in June 2026 — [Gasworld](https://www.gasworld.com/story/south-africas-first-lng-import-terminal-to-supply-exxonmobil-as-project-seeks-interest/2252607.article/) `[SECONDARY]`. **But Global Energy Monitor lists both the Richards Bay and Saldanha Bay FSRU LNG projects as cancelled** — [GEM Richards Bay FSRU](https://www.gem.wiki/Richards_Bay_Transnet_FSRU), [GEM Saldanha Bay FSRU](https://www.gem.wiki/Saldanha_Bay_FSRU) `[SECONDARY]`. These may be different projects; the sources do not settle it. **LNG is piped natural gas infrastructure and in any case does not supply household LPG — it is not relevant to the residential gas case and should not be cited as one.**
- **No verified data on LPG cylinder availability or deposit-system friction** as a barrier to adoption.
