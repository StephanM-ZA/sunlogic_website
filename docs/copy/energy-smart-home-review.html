As submitted. The energy argument is real and the section now belongs on the page, but the heading was inert, the honesty arrived too late to disarm anybody, and two of the three cards were quietly repeating work done elsewhere. All five findings are applied, which takes it to about 8.5.

Question one: does it belong on an energy page now

Yes, and the mechanism is the reason why. The old version listed things you can buy. The new one describes a method: measure it, then decide whether to schedule it, replace it or leave it. That is an energy argument, it is the same argument the rest of the page makes about geysers and switching, and it is the first version of this section that a reader could act on without spending anything.

Where it was still slipping back into the footnote was the closing line. "Lighting, cameras and access control we will happily do as well. Just don't expect to find them on the bill." Two problems. "Happily" is limp, and "them" is ambiguous: on first read it attaches to the whole brand list above it, which would say Home Assistant and Plentify won't touch your bill. That is the opposite of what three sections just argued.

AppliedWe install and support Home Assistant, Plentify, Sonoff, Tuya and Shelly, and most smart appliances that will talk to one of them. Lighting, cameras and access control we'll do as well, and they're worth having for how they make the house work. They're just not what changes the bill. Spend there last.

"Spend there last" restates the page's spine in three words, at the exact moment the reader is deciding what to do about any of it.

Question two: card two repeats pillar two

It was repetition, and the fix is a distinction rather than a cut. As written, "Anything that doesn't care when it runs should run while the sun is up" is the same sentence pillar two already delivers with more force and more specificity. Worse, the example list said "Dishwasher, washing machine, pool, dryer" while card one had just said "pool pump" and "tumble dryer" one column to its left. Three of the four examples on the page appear twice.

There is a genuine boundary here and the copy was not drawing it. Pillar two is hard wiring at the distribution board: geyser, pool pump, car charger, things on a circuit. This is plug level: things you can pick up and move. Say that and the card reads as a callback. Leave it unsaid and it reads as the writer forgetting what they already wrote.

WasThe same logic as the geyser, on a smaller scale. Anything that doesn't care when it runs should run while the sun is up. Dishwasher, washing machine, pool, dryer.
AppliedSwitching at the board handles what's wired in. This is everything else, the things on a plug that don't care what time they happen. Dishwasher, washing machine, the pool cleaner, anything nobody minds running at eleven in the morning.

Pool cleaner rather than pool: the pump is wired and already claimed by pillar two, the cleaner is on a plug. The examples across the two cards no longer collide.

Against the H1 it sits correctly. "Your Geyser Doesn't Know You're Out. Neither Does Your Pool Pump." is about machines running blind. This is about the owner running blind. Same idea, moved from the equipment to the person, at the end of the page rather than the start. That is a progression, not a repetition.

Question four: did the honest limit survive

No, and that was the most expensive thing lost in the rewrite. The old heading was "Worth Doing. Not Where the Saving Is." It disarmed the reader before the section started. The new version moved the same admission to the final clause of the final line, after all the selling, where it functions as a disclaimer instead.

Position is the whole point of a concession. Said first, it buys you the right to be believed for the rest of the section. Said last, it reads as small print. This page has spent three sections building an argument that Sunlogic tells you what not to buy, and the one section where that would cost them something is the one where they said it quietly and late.

WasHot water, switching and monitoring cover the big three. What's left is a long tail of smaller loads, and none of them will move your bill the way a geyser does. What they will do is stop you guessing. A plug that reports what an appliance actually draws turns an argument into a number.
AppliedHot water, switching and monitoring are where the money is. Everything after them is smaller, and it's better you hear that from us now than work it out from the bill later. What the rest is genuinely good for is ending the guesswork. A metering plug tells you what an appliance actually draws, and once you have that number you can decide whether it's worth scheduling, replacing or leaving alone.

The concession is now the second sentence. It also removes a duplicate: "turns an argument into a number" and card one's "not the appliance everyone in the house has been blaming" are the same joke told twice, and the card tells it better.

Question five: is "five apps" a customer objection or an installer's

It is a real customer objection, but it was written from the installer's side of the desk, and it never connected the hub to money.

The homeowner's version of this complaint is not that five apps is untidy. It is that the automation stops happening, because everybody else in the house gives up and does it manually. The original gestured at that with "everyone except the person who installed it" and then dropped it.

The bigger miss is that card three was the only card on the page not making an energy argument. Home Assistant's actual case here is that it can see what the solar is producing and what the appliances are drawing in the same place, which is the only way a decision runs on conditions instead of a clock. That is the page's central mechanism, stated in pillar two, and card three had it sitting right there unstated.

AppliedSonoff, Tuya, Shelly and Plentify each arrive with their own app, and a house run from five apps is a house where nobody but the person who set it up ever changes anything.

Home Assistant puts them in one place, on a small computer in your house, with what your solar is making shown next to what your appliances are drawing. That is what lets a decision run on conditions instead of a clock. It also keeps working when the internet doesn't.

On the zero numbers

You flagged it and asked whether it is a weakness. It is the correct call and I did not invent one to fix it. Every number available here is household specific: what a second fridge costs to run depends on its age, its seals, where it stands and what the tariff is. A "typical" figure would be a guess wearing a decimal point, and this page has already made a virtue of not doing that.

The section also has the rare luxury of turning the absence into the argument. It is a section about measuring, so "we can't tell you and that is exactly why you measure" is a stronger position than a borrowed average. The applied intro carries that implicitly. Making it explicit is optional and I would leave it as is.

House rules and voice

  • No em dashes, no emoji, no hype words, no manufactured urgency. Clean.
  • No gendered pronouns. "The person who set it up" is neutral and stays neutral.
  • Nothing claimed as free. The section sells nothing, which is correct for its position.
  • South African English throughout: tumble dryer, borehole pump, pool cleaner, garage.
  • Title case slip, fixed. "Then Move It To Daytime" capitalised a two letter preposition. Every other heading on the page lowercases minor words: "Smart Switches on the Things", "This Part Is Just Arithmetic". Now "Then Move It to Daytime".
  • Contraction slip, fixed. "we will happily do as well" in body copy, where the house rule is contractions. Now "we'll do as well".
  • Tense wobble, fixed. "the appliance everyone in the house had been blaming" used past perfect for no reason. Now "has been blaming".

The verdict

Top three, by impact.

  • Move the honest limit to the front of the intro. It is the single change that keeps this section consistent with the trust the page has spent three pillars building.
  • Replace the heading. An inert opener in a set of four sharp ones tells the reader this section matters less, which is true, but it tells them by being boring rather than by being honest.
  • Draw the wired versus plugged boundary in card two. It converts the page's only piece of genuine repetition into a deliberate callback.

What was already working.

  • "Measure Before You Change Anything" is the best card heading in the section and possibly the most useful sentence on the page for somebody with no budget.
  • The example list in card one is specific enough to be recognised: the second fridge in the garage is in half the houses this page is written for.
  • "It keeps working when the internet doesn't" is a verifiable, unglamorous, entirely true benefit, which is this site's whole register.
  • Naming the brands rather than saying "leading smart home platforms" is what makes this a contractor's page and not a brochure.

Kill list.

  • "turns an argument into a number" in the intro. Card one makes the point better.
  • "pool" and "dryer" from card two's list. Card one already used both.
  • "Everything Else You Own Is Also Using Electricity". Replaced.
  • "we will happily". Nobody is happy in this register, and it does not need to be.

Structural note, not applied

Three cards gives this section the same visual weight as switching and monitoring, which contradicts the eyebrow that demotes it. Two cards would signal the demotion in the layout rather than only in the words, by merging measure and schedule into one card and leaving the hub as the second. It is a build change rather than a copy change, the copy works either way, and I have left it at three. Worth deciding on when the page is next looked at whole.
