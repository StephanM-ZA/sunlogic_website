Copy chief review · the new energy copy

The argument is here. It is in third place.

Reviewing Sunlogic Energy Site Copy, the homepage section, as written on 4 September. This is a large step up from what is live. Most of what follows is about order and proof, not about the writing.

What this is

PieceHomepage copy, roughly 980 words, for a purchase worth six figures.
ReaderAlready shopping. They have two or three quotes and no way to tell them apart.
Your argumentWe do the whole job, not just the sale. It is stated clearly, it is true, and no competitor can copy it by writing a better sentence.
Where it sitsSection three of nine.

The order is the main problem

The document knows what its best section is. It says so in the brief: "This is the differentiator, so give it room on the page." Then it puts it third, after a section about the market and a section of audience labels.

Your reader is holding two other quotes. The question in their head is not what solar is or who you serve. It is why your quote is different. They should not have to scroll twice.

"Why now" already ends on the right line: we can take the biggest line on your bill and put it back under your control. That hands straight into how. Putting audience labels between them breaks a join the copy has already made.

Line level

Section · Who we work for

Issue

Three headings with one line each, sitting next to a five block section that carries the whole argument. It is a signpost dressed as a section. Its real job is sending people to the three parts of the solar page, and that is a row of cards, not a chapter.

Fix

Keep the three lines, drop the section status, move it below the argument. The wording is fine. "You trade during the day, which is exactly when solar produces" is a good sentence.

Why it matters: A section heading is a promise that something important follows. Spending one on a menu teaches the reader to skim the next heading too.

Section · Plentify

"Their technology has been checked by an independent testing body at the University of Cape Town, trialled with the City of Cape Town, and named project of the year by an international energy fund in 2024."
Issue

Three proof points, all of them about Plentify. The reader finishes the paragraph convinced that Plentify is a serious company, which was not in doubt and is not what you are selling. The only Sunlogic fact in the section, that you were one of their first installer partners, is the last line.

Fix

Turn it around. Lead with what the customer gets, then who makes it, then one credential. Something close to: Your geyser is the biggest single user of electricity in most homes, and it heats water on a timer whether you are there or not. We fit controllers that decide when it runs. They are made by Plentify, a Cape Town company we have partnered with since 2024, and their technology has been independently tested at the University of Cape Town.

Why it matters: Borrowed credibility only works after the reader knows what is in it for them. Otherwise you have written an advert for your supplier.

Section · Our work

"Three recent jobs, each with a photo and one real number. A home, a business, and something larger."
Issue

The section the document itself calls the highest value on the site is a brief, not copy. As it stands it ships as three empty cards.

Fix

This one is not a writing job. Three to six jobs with a photograph, the suburb, what went in and one honest number. Until those exist, the current live page already does a smaller version of this correctly, so keep that rather than shipping placeholders.

Why it matters: It is the only section a competitor cannot write their way past, which is exactly why it cannot go out empty.

Trust strip

Registered electrical contractor · Plentify partner since 2024 · Certificate on every job · Council registration handled · [X] systems installed
Issue

An unfilled slot in the first proof the reader meets. Two of the five entries are not yet provable: the registration number and the install count. The count is live on the site today as "300+" with nothing behind it.

Fix

Ship four true items rather than five with a gap. Add the fifth when you can show it.

Why it matters: A trust strip is read as a set. One item nobody can check invites doubt about the other four.

Block · WE WATCH IT

"Solar fails quietly. The lights stay on, the inverter hums, the app still shows power coming in, and you find out on the bill six weeks later."
Issue

Nothing is wrong with it. It is the best writing in the document. The problem is that the page it will sit on already shows live output from the systems you monitor, updating through the day, and this block does not mention it.

Fix

One sentence: the numbers at the top of this page are real, from the systems we are watching right now. You are describing a service while the evidence of it runs in the corner of the same screen.

Why it matters: Showing beats telling, and you already have the showing built and running.

Block · WE CLEAN IT

"Done badly it scratches the glass and voids warranties, which is why it matters who's on your roof."
Issue

A claim about the consequences of other people's work. The copy document's own checklist says to have an attorney confirm the cleaning line before publishing it.

Fix

Keep the sentence, get it checked. Softening it would cost the point. This is a note to act on, not a rewrite.

Final call to action

"A site visit costs you nothing and ends with a straight answer: what it'd take, what it'd cost, and what it'd save."
Issue

It repeats WE QUOTE IT, which already promised a design, a price and a saving figure. Same three things, twice on one page, and the second time carries less because the reader has met it.

Fix

Close on the part the reader has not been given yet, which is what happens next and how long it takes. An hour at your property, twelve months of bills, and a number you can hold us to. We come to you.

Why it matters: The close should add the last missing thing, not summarise. A summary tells the reader they have already got the point and can leave.

The verdict

7/10

A serious piece of work. It has a real argument, it is specific, and the voice is right. It loses marks for burying its best section, for a proof section that is still a brief, and for crediting Plentify where it should be selling the customer a warmer house and a smaller bill.

Three changes

  1. Move the whole job to second. It is the answer to the only question your reader actually has. "Why now" already ends on the line that sets it up.
  2. Rewrite the Plentify section from the customer's side. What they get, then who makes it, then one credential instead of three.
  3. Point at the live numbers in WE WATCH IT. One sentence, and a claim becomes evidence.

Do not let anything change these

"Solar fails quietly." Four words that make a reader think about their own roof. Then the detail earns it: the lights stay on, the inverter hums, the app still shows power coming in. That is the paragraph the whole site should be built around.

"If solar isn't worth it on your property yet, we'll say so." The most persuasive sentence in the document, and the one every sales copy rulebook would cut.

"One company, one certificate, one number to phone." Rhythm doing real work. It names a genuine fear, being passed between trades, without naming it.

"We'll take on a system we didn't install." Reaches people nobody else is speaking to, and they are the easiest customers you will ever get, because they have already learned what the alternative costs.

The fixed charge paragraph. "Your bill can rise in a month you actually used less" is checkable, surprising, and yours. Keep it exactly as written.

Cut or hold

  • Who we work for as a section. Keep the three lines, lose the chapter status.
  • Two of the three Plentify credentials. One is proof. Three is a press release.
  • The final call to action body. Rewrite so it does not repeat WE QUOTE IT.
  • [X] systems installed. Hold the whole item until the number exists.
  • Our work. Hold the section until there are real jobs to put in it. Keep what is live.
