The section now sells what the platform is for rather than what it protects against. One open item, and it is a fact I do not have.

What was wrong with the first version

HighApplied

Every card was about something breaking

all three tiers

First versionSomebody is watching, and if the inverter drops, the battery starts running flat, or something in the house begins drawing through the night, you hear about it from us.

Accurate, and it frames a R149 a month subscription as insurance. Nobody enjoys buying insurance. It is also the smaller half of what VoltIQ does: upsell.py carries eleven separate signals about a system falling short of what it could be doing, and not one of them reached the copy except a single line in the installer card.

Applied, as the section headingMost Systems Are Not Broken. They Are Just Losing.

A system can run for years without a single fault and still cost money every month. That is the argument, and it was sitting unused in the source the whole time.

HighApplied

The cards re-explained the product

all three tiers

By the time a reader reaches this section the page has explained VoltIQ three times: a section on what it is and what you receive, two screenshots of real reports, a case study, and three cards on what it detects. The tiers were doing a fourth, shorter version of the same thing.

AppliedEach card now leads with what that owner loses, not with what VoltIQ is. A battery emptying at nine and the evening bought back at full price. A payback figure somebody signed off, moving every month without anything on the books saying so. A hundred systems nobody can read before seven.
MediumResolved by a price change

Two cards, one price, no stated difference

Home and Business

Both were R149 and the Business card opened "The same watch, on a system that has to pay for itself". A reader looking for the difference between two identically priced cards was told in four words that there is not one.

I had written a line arguing the shared price was deliberate. The owner then corrected the price: Business is R199 because it is checked more often. That is a real product difference, so the argument for sameness was retired rather than kept. It was solving a problem that had stopped existing.

MediumApplied

VAT was stated on one set of prices and not the other

across the page

The Plentify cards said R849 once off then R149 a month, with no VAT treatment. The VoltIQ cards said excluding VAT. A reader weighing R149 against R149 had no way to know one included VAT and the other did not, and the two numbers happened to be identical.

AppliedPlentify prices now say including VAT. VoltIQ says excluding. Both comparable for the first time.
LowApplied

The heading did not say the section was about price, and "watched" read as ominous

section heading

"Three Ways to Be Watched" under an eyebrow reading "What it costs". The eyebrow was doing the work the heading should have done, and "watched" without a subject has an unfortunate second meaning.

MediumApplied

The buyer changes at the third card and the page did not say so

section intro

Two thousand words of this page talk to a homeowner. The third card talks to a solar company. The owner has confirmed it belongs here, so the fix is to signal the switch rather than slide into it: a homeowner should stop reading at card two, and an installer should know card three is theirs.

AppliedThe first two below are for one system. The third is for a company looking after a lot of them.

Still open

"Updated more often" is doing the work of a specification. The Business tier costs fifty rand more because it is checked more frequently, and the copy can only say "more often" because that is all I have been told. A reader deciding between R149 and R199 cannot decide from that.

What would make it concrete: how often each tier is actually reported on. The platform polls hourly through daylight and assembles a report at 07:00 daily, so if the difference is, say, daily against weekly, saying it plainly turns fifty rand from a mystery into an obvious choice.

The one remaining gap on the whole page is unchanged: smart switching still has no price at all.

What is working

  • "That second list is not a support queue. It is your next month's quotes." The strongest line in the section. It reframes a monitoring subscription as a sales tool for the only buyer on the page who thinks in those terms.
  • The Business card names the mechanism, not the feeling. A payback figure somebody signed off, moving quietly, with nothing on the books recording it. That is a specific fear and it is true.
  • Neither of those is a fault, so nothing alerts you and nothing looks wrong. This is the whole product in one sentence and it is why the section works now.
