Before anything else: I was wrong on the Energy site today

Reviewing this site surfaced an error I shipped on Energy a few messages ago, and I have corrected it. I published Plentify's public marketing line as the cancellation term and wrote that "the subscription is Plentify's rather than ours."

It is not. legal.html, sitting in this repo the whole time, carries Sunlogic's own subscription agreement:

"This agreement starts on the Subscription Date and continues on a month to month basis until you cancel … During the first 24 months after the Free Trial ends ("Initial Term"): you pay a fee of R750 per Device … You may cancel this agreement at any time on 20 business days' notice sent to support@sunlogic.co.za … We retain ownership of the Device(s)"

Sunlogic owns the device, takes the notice and charges the fee. Plentify's page describes their own direct-to-consumer contract, which is a different agreement with different terms. I trusted a supplier's marketing page over a contract that was in the repo.

Corrected on the Energy Smart page and gated clean. It is uncommitted, pending your review of the wording.

The question that matters: Craig's item 1

Craig identified the real problem and this copy has not solved it. Solar sells itself on a grievance everybody already has. Electrical has no equivalent, and the site currently pretends otherwise by keeping the solar hook on an electrical page.

The honest answer is not a saving. It is a deadline somebody else sets for you. Nobody phones an electrician to save money. They phone when an external event forces them to:

  • You are selling. A certificate no older than two years, or the transfer does not go through.
  • You are claiming. Surge and fire claims turn on whether protection was properly installed and certified.
  • Your lease is up. The shopfit you inherited is now yours to certify.
  • You are adding load. A car charger, aircon, a hob or solar, and the board cannot carry it.
  • Something already broke. The tripping circuit, the dead board.

That is the electrical equivalent of "your bill keeps rising", and it is stronger, because a deadline beats a preference. The hub page half-discovers it in the compliance section and then buries it two thirds of the way down a page nobody reaches. It should be the site's spine, the way the order of spending is the spine of the Energy division.

The Energy site says what not to buy. The Electrical equivalent is telling people when they do not need you: a valid certificate stays valid, plenty of sites do not need power factor correction. The copy already does this in places and gets no credit for it because it is scattered.
