Your question about the home page

You asked whether the home page should stay lighter, with the energy page fleshing it out. You are right, and it changes something I did in v2.

A home page owes a business reader three things. They have to recognise themselves early. They need one thing that is true for them and not for a homeowner. And they need a door. That is recognition, not depth, and everything past it belongs on solar.html where a reader who has already put their hand up can take it in.

Measured against that, v2 overbuilt. I had added four commercial pieces to the home page. The demand charge callout is the one that does not belong there. It is detail, it is the fourth thing a business reader meets before they have chosen to be interested, and it is the one claim on the page I cannot verify. Putting it on your highest traffic page was the wrong trade twice over. It has moved to the business section of solar.html.

What stays is the two sums block, which is recognition and earns its place, the payback clause inside We Quote It, which is one clause, and the rewritten card.

Who we work for · Businesses card

"You use it as you make it. Most of what the panels produce goes straight into the building, so less of the capital goes into storage."
Working

This is the first sentence on the page that gives a business reader an advantage a homeowner cannot have. Not a different sum, a better one, and cheaper to build. That is what equal weight is supposed to buy them.

But

It is unverified, and it is a tendency rather than a law. A restaurant, a bar, a bakery running ovens at five in the morning breaks it. Check it against a commercial job you have already done, and if the pattern does not hold for the businesses you actually serve, the sentence has to go, however good it reads.

The verdict

Three rounds ago this was a document with a good argument in the wrong order. It is now a page whose argument, order, language and calls to action all hold up, for two different readers, without either of them feeling like an afterthought.

Every remaining finding is a photograph, a figure, a lawyer or a component. That is a different kind of work and it is not mine. I would stop writing here.

What to do next, in order

  1. Pull two numbers off your own bills. The fixed charge then and now. One line, and it turns the strongest paragraph on the page from an assertion into something a reader can go and check.
  2. Photograph three jobs, one of them a business. It closes the unkept promise and the missing commercial proof together, and it is the only section a competitor cannot write their way past.
  3. Decide the phone number. One, or label the second. Cheapest fix on the list and it is currently contradicting your best line.

Do not let anything change these

"You use it as you make it." Five words, and a business owner understands their whole case. Best line added in three rounds, if it survives the check.

"We can change how much of it you pay." Ends the section on the reader.

"Solar fails quietly." Unchanged since round one and still the paragraph the site should be built around.

"The roof sets the ceiling. What you use sets the target." Works identically for a cottage and a cold store.

"If solar is not worth it on your property yet, we will say so." Still the most persuasive sentence you have, and still the one every rulebook would cut.

Do not put these on the home page

  • The demand charge callout. Moved to solar.html. Detail, and unverified.
  • Payback worked examples. Same reason. The clause in We Quote It is enough here.
  • Tax treatment. Belongs on solar.html, and only once an accountant confirms what currently applies. Do not let me write a percentage.
