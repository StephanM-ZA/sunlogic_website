Both Solar Articles Describe the Wrong Product

Four posts reviewed worst first. The unit errors and the dead statistic are real and all confirmed, but they are not the serious problem. The serious problem is that the two 2022 solar articles share a mental model in which off-grid means having a battery and grid-tied means not having one, which is not what Sunlogic sells.

Average across the set. Individually: bill 7, off grid 4, five tips 3, rent to own 2. Three of the four contain a statement that is either false, unverifiable, or contradicted by another page on this same site.

The finding that outranks the typos

Both 2022 solar articles think a battery is what makes a system off-grid. Off the Grid vs. Grid-tied says an off-grid system needs "a battery, which comes in different sizes", then describes grid-tied as panels and an inverter, "or an inverter and a battery so that they can keep the lights on during load shedding". Five Tips says to choose between "a grid-tied solution that will supplement your energy requirement when the sun is available or an off-grid system that can store excess solar power produced during the day".

That is the same error twice, and it misdescribes the category Sunlogic actually installs. A hybrid grid-tied system with batteries stores excess solar and rides through an outage while staying connected. Off-grid means no connection to the utility at all, which is a different decision with different economics and different failure modes. Two articles telling a reader that the way to store solar is to disconnect from the grid will send people to the wrong quote, and the Noordhoek and Rondebosch Prep installations on the solar page are both the thing these articles say does not exist.

Every unit error below is embarrassing. This one loses work.

Pressure test: should archive pieces be rewritten at all

Your callout on Five Tips argues no, and it is a good argument. A dated article is a record, and rewriting the words while keeping the date presents new writing as if it were written then. That is quietly dishonest and I would not do it.

But the choice is not rewrite or don't. It is what the date means. Three routes, and only one of them survives contact with the errors above.

RouteDateWordsHolds up?
Rewrite in place2022 keptNewNo. Backdates new writing
Archive, fix facts only2022 keptOriginalLeaves press release prose and a live contradiction
Republish as newTodayNewYes

Republish under today's date with a one line provenance note. "An earlier version of this appeared in 2022." The reader is told exactly what they are reading, the original is honoured, and the rewrite is free to be a real rewrite.

There is also a fact that undercuts the archive purity argument. Rent-to-Own has already been partly rewritten. "Homeowners and businesses are looking at solar to take the biggest controllable line on the bill back under their own control" is not 2022 press release prose, it is current site voice. The archive was breached before this conversation started, and it was breached in the middle of a paragraph, which is the worst possible place because nothing tells the reader which sentences are which.

The Craig Lewis constraint, and why it dissolves

You are right not to write new words for a real person, and the constraint is smaller than it looks. The quotes exist because these were press releases, and a press release needs an attributed spokesperson to have a voice at all. Republished as Sunlogic's own current writing, the pieces do not need a spokesperson. The company is the voice.

So the rule set is short. A quote that still fits stays verbatim, inside quotation marks, attributed. A quote whose surrounding argument has changed comes out entirely, and its substance is restated unquoted in the company's own voice. Nothing is paraphrased inside quotation marks. No quote is lengthened, shortened mid sentence, or moved to a claim it was not making.

Applied to the actual quotes: Lewis on "you don't need to go completely off-grid to benefit from a solar system" is still true and still useful, so it can stay. Lewis on "no sign of load shedding easing any time soon" was accurate in July 2022 and is not now, so it goes. Lewis on the perception that solar is too expensive is still true and can stay. That is one quote lost out of four.

opening paragraph

Confirmed, and worse than you flagged. R2.2 million is roughly two residential installations. Medium

Wrong unit, twice, on a solar installer's website

pricing paragraph

should produce around 15-20KW of power a day … produces around 20-35KW of power a day

Confirmed. kW is a rate, kWh is a quantity. "20KW of power a day" is not a quantity of anything. The correct unit is kWh, and the capitalisation is wrong too: it is kWh, not KWH or KW.

This is the error that costs most reputationally, because it is the one an informed reader spots instantly and it appears on the page where you ask for money.

2. Five Tips to Beat Load Shedding · 593 words · score 3

Verdict Rewrite, and re-aim the premise rather than retiring it. The tips themselves are good and still work. What expired is the reason given for needing them.

High

The premise is false in the present tense, in the first and last lines

With no end in sight to load shedding disruptions … With no sign of load shedding easing any time soon

The archive callout tries to manage this by telling the reader the outlook has changed. That is damage control on copy that should simply be correct. A note explaining that the article is wrong is worse than an article that is right.

The re-aim is sitting there. The advice is about outages, and outages did not stop when scheduled load shedding eased. Municipal faults, storms, cable theft and planned maintenance all produce the same evening without power, with the difference that nobody publishes a schedule for them. Same five tips, an honest premise, and a sharper one: you used to know when.

High

Tip four contradicts the flagship page

Only switch on your geyser when you need warm water.

Confirmed, and it is a direct contradiction rather than a soft one. Smart Solutions argues that a geyser running to a human decision is precisely the problem, that people forget, go away, and heat water for a house nobody is in, and it sells a product to fix it. This post tells the same reader to do it manually.

The tip is not wrong so much as superseded. Rewritten, it becomes the strongest internal link in the set: the geyser is the biggest load, here is the manual version, here is what we do about it properly.

Medium

An unsourced percentage

you will use up to 20% less power to heat it up

Confirmed unsourced. "Up to" is doing the work of a citation. Either source it or state the mechanism without a number: a geyser blanket slows standing losses, so the element runs less to hold temperature. That is true, checkable and needs no figure.

Medium

A UPS does not do what tip two says it does

If you have a UPS system (uninterrupted power supply), this will kick in with additional power when the electricity switches off.

Found on my own read. A UPS does not supply additional power. It keeps the existing load running without a break, which is a different claim and the actual reason to own one. The tip also welds two unrelated ideas together: unplug against surges on restoration, and buy a UPS.

Low

Voice

Both are really great solutions, but with the recent increase in fuel prices, maybe a consideration for a greener solution is the way to go!

An exclamation mark, "really great", "maybe", and a swerve into fuel prices that belongs to a generator argument the article never makes. Also "do what they can to get through the outages as best they can" in the opening line.

3. Off the Grid vs. Grid-tied · 325 words · score 4

Verdict Rewrite. Best bones of the three archive pieces. It answers a question people genuinely ask and it is the natural feeder to the calculator. It just never finishes.

High

It poses a question in the title and never answers it

Confirmed. The piece describes off-grid, describes grid-tied, and stops. No recommendation, no "here is how you tell which one you are", nothing. A comparison article that does not compare is a glossary.

Worse, the site has an opinion and states it everywhere else. The answer for almost every house is hybrid grid-tied, and off-grid is for properties with no viable connection or an owner who wants it for reasons that are not financial. Say that.

Medium

Two electrical errors in one paragraph

the inverter which converts the low voltage received from the panels to high-voltage 230VA

Confirmed on 230VA: volt amps are apparent power, not voltage. Two more found on my read. The conversion that matters is DC to AC, and the article never mentions it, so it describes the inverter's job without naming what the inverter actually does. And 230V is not high voltage; by any standard used in this country it is low voltage. Three errors, one sentence, on the component the company sells.

Medium

Sunlight hours

South Africa enjoys about four to six hours of sunlight a day

Found on my read. South Africa gets far more than six hours of daylight. The figure being reached for is peak sun hours, which is a different measure and worth naming, because naming it correctly is the sort of thing that separates an installer from a reseller.

Medium

A promise one hyperlink away from being kept

Sunlogic offers calculators that do this based on your monthly electricity bill.

The calculator exists, on the solar page, and this sentence does not link to it. It is the single highest value link available anywhere in this set and it is currently a dead mention.

Medium

Both images belong to a competitor

Confirmed. Hero and in-body are credited to stendysolar.co.nz, a New Zealand solar company. The credit is honest, and it still means the two photographs illustrating a South African installer's own explainer are somebody else's installations on the other side of the world.

Low

Municipal approval is vaguer than it needs to be

make sure that any inverter you use has been approved by your local municipality

True but soft, and it is a place where the company's own expertise should show rather than hide. The reader's actual question is who checks this, and the answer is that Sunlogic does, as part of the job.

Does "Worth Knowing" have a reason to exist

Not as it stands. It has one available, and it is the site's own spine. Four posts currently read as whatever was to hand. Named properly they are a sequence, and the tags already half admit it.

The verdict

Top three, by impact.

  • Fix the off-grid-means-battery model in both solar posts. It is the only error in this set that sends somebody to the wrong quote.
  • Delete the staff note and the unverifiable import figure from Rent-to-Own, today, before anything else on that page is touched.
  • Republish the three archive pieces under today's date with a provenance line, which is what makes an honest rewrite possible at all.

What is already working.

  • Why Your Electricity Bill Went Up is properly sourced, has a genuine thesis, and its chart is accessible and cited. Rewrite the other three towards it.
  • The five tips are actually useful. Frozen water bottles in the fridge is the kind of concrete, cheap, immediately actionable advice this whole set should be full of.
  • The archive callout was the right instinct even though the conclusion is being reversed. Somebody noticed the problem and refused to paper over it.
  • Every post ends on the same site visit CTA. Consistency across the set is correct and should stay.

Kill list.

  • The R2.2 million import figure. Unverifiable and self-defeating.
  • "With no end in sight" and "no sign of load shedding easing any time soon". Both false now, and the second is inside a quotation, so it comes out rather than being edited.
  • "Both are really great solutions … maybe a consideration for a greener solution is the way to go!"
  • The archive callout on Five Tips, once the article no longer needs apologising for.

Blocked on the owner. Rent-to-Own cannot be completed without current rental pricing, or a decision to describe the structure and send the reader to the calculator instead. That is the only thing in this review that better writing cannot solve.
