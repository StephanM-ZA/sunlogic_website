Round two, line level

Why now · new callout

"On a commercial tariff you are billed on your highest demand of the month as well as on what you used. One busy half hour sets a charge you carry all month."
Issue

The best sentence added in v2 and the only one on the page I cannot check. It is the commercial twin of the fixed charge line: specific, and the sort of thing a business owner half suspects and has never seen written down. It is also a claim about how somebody else bills them.

Fix

Hold it against one real client invoice on a commercial tariff before it ships. If it is right, put the actual charge beside it and it becomes the strongest thing on the page. If it is wrong, it is wrong in front of exactly the reader it was written to win.

Why it matters: A reader who can check a claim against their own paperwork will check it. That is the entire reason the fixed charge line works.

Why now · the fixed charge

"It is not only the rate per unit going up. The fixed monthly charge has been climbing faster than the rate."
Issue

I called this the best fact on the page in round one. It is still running without a number. Faster than the rate is a comparison the reader has to take on trust, on the one line where they were most willing to go and look it up.

Fix

Two figures and a year. What the fixed charge was, what it is now. The highest value blank on the page, and unlike the demand charge above, this one is your own reading of a bill rather than a claim about someone else's billing.

Our work

"Each with a photograph and one real number, so you can judge the work rather than the claim."
Issue

There are no photographs. I counted the images inside those three cards on the rendered page and got zero. The sentence promises the reader something the section does not deliver, in the section whose whole job is proving you do not overpromise.

Fix

Photographs in, or the sentence out. Not both ways. And the numbers given are hardware, not outcomes. Eighteen panels and fifteen kilowatt hours of storage says what you fitted. It does not say what it did.

Why it matters: A page that says judge the work rather than the claim, then shows no work, has made the claim and skipped the work.

Our work · the commercial gap

Issue

All three named jobs read as houses. Meanwhile the live day feed further up the same page is showing a commercial install in Strand and a thirty kilowatt ground mount. The commercial proof is running on the page as a widget and missing from the section built to carry proof.

Fix

One of the three becomes a business. A shop, a workshop, a warehouse, with the suburb, what went in, and what it did to their running cost. Until that exists, the SME half of the page is an argument with nothing behind it, which is the criticism I made of Our Work in round one, now pointed at a different reader.

Why it matters: You cannot claim equal weight for a market you cannot show one job for.

Why now · two different sums

"You trade during the day, which is exactly when solar produces, so more of what you generate goes straight into the building."
Working

Moving this up is right, and it was already the right sentence. Sitting beside the residential sum it now does two jobs: it makes the business case, and it tells a homeowner why theirs is different, which makes the storage conversation later feel earned rather than upsold.

Hero · second button

Book a Free Site Assessment  ·  Work Out What You Need
Issue

Two buttons split the click, which is fine for a purchase this size. The second is the problem. Work Out What You Need does not say what it is or what the reader gets back. It reads like a section heading that grew a border.

Fix

Name the tool and name the output. The reader is choosing between letting a stranger visit their property and doing it themselves, and only one of those two options currently explains itself.

Smart control at home

Honest, and it exposes something

Labelling the geyser section as the home side was the right edit. It stops a business reader hitting a domestic appliance at section nine and deciding they were on the wrong page.

What it exposes is not a copy problem. On a page claiming equal weight, the home reader gets a named product, a partner and a credential, and the business reader gets no equivalent. If you have a commercial load control story it belongs here. If you do not, the asymmetry is now visible, which beats hidden but is still an asymmetry.

Close · contact

Issue

Section four promises one company, one certificate, one number to phone. The rendered page carries two distinct numbers, repeated across the call to action, the footer and the dock, six phone links in total. The copy makes a promise the furniture contradicts.

Fix

One number, or label the second so the reader knows why it is there. Not a writing change, a components change, and it undercuts the best line in the whole argument.

Why it matters: Every specific promise becomes a thing the reader can catch you on. That is the price of being specific and it is worth paying, but only if the page keeps its side.

The verdict

Three changes

  1. Check the demand charge line, then put a number on the fixed charge. One is a claim you must verify, the other a blank you can fill from your own reading. Together they are the two most persuasive sentences available to you, and neither is finished.
  2. Make one of the three Our Work jobs a business, with a photograph. It closes the unkept promise and the missing commercial proof in one piece of work.
  3. One phone number. The page argues for one number to phone and shows two, six times.

Do not let anything change these

"We can change how much of it you pay." Shorter than what it replaced, and it ends the section on the reader instead of on their bill. Best edit in v2.

"Solar fails quietly." Unchanged, and still the paragraph the site should be built around.

"If solar is not worth it on your property yet, we will say so." Now sitting next to a payback promise, which makes it stronger, not weaker.

"The roof sets the ceiling. What you use sets the target." Two short sentences that work identically for a cottage and a cold store. This is what the rest of the shared copy should sound like.

Verify before publishing

  • The demand charge sentence. Against a real commercial invoice.
  • The fixed charge comparison. Needs the two figures behind it.
  • The cleaning line. Still waiting on the attorney, from round one.
  • Registration number and install count. Still out of the trust strip, correctly.

Cut or hold

  • The photograph promise in Our Work. Out, until photographs exist.
  • Work Out What You Need. Rename it to what it does.
  • The second phone number, or the one number to phone line. You cannot keep both.
