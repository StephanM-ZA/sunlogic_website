Two Trades, One Buried Argument

The apex page has one job and now does it. The line that would make somebody choose Sunlogic over the other three quotes was in the third card of the third section; it opens the page. Three rounds, twenty findings, nineteen applied.

Every finding below is applied and live. All High and Medium, all three rounds, verified against the built pages rather than the source: each removed line was searched for in dist/main/ and none of them is still there. The single open item is at the foot of this page and is a flag, not a finding.

8

Final, round three. Was 6 at the start. The mechanism opens the page, a checklist that restated the trust strip is now two named directors, and the reviews block argues the relationship instead of reciting a source. Round two found six issues created by round one; round three found six more, four of them again created by the round before it. That pattern is the finding worth keeping: on a page this tight, a fix is a move, and the thing it was covering is what you look at next.

The strategic read

The big idea is sound. "Two Trades, One Contractor" is a real positioning line, not a slogan. It passes the bar test: they do the solar and the sparky work, so nobody points fingers. For a page whose entire reason to exist is explaining a two-division structure, that is the correct headline.

The lead does not carry it. The hero body underneath is three noun fragments: certified contracting and compliance, solar backup and smart control, one registered company. That is a services index. It restates the headline at lower resolution and adds no reason to care. A reader who has three quotes on the table learns nothing from it that the headline did not already say.

The routing works, and it works well. "If it makes power, stores it or controls it, that is Energy. If it is wiring, a fault, a board or a certificate, that is Electrical." Two sentences, no jargon, and the reader knows which button to press. This is the best-working copy on the site and nothing should be done to it.

The middle sags. Divisions is strong, then "What You Get Either Way" is four cards of which one is the actual argument and three are table stakes, then Reviews addresses the wrong reader, then the company section says what the top of the page already said. Momentum drops between the divisions and the close.

HighApplied

The mechanism is buried in card three of section three

index.html · Why Sunlogic · "One Team, Both Trades"

As it standsA solar system is an electrical installation. Having both under one registered company means nobody is arguing about whose work the fault is.

That is the whole argument for the company. It is concrete, it names a fear the reader actually has, and no competitor holding one trade can say it. It is currently the third of four equal cards, two thirds of the way down.

AppliedA solar system is an electrical installation. Most people end up hiring two companies for it and then refereeing between them. We hold both trades in one registered company, across the Western Cape.

Schwartz: the mechanism goes where the reader is still deciding whether to keep reading, not where they have already decided.

HighApplied

The same two credentials are stated three times on one page

index.html · trust strip, then "Not a Call Centre" checklist

The trust strip says compliance certificate on every job and council registration handled for you. Six hundred words later the checklist says Compliance certificate on every job and Council and SSEG registration handled for you. Close to verbatim.

The other two checklist items have a different problem. They restate the paragraph sitting immediately beside them: the body says both directors run the teams and the person who quotes your job is answerable, and the checklist says Both directors run the teams and the projects and The person who quotes your job stays on it.

All four items are redundant. Two duplicate the paragraph they sit next to, two duplicate the top of the page.

AppliedCut the checklist, or replace all four items with things not said anywhere else on the page: the years trading, the trades held, the areas covered, what happens if something goes wrong after sign-off.

Repetition is a persuasion tool when it lands on a different facet each time. Repeating a credential verbatim just tells the reader you ran out of material.

Changed after the review, on your call

Three things came from you rather than from the review, and each turned into a system rule rather than a one-page edit.

  • The reviews block now sells the relationship. It said "Pulled straight from our Google profile", which is a source note, not an argument. It now says the same two names keep coming up, which is the thing the reviews actually show. No counts, at your instruction: a tally has to be re-checked against the export every time it refreshes.
  • Never a light-on-light button. On cream, navy at rest, orange on hover. This retired .sl-btn--secondary (cream on beige, 1.09:1, used by no page) and moved the contact form's submit off the emphasis treatment, whose hover clears its orange fill and leaves an orange hairline on beige.
  • The two directors' emails now look like links. They were always real mailto: anchors, but the hover rule only matched the contact-row wrapper, not the bare anchor the person card emits. They rendered as plain body text.

What this cost, and what it caught

The button rule was a two-line stylesheet change that broke something in a place nobody would have looked. wipe="navy" existed so a paired Solar and Electrical row read as two things before you got to the labels. The moment outline became navy at rest, those nine buttons wiped navy over navy: a hover that runs, repaints, and changes nothing. It would not show in a screenshot.

Four checks now enforce what used to be prose, and each was proved by injecting a violation and watching the build fail:

What is already working

  • The routing paragraph. "If it makes power, stores it or controls it, that is Energy." Two sentences that do the page's entire job. Do not touch it.
  • "Backup batteries, from a gate motor to a full home system." The most specific line on the page, and the only one that shows the range rather than claiming it.
  • "If something is not worth doing yet, we will say so rather than quote it anyway." A real risk taken in the copy. It is the sort of line that gets remembered and repeated.
  • "Not a Call Centre." Names the fear rather than the feature. The heading is better than the section under it.
  • The trust strip now holds three provable items. Dropping the install count cost the page a number and bought it credibility.
  • The 404 and thank-you pages. Both do their job with no padding. Three steps, concrete, and a phone number for people who would rather not wait.

Kill list

  • The four-item checklist in "Not a Call Centre". Every item is said elsewhere on the page.
  • "not curated testimonials". The denial, not the claim.
  • "costs nothing" in the closing block. The button says it.
  • "usually" in "The person who quoted the job is usually the one you see again." Either it is true and you say it, or it is not and you write the honest version. The hedge in the middle buys nothing.

The three that matter

  • Move the mechanism into the hero. One paragraph move, and it is the difference between a page that lists two divisions and a page that argues for holding both.
  • Cut the redundant checklist. Four lines out, and the company section stops repeating the top of the page.
  • Settle the voice, then apply it everywhere. Nought against forty two across one estate is not a style, it is an accident, and it is the finding that will keep coming back until somebody decides.
