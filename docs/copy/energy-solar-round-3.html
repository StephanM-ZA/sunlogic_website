The Audience With No Close

Third review of solar.html, the division's long service page. Its structural bet is three audiences in one scroll. That bet pays off for two of them. The one it failed is the one with the largest deal sizes.

Question asked
Is three audiences in one scroll paying off, and where does the length cost conversions
Result
Six findings, all applied
8

A strong page with one structural hole and one asymmetry between its two main audience sections. Both are fixed. The length is not the problem it looks like: each audience can finish and convert without reading the other two.

The verdict on the structural bet

It works, and here is the test it passes. A homeowner can read to roughly forty per cent of the page, hit a close written for them, and book without ever meeting the business argument. The subnav jumps a business reader straight past the home section. The arguments genuinely differ rather than repeating at three sizes: battery necessity at home, the cost-per-unit comparison in a business, the demand charge and the tax position at scale. It reads as three pages that share a URL, which is what it should be.

Where it failed: only two of the three audiences actually had a close.

HighApplied

One audience section highlighted advice, the other highlighted itself

solar.html · the two callouts

The home section puts genuinely useful reader advice in its highlight: selling power back can move you onto a tariff whose fixed charge costs more than the export earns. That is worth knowing whoever you buy from.

The business section put a company process in its highlight, headed "How we quote", and buried the equivalent reader advice in the middle of a prose paragraph:

Ask us to show the quote as a cost per unit as well as a monthly saving.

That line works on a competitor's quote as well as on ours, which is exactly why it earns the highlight. It was also the fourth appearance of "twelve months of your bills" on one page, one of them inside a highlight box.

AppliedAsk any installer for this. Ask for the quote as a cost per unit, not only as a monthly saving. That is the number you can hold against what you already pay, and it is the fastest way to compare three proposals that have all been laid out differently. Ask us for it too.

Not a finding

The length. At 13,600px this is the longest page in the estate, and the obvious note to write is that it should be split. It should not. Each audience has its own marker, its own argument, its own FAQ and now its own close, so nobody has to read another audience's section to reach a booking. The subnav makes any of the three one click from the top. Splitting it would trade one long page a reader can navigate for three thin ones that each repeat the tariff argument.

The one real cost of the length is that a larger-site reader arriving from the nav rather than from the home page's routing card passes two audiences before reaching theirs. The subnav is the mitigation and it is doing its job.
