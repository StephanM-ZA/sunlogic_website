How a lead reaches a director

Every enquiry from the website is offered to one director at a time. Nothing is shared until someone actively accepts it, nothing is lost if nobody does, and every step is recorded.

The three stages

Enquiries alternate strictly between the two directors. Each offer is exclusive — the other person is told nothing while it stands.

1

Offered to one director

Whoever is next in the rotation receives an email saying an enquiry has arrived, its division, and the time it came in. No name, no contact details, no message. A single button accepts it.

Exclusive for 24 hours
2

Passed to the other director

If it isn't accepted, the first director is told that 24 hours has passed and the enquiry has moved on. It is then offered to the other director, alone, on the same terms.

Exclusive for a further 24 hours
3

Opened to both

After 48 hours with no acceptance, both directors are emailed and both links become live. Whoever clicks first takes it. This alert repeats every day until somebody does.

Repeats daily until accepted

What the enquirer's details are protected by

The offer email deliberately carries only the division and the arrival time. The customer's name, phone number, email address, suburb and message are sent only to the person who accepts, and only once they have.

Accepting is a deliberate action, not a side effect of opening the email. The link shows a page that performs the acceptance — a mail scanner following links in the background cannot claim an enquiry.

Guarantees

Nothing is lost

An unaccepted enquiry is never dropped. The reminder repeats daily, indefinitely, until a person takes it.

Offers are strictly even

The rotation alternates on every offer. Neither director can receive two first offers in a row.

One owner, never two

If both click at the same moment, exactly one acceptance is recorded. The other is told someone got there first.

Nothing is deleted

Offers change state, they are never removed. Every enquiry keeps its full history.

The record

Every enquiry occupies one row in the Sunlogic Leads spreadsheet, updated in place as it moves. Four columns answer the questions that get asked:

ColumnAnswers
Assigned ToWho it was offered to first
Accepted AtWhether they took it, and when
Reassigned ToWho it passed to, if it wasn't accepted
Reassigned Accepted AtWhether that person took it, and when

A History column carries the whole sequence in plain language, so a question about any enquiry has a factual answer:

Offered to Stephan 4 Sep 2026 at 10:36 SAST → Not accepted within 24 hours → Offered to Craig 5 Sep 2026 at 10:36 SAST → Accepted by Craig 5 Sep 2026 at 11:02 SAST

It records what happened, never why. Whether someone was on a roof, on leave, or simply hadn't seen it is not something the system knows, and it does not guess.

A daily summary

Every morning at 07:00 an email reports the last 24 hours: enquiries received, accepted, still unclaimed and for how long, and anything that failed to send. It is sent whether or not anything needs attention.

That is deliberate. A summary that only appears when there is a problem is indistinguishable from one that has stopped working — and “no enquiries today” would look identical to “the system has been down since Tuesday”. If the summary stops arriving, something is wrong.

Enquiries do not depend on any office equipment. If the iMac is off, leads still arrive, are still assigned, and directors are still emailed — only the spreadsheet row waits. If email delivery fails entirely, the enquiry is held and retried for 48 hours rather than discarded.

Known limits

Stated plainly, because a reference that only lists strengths is not a reference.

LimitWhat it means
The clock starts on sending The 24 hours is measured from when the email is sent, not when it is read. A long outage at the receiving mailbox could consume part of a window.
Timing is accurate to five minutes Expiry is checked every five minutes, so “24 hours” means 24 hours give or take a few minutes.
Even offers, not even work The rotation guarantees an equal split of offers. If one director accepts less often, the other does more of the work while the offer counts stay identical. The daily summary reports both figures.
Anyone with the link can accept Access to the mailbox is the authentication, as with a password reset link. A forwarded offer email could be accepted by the recipient.
