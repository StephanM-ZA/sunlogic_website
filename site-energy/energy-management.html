The switch is the cheap part

Anyone can fit a relay. What decides whether it saves you anything is what it is told to do, and that is set up against your system, your tariff and how your household actually runs. A smart switch with no logic behind it is a light switch you can reach from the couch.

The switch asks whether there's spare power right now, not what time it is, and starts the geyser, the pool pump or the car charger when there is.

Nothing gets exported cheaply that could have been used at full value. It's the most effective thing you can do to a solar system after installing it, and the one most people never get round to.

And the one you will be sold next

You'll start hearing that your battery can earn money by helping the national grid. That's genuinely being built. It also has no formal place in South African electricity rules yet, and the regulator has said it isn't drafting any. So we won't promise you an income from a battery, and be wary of anyone who does. What we will say: buy equipment that can join in later rather than equipment that can't. That choice is cheap now and expensive to undo.
