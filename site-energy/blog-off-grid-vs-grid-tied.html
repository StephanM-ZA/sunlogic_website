Solar

People usually arrive at this question having already decided that off-grid is the proper version and grid-tied is the compromise. It's worth saying up front that it's the other way round for almost every house we look at, and that the thing most people actually want when they say "off-grid" has a different name.

The battery is not what makes a system off-grid

This is where the confusion sits, and it costs people money. Off-grid means no connection to the municipality at all. No meter, no monthly account, no fallback on a week of cloud. Storing your own power in a battery is a separate decision, and you can do it while staying connected.

A system that has panels, a battery and a grid connection is called hybrid. It runs the house off the sun during the day, off the battery in the evening, and off the grid when both have run out. That is what keeps the lights on through an outage, and it's what we install on most homes. It is grid-tied, and it has a battery, and those two facts are not in tension.

What each one is actually made of

Panels turn sunlight into direct current. The inverter is the piece that turns that into the 230 volts of alternating current your house is wired for, which is why it's the component worth spending on and the one your municipality cares about. Grid-tied inverters have to be on the approved list for your municipality before the connection can be signed off, and the system has to be registered. We handle that as part of the job rather than leaving it with you.

Sizing runs on peak sun hours, not daylight hours. Most of South Africa sits somewhere around four to six peak sun hours a day, which is the number of hours' worth of full-strength sun a location effectively gets. It's a good deal less than the time the sun is up, and it's the figure a system gets designed against.

So which one

Hybrid grid-tied, for almost everybody. It costs less than off-grid because it doesn't have to carry the worst week of the year on batteries alone, it can be built in stages as budget allows, and the grid stays there as a backstop you rarely use and are glad of when you do.

Off-grid earns its place in two situations. The property has no viable connection, or getting one costs more than the batteries would. Or the owner wants independence for reasons that aren't financial, which is a legitimate thing to want as long as it's a decision rather than a misunderstanding.

The way to tell which one you are is to work off your own usage rather than a rule of thumb. Our solar calculator gives you a realistic range in a couple of minutes from your monthly bill, and a site visit turns that into an actual figure.
